# CNBC — 2026-08-15

## Lead Story
The artificial intelligence infrastructure trade reached a watershed moment as **[Nvidia partnered with six major asset managers on a historic \$500 billion financing push](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/15/inflation-moderated-as-intel-and-nvidia-fueled-the-ai-trade-in-last-week-market.html)** to turn compute power into a bankable, revenue-generating asset class, while **[Anthropic’s Q2 revenue surged over 14-fold to \$11.5 billion](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/15/anthropic-revenue-jumps-to-over-11point5-billion-in-q2-report.html)** as it gears up for a blockbuster IPO. This massive capitalization underscore highlights a shifting consensus that AI hardware and compute centers are transitioning from rapidly depreciating tech into long-term critical infrastructure.

## Markets & Economics
Wall Street logged its third consecutive winning week, propelled by the **[S&P 500 crossing the 7,800 milestone](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/15/inflation-moderated-as-intel-and-nvidia-fueled-the-ai-trade-in-last-week-market.html)** for the first time in history, though the Dow bucked the trend to slide 0.6%. Equities were buoyed by a highly benign July inflation report showing consumer prices rose just 0.1% month-on-month (3.4% annually) alongside a completely flat producer price index, prompting treasury yields to retreat as traders priced in a 67% probability that the Federal Reserve holds interest rates steady in September. However, geopolitical headwinds are keeping energy prices stubbornly elevated; the U.S. continues to enforce an indefinite naval blockade of Iranian ports in the Strait of Hormuz—sending national average gasoline prices up to \$4.07 per gallon—while the **[U.S. Strategic Petroleum Reserve has plunged below 300 million barrels](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/15/strategic-petroleum-reserve-spr-oil-iran-war-caverns.html)** for the first time since the early 1980s, sparking warnings from experts of irreversible salt cavern degradation.

## Business & Earnings
In corporate moves, Warren Buffett's Berkshire Hathaway executed a **[blockbuster \$17 billion Q2 addition to its Alphabet position](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/15/berkshire-adds-17-billion-to-alphabet-stake.html)**, vaulting Google to its third-largest holding at \$36.6 billion, even as it systematically pared down Bank of America for the eighth consecutive quarter. Intel also capitalized on robust AI demand, raising a massive \$20 billion through an upsized capital offering to fund its heavy foundry buildout, supported by a high-conviction \$12 million personal stock purchase from CEO Lip-Bu Tan. Meanwhile, in the data center arms race, **[Nvidia is in talks to invest up to \$3 billion](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/15/nvidia-mulls-3b-investment-in-sb-energy-in-openai-data-center-deal-report.html)** in SoftBank subsidiary SB Energy as part of a structured credit-support package of up to \$100 billion for an OpenAI-backed super-campus in Ohio. Additionally, UK betting exchange Smarkets is executing a highly watched **[dual-track regulatory play in the U.S.](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/15/prediction-markets-and-sports-betting-how-this-uk-exchange-is-trying-to-do-both-in-the-us.html)**, seeking federal CFTC prediction-market status while simultaneously securing sports-betting licenses in Illinois and Michigan to bypass state-level legal crackdowns.

## Investing & Commentary
Goldman Sachs issued high-conviction buy ratings on **[top stock opportunities in the wake of Q2 earnings](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/15/goldman-says-these-stocks-are-top-ideas-in-wake-of-their-2q-earnings.html)**, highlighting aerospace manufacturer Loar Holdings, restaurant fintech Toast, and infrastructure firms MasTec and Quanta Services as prime plays levered to massive secular tailwinds in grid and AI data center buildouts. Concurrently, wealth managers are advising clients to harvest record equity gains and rotate capital into **[yielding short-term duration bond funds and money markets](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/15/cash-money-market-funds-bonds.html)** like Baird Ultra Short Bond (BUBIX) and JPMorgan Ultra-Short Income (JPST) to secure yields up to 110 basis points over standard money market ETFs without exposing themselves to long-term treasury rate volatility. On the contrarian front, "Big Short" manager Michael Burry warned on Substack that Berkshire Hathaway is no longer an attractive buy, claiming Greg Abel's aggressive Q2 cash deployment confirms his fear that Buffett’s successor lacks the legendary discipline to "wait for the fat pitch".
