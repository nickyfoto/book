# Apple Daily Digest — 2026-07-25

## Highlights
Today's news revolves around Apple's future product strategies and the shifting geopolitical and legal landscapes the company is navigating. From a rumored "MacBook Ultra" touchscreen laptop to a completely revamped, staggered release schedule for the upcoming iPhone 18 lineup, Apple appears to be breaking its own hardware traditions. At the same time, the company is dealing with consumer lawsuits over App Store scams, while facing fierce political lobbying from domestic chipmakers over its international supply chain.

## Top Stories
- **['MacBook Ultra' Coming Soon With These 12 New Features](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/07/24/macbook-ultra-12-new-features/)**: Apple is reportedly preparing to launch its first high-end laptop redesign in five years, which will likely be positioned as a new tier above the MacBook Pro. The "MacBook Ultra" is expected to arrive between late 2026 and early 2027 and could feature an OLED display, a touch screen with touch-optimized macOS controls, a hole-punch camera, and an interactive Dynamic Island. ([Source](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/07/24/macbook-ultra-12-new-features/))
- **[iPhone 18 won’t debut this fall, here’s why](https://9to5mac.com/2026/07/25/heres-why-iphone-18-wont-launch-in-september/)**: As Apple's annual smartphone lineup swells to six models, the company is expected to shift to a split release schedule starting with the iPhone 18 family. Flagship devices like the iPhone 18 Pro, Pro Max, and the new Ultra will reportedly launch in the fall of 2026, while the standard iPhone 18, 18e, and Air 2 are slated for a spring 2027 release to better segment the market. ([Source](https://9to5mac.com/2026/07/25/heres-why-iphone-18-wont-launch-in-september/))
- **[Report: Apple and Micron clash before Trump over Chinese memory chips](https://9to5mac.com/2026/07/24/report-apple-and-micron-clash-before-trump-over-chinese-memory-chips/)**: Micron is aggressively lobbying the Trump administration to block Apple from utilizing memory chips from Chinese suppliers like CXMT and YMTC. Micron argues that allowing Apple to use these heavily state-subsidized chips—even for devices sold exclusively outside the U.S.—could ultimately undercut U.S. producers and damage the domestic semiconductor industry. ([Source](https://9to5mac.com/2026/07/24/report-apple-and-micron-clash-before-trump-over-chinese-memory-chips/))
- **[Top Stories: 'Apple Upgrade’ Program, Apple Music Price Hike, and More](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/07/25/top-stories-apple-upgrade-program/)**: Apple is preparing to partner with Klarna to launch a new "Apple Upgrade" leasing and financing program in the U.S. on July 28, which will eventually wind down the existing iPhone Upgrade Program. Simultaneously, the company has raised prices across several services, pushing Apple Music up to $11.99 and hiking the cost of Apple One bundles and iCloud+ in select regions. ([Source](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/07/25/top-stories-apple-upgrade-program/))
- **[Apple Sued by Customers Who Lost Combined $1.8 Million Through Fake Bitcoin Wallet in App Store](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/07/25/apple-app-store-fake-bitcoin-wallet-lawsuit/)**: Three customers have filed a federal lawsuit against Apple in California after losing significant cryptocurrency funds to a fake "Sparrow Wallet" app that slipped through App Store reviews. The plaintiffs argue that Apple failed to adequately monitor the App Store despite deliberately cultivating consumer trust by marketing it as a safe and trusted environment. ([Source](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/07/25/apple-app-store-fake-bitcoin-wallet-lawsuit/))
