# The Post-Cook Era Takes Shape Amid Hardware Pivots and AI Leaks — 2026-04-29

## Highlights
Today's news paints a picture of an Apple in transition under incoming CEO John Ternus, marked by aggressive hardware pivots and mounting supply chain pressures. While the company is reportedly shelving ambitious projects like the M5 Vision Pro and the foldable "iPad Ultra" to focus on next-generation smart glasses, it is simultaneously gearing up for a massive artificial intelligence push with iOS 27's new camera-integrated Siri capabilities. Looming over these bold product decisions is a brewing component cost crisis that could force Apple to entirely rethink its iPhone pricing strategy by 2027.

## Top Stories
- **[Apple Has Given Up on the Vision Pro After M5 Refresh Flop]**: Apple has reportedly abandoned the Vision Pro line after its M5 processor refresh failed to spur consumer interest, with the development team being redistributed to other projects like Siri. Moving away from bulky VR headsets, Apple is now pivoting its wearable ambitions toward screen-less AI smart glasses featuring hand-gesture controls and dual cameras. ([Source](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/04/29/apple-vision-pro-m5-flop/))
- **[Epic Games Wins Reversal of Stay in App Store Fee Legal Battle]**: The U.S. Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit reversed a prior decision that had allowed Apple to pause changes to its App Store commission structure while seeking a Supreme Court review. The ruling forces Apple back to a lower court to determine what fees it can legally charge developers who direct customers to alternative, external payment systems. ([Source](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/04/29/epic-games-wins-reversal-app-store-fee-battle/))
- **[Apple Has Likely Abandoned 'iPad Ultra’ Plans]**: Apple has reportedly canceled its plans to release a massive, 20-inch foldable "iPad Ultra" due to persistent engineering challenges and the weak sales performance of the existing high-end iPad Pro lineup. Instead, Apple's experimental "Ultra" pipeline appears focused on an upcoming ultra-thin, foldable "iPhone Ultra" and an OLED MacBook Ultra. ([Source](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/04/29/apple-likely-abandoned-ipad-ultra-plans/))
- **[iOS 27 Camera App to Get 'Siri' Mode With Nutrition Label Scanning]**: Apple plans to deeply embed its AI features into the iOS 27 Camera app with a new dedicated "Siri mode" that leverages Visual Intelligence. The upgraded tool will allow users to scan food nutrition labels for dietary tracking in the Health app and automatically save contact information from physical business cards directly into the Contacts app. ([Source](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/04/29/ios-27-camera-app-siri-mode/))
- **[Report: iPhone Memory Costs Set to Quadruple by 2027]**: Fueled by the massive AI infrastructure boom, Apple is facing a 400% increase in memory costs by 2027, which could account for up to 45% of an iPhone's total component cost. This leaves incoming CEO John Ternus with a critical decision: absorb the margin hit to maintain market share against global rivals, or pass the massive cost increases onto consumers. ([Source](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/04/29/iphone-memory-costs-quadruple-2027/))

## Articles Worth Reading

**[Apple Reportedly Questioning Whether iPhone Should Drop MagSafe]** ([Source](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/04/29/apple-questioning-iphone-magsafe/))
Fascinating internal debate is reportedly brewing over the future of MagSafe on standard iPhones, as Apple weighs the component cost and physical space required by the magnets against its massive third-party accessory ecosystem. With rumors pointing to an incredibly thin, foldable "iPhone Ultra" model coming soon that may lack the internal space for a magnet array, MagSafe could be dropped from the highest-end tier entirely or removed from standard models to cut costs. However, given Apple's recent reversal to add MagSafe back to the iPhone 17e after omitting it from the 16e, a complete removal of the feature seems like a massive risk.

**[Apple researchers built an AI that tests several ideas in parallel before answering]** ([Source](https://9to5mac.com/2026/04/29/apple-researchers-built-an-ai-that-tests-several-ideas-in-parallel-before-answering/))
Apple researchers have published an intriguing new study detailing "LaDiR," a novel AI framework that combines latent diffusion with autoregressive generation to drastically improve reasoning in Large Language Models. During inference time, the system tests multiple reasoning paths in parallel before generating a final answer, achieving notably higher accuracy in complex math problems and code generation. This is a great, technical look into how Apple is aggressively exploring alternative software architectures to push on-device intelligence beyond standard LLM capabilities.

**[Apple’s biggest win last week made the fewest headlines]** ([Source](https://www.macworld.com/article/3126723/apples-biggest-win-last-week-might-be-promoting-johny-srouji.html))
While Tim Cook's transition and John Ternus's elevation to CEO dominated recent news cycles, this piece effectively argues that Johny Srouji's promotion to Chief Hardware Officer is just as vital to Apple's future. As the so-called "father of Apple silicon," Srouji was reportedly considering leaving the company during this executive reshuffle, prompting Apple to hand him control of the entire hardware division to ensure his retention. The consolidation of hardware under Srouji is a massive internal shift that will heavily influence Apple's ability to maintain its competitive edge in tightly integrated silicon and device design.
