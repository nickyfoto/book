### Recipes & Tutorials
Chef MASA delivers a masterclass in comforting, flavor-packed home cooking with his latest release, [日式馬鈴薯燉中卷｜比燉肉更香！中卷鮮味爆發的超下飯家常料理](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JitApv1X6M4). The standout technique in this Japanese-style potato and squid stew is heat management; MASA stresses that the squid must be added at the very end of the cooking process with a shortened boiling time to prevent the delicate seafood from becoming tough and rubbery. Even with a short cooking time, the squid imparts a brilliant umami flavor directly into the potatoes, and MASA notes the recipe is versatile enough to swap in scallops or shrimp, making it an ideal, texturally satisfying candidate for meal prep or bento boxes.

In a more experimental culinary adventure, Chef James tests out highly creative, audience-submitted condiment recipes in [水餃沾醬再進化！6款網友特調實測，東泉辣椒醬加這個竟然變肉圓醬？ [詹姆士]](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-o_wYF7qWKg). He discovers that mixing Dongquan chili sauce with a little soy sauce creates a flavor profile remarkably similar to the sweet and savory glaze used for Taiwanese meatballs (Ba-wan), which he highly recommends pairing with pan-fried dumplings to complement the oil and fat. Another fascinating highlight is an Oden-inspired sauce blending miso, mirin, sake, and sesame, which surprisingly pairs perfectly with boiled dumplings because the fermented soy complements the sweet, water-boiled flavors of the dumpling filling. James also brilliantly refines a Russian-inspired dipping sauce, suggesting that home cooks mix Greek yogurt with finely minced garlic, salt, pepper, and chili oil to cut through the heavy, greasy mouthfeel of rich pork dumplings with a refreshing tang.

### Food Science & Tips
The seasoned chefs over at Lao Fan Gu offer a treasure trove of culinary science and practical kitchen advice in [炒菜中途要加水嗎？凍肉怎麼處理？14個答疑解惑小技巧，總有一個是你需要的！| 老飯骨](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ReAvirkmqfM). They explain the crucial difference between wok temperatures, noting that a hot pan with cold oil is the secret to keeping sliced meats from sticking, whereas a hot pan with hot oil is essential for flash-frying vegetables and drawing out their aromatic char. For achieving a beautiful, glossy shine on your pan sauces, they highly recommend using potato starch instead of cornstarch because it provides superior viscosity and brightness. Furthermore, they reveal the science behind restaurant-quality textures: using egg whites in meat marinades both brightens the visual color and tenderizes the protein, while adding a splash of water or milk to raw eggs ensures a perfectly soft, pale, and fluffy scramble. Finally, they caution home cooks against dropping frozen meat directly into hot fryers, as the high water content will cause dangerous oil splatters.
