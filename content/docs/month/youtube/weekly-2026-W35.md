# YouTube — Week of 2026-08-22 to 2026-08-28

## Watch First
**The Wall Street Journal’s teardown of a smuggled North Korean smartphone** stands out as the week's most gripping watch, exposing how a modern consumer operating system is weaponized for total surveillance through automated background screenshots and forced regime censorship. It provides a rare, tangible window into digital totalitarianism that is as chilling as it is technically fascinating.

## Week in Review
This week’s uploads were dominated by **mounting macroeconomic friction**, from unexpected tariff escalations between the U.S. and Canada to volatile bond yields and emergency currency interventions to rescue the Japanese yen. Simultaneously, coverage of artificial intelligence matured into a sober reality check, shifting away from speculative hype toward the punishing fiscal, infrastructural, and legal constraints confronting hyperscalers. Across cultural and historical channels, creators leaned heavily into dissecting ideological distortion, state propaganda, and the friction between individual human agency and rigid institutional power.

## Highlights by Theme

### News & Business
Central bank policy and fiscal strain took center stage, highlighted by **Kevin Warsh’s debut Jackson Hole address** and Stanley Druckenmiller’s critique of Scott Bessent’s Treasury bond buybacks, both parsed in depth across *The Wall Street Journal* and **美投侃新闻**. Meanwhile, currency tensions peaked as **小Lin说** and the *Financial Times* tracked the U.S. Treasury utilizing the FIMA repo facility to halt a historic slide in the Japanese yen. Trade relations also fractured dramatically following Washington's invocation of a Smoot-Hawley provision to slap a 50% tariff on Canadian imports. Ground-level financial vulnerability was captured movingly by Tencent Video’s documentary series **【前浪 第2季】**, which chronicled an elderly Shenzhen couple delivering food after losing their life savings to an online scam.

### Learning & Ideas
Historical critique was led by **LIFEANO CLUB’s blisteringly witty takedown** of nationalist pseudo-history comparing online historical denialism to Nazi Germany's Ahnenerbe institute, paired later in the week with the Hoover Institution's symposium re-examining the intellectual legacy of Thomas Sowell. In the sciences, Veritasium delivered an elegant demonstration proving the computational impossibility of true randomness, while cosmologist Katie Mack mapped the ultimate end scenarios of the universe on TED. Highlighting advanced engineering, the Chinese-language **Zhang Xiaojun Podcast** featured a Tsinghua researcher breaking down Kimi K3’s hybrid attention mechanisms and 2.8-trillion-parameter MoE architecture.

### Tech & AI
The tech sector faced the physical and financial ceilings of the AI expansion, with *The Wall Street Journal* and **美投侃新闻** analyzing NVIDIA’s blockbuster guidance alongside hyperscaler debt buildouts, circular financing risks, and grid power shortages. On the consumer and platform front, Veritasium exposed serious terminal-script vulnerabilities capable of bypassing locked iPhone protections to trigger unauthorized Apple Pay transactions, while *The New York Times* covered Meta’s landmark \$17 billion settlement concerning teen platform safety.

### Everything Else
Curious human-interest stories ranged from *The Wall Street Journal’s* report on radiator-fluid-drinking marmots vandalizing cars in Sequoia National Park to a chef running a licensed micro-pizzeria out of a Smart Car in New York City. On the lifestyle side, **Susie Woo’s vlog** detailed the practical hurdles of practicing traditional postpartum confinement (*zuo yue zi*) with newborn twins in the UK, contrasting nicely with *GQ Taiwan’s* sobering look into Golden Triangle scam compounds.
