# CNBC — Week of 2026-08-08 to 2026-08-14

## Story of the Week
**Nvidia’s landmark \$500 billion financing alliance with six of Wall Street's largest asset managers redefines AI graphics processing units (GPUs) and data centers as an investable, asset-backed infrastructure class akin to commercial real estate.** The massive mobilization allows neoclouds and startups to secure debt and equity to buy Nvidia chips without burdening their own balance sheets. However, the program has already sparked fierce debate, with "Big Short" investor Michael Burry comparing it to "shades of Enron" and warning that these unnatural credits are being structured late in the market cycle to prolong momentum.

## Markets & Economics
* **[Strait of Hormuz Blockade Propels Brent Crude Toward \$90]** · [CNBC Video](https://www.cnbc.com/video/2026/08/11/risks-to-our-base-case-for-brent-crude-are-skewed-to-the-upside-says-goldmans-daan-struyven.html): Hopes for a shipping resolution evaporated as Iran rejected direct talks and demanded an end to the U.S. naval blockade, driving Brent crude near \$90 a barrel as shipping transits fell 90% below prewar averages. With geopolitical risk premium returning, Goldman Sachs warns that risks to its Brent crude base case remain skewed to the upside.
* **[July Inflation Moderates, Calming Fed Rate-Hike Fears]** · [CNBC Interview](https://www.cnbc.com/video/2026/08/11/fed-will-need-to-hike-rates-to-tackle-inflation-says-fmr-trump-special-assistant-joe-lavorgna.html): July CPI moderated to 3.4% headline and 2.5% core annual growth, matching consensus estimates and lowering September rate-hike probabilities to 38%–42%. A flat July wholesale PPI print further bolstered the cooling narrative, even as hawkish commentators warn the Fed must tighten further to tackle persistent structural inflation.
* **[Treasury Yields Rebound on Geopolitical Headwinds]** · [CNBC Video](https://www.cnbc.com/video/2026/08/11/treasury-yields-up-as-oil-prices-jump-investors-await-inflation-data-.html): Geopolitical bottlenecks and surging energy costs pushed sovereign borrowing costs higher, driving the 10-year Treasury yield to a peak of 4.7334%. Yields remained elevated, closing the week at 4.661% after Washington threatened Iran with "never-before-seen" economic isolation.
* **[July Retail Sales Unexpectedly Plunge 0.6% Amid Consumer Slowdown]** · [CNBC Video](https://www.cnbc.com/video/2026/08/14/retail-sales-unexpectedly-fell-in-july.html): July retail sales unexpectedly fell by 0.6%, adding to economic worries following the previous week's soft payrolls print. However, Goldman Sachs' Lizzie Dove pointed out that high-income travel and tourism spending remains "off the charts," showing a highly resilient, K-shaped consumer divide.
* **[Existing Home Sales Drop as Credit Delinquencies Spike]** · [CNBC Video](https://www.cnbc.com/video/2026/08/11/july-existing-home-sales-dropped-1-point-7-percent.html): July existing home sales dropped 1.7% amid persistent housing affordability challenges. Concurrently, U.S. credit card debt spiked to \$1.26 trillion, with late-stage delinquencies climbing to a troubling 12.8%.

## Business & Earnings
* **Abel Puts Berkshire’s Cash to Work with Net Stock Purchases**  
In his second quarter as Berkshire Hathaway CEO, Greg Abel deployed nearly \$20 billion in net equity purchases—reversing a 14-quarter selling streak—and conducted \$4.5 billion in stock buybacks. Berkshire's Q2 operating earnings climbed 16% year-over-year to \$12.98 billion as retail and energy strength offset underwriting declines, and latest 13F filings revealed that the company boosted its Alphabet position by 83% to \$37.9 billion.
* **AI Infrastructure Demand Drives Historic Tech Capex and Cloud Rebounds**  
Neocloud powerhouse CoreWeave posted blockbusting growth, with Q2 revenue climbing 112% to \$2.58 billion and dispelling obsolescence fears with active A100 chip contracts running through 2029. To keep pace with relentless AI chip demand, bitcoin miner Riot Platforms pivoted to an AI landlord by signing a \$9.1 billion Anthropic hosting deal, while SK Hynix initiated a colossal \$720 billion memory expansion in South Korea.
* **OpenAI Scales to \$40 Billion Run Rate Amid Intense AI Capital Demands**  
Despite a wave of high-profile C-suite exits, OpenAI Chief Financial Officer Sarah Friar announced that the company's annualized revenue run-rate has scaled to \$40 billion, fueled by a major acceleration in high-margin enterprise clients. The massive capital needed for frontier AI was further underscored by OpenAI completing a \$7 billion secondary share sale at an \$852 billion valuation, while competing chipmaker Cerebras Systems tumbled 14% after missing revenue expectations.
* **Megadeals and Private Equity Pursuit Sweep Corporate America**  
Silver Lake is in talks to acquire software giant Workday, sending shares up over 19% before trading was halted, while activist Nelson Peltz's Trian Fund Management is preparing a takeover bid to take Wendy's private. In professional sports, Disney CEO Bob Iger and Joshua Kushner partnered to acquire a majority stake in the Los Angeles Lakers at a record-shattering \$12.5 billion valuation.
* **Hardware Giants Face Margin and Execution Hurdles**  
Cisco Systems plunged 9% after issuing conservative gross margin guidance of 65% to 66% that overshadowed its quarterly earnings beat, while semiconductor equipment maker Applied Materials fell 5% as results failed to satisfy high near-term expectations. Meanwhile, Boeing CEO Kelly Ortberg streamlined operations by selling three noncore subsidiaries to Archer Aviation for a 20% stake, and Home Depot announced CEO Ted Decker is taking a temporary medical leave.

## Investing & Commentary
* **"Big Short" Legends Flash Warnings on Late-Cycle AI and Technical Risks**  
Steve Eisman cautioned that the AI buildout has a critical "Achilles heel," as hyperscalers remain overly dependent on OpenAI and Anthropic for 25% to 35% of cloud revenues. Simultaneously, Michael Burry warned that the market's historic run of 182 days without an 80%-plus downside-volume day is an unprecedented technical red flag, while calling Nvidia’s \$500 billion financing alliance "shades of Enron".
* **Value Rotation and Long-Term Tech Compounding Win Backing**  
Bank of America's Jill Carey Hill urged investors to pivot toward value over growth, highlighting critical valuation support as overcrowding in high-beta momentum peaks. Conversely, Bill Ackman's Pershing Square initiated a new position in Netflix, claiming the streaming giant has effectively won the streaming wars and represents an attractive value play at 21 times forward earnings after a 50% pullback.
* **Structural Upgrades and Tactical Shifts Reframe the Memory Sector**  
The memory sector is seeing structural upgrades, with SanDisk rated overweight by JPMorgan with a \$2,250 price target on the strength of high-margin, long-term customer agreements. This pivot is expected to boost Micron, which Jim Cramer's Charitable Trust tactically accumulated at \$872, and which is projected to generate a scorching \$50 billion in free cash flow for fiscal year 2026.
