### Food Science & Tips
This week was packed with game-changing textural tips, like Chef James explaining how cutting chicken breast against the grain and lightly pounding it prevents the meat fibers from violently shrinking and toughening over heat. To achieve explosive juiciness in lamb dumplings, 老饭骨 shared the brilliant traditional trick of whipping Sichuan peppercorn water into the minced meat, which simultaneously neutralizes gaminess and creates a rich soup inside the wrapper. Lastly, for a perfect stir-fry glaze, a 50-year veteran banquet chef demonstrated how a classic velveting marinade of egg white and starch helps sauces grip the meat far better than modern oil-heavy shortcuts.

### Restaurant & Travel
On the culinary exploration front, Chef James took a nostalgic dive into Taiwanese snack history, tasting iconic treats with over 40 years of history, including oyster omelet chips originally meant to taste like Hiroshima okonomiyaki. He also explored modern convenience store culture, taste-testing an under-80 TWD bento box to reveal a surprisingly stellar, gelatinous braised pork rice that absorbed the savory broth without leaving a greasy mouthfeel.
