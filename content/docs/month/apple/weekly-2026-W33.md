# Apple — Week of 2026-08-08 to 2026-08-14

## Week in Review
Apple is currently navigating a transformative era defined by sweeping hardware strategy adjustments, an impending leadership transition, and intense cost pressures from a global memory crisis. This week revealed a major restructuring of Apple’s autumn release playbook—including a delayed base iPhone 18 model and a U.S.-exclusive initial rollout for the upcoming foldable "iPhone Ultra"—alongside crucial proposed App Store commission structures and strategic, highly customized AI developments for the Chinese market. As CEO Tim Cook enters his final weeks before John Ternus takes the helm on September 1, the company is aggressively squeezing its supply chain to shield margins while actively laying the groundwork for its next-generation silicon and AI roadmaps.

## Top Stories

**Fall Lineup Overhaul: Delays, Split-Launches, and the Foldable iPhone Ultra** · [Source](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/08/12/apple-skipping-iphone-18-launch-this-year/)
Apple is fundamentally reshaping its autumn launch playbook by splitting the upcoming iPhone 18 family to focus September's event exclusively on the premium **iPhone 18 Pro**, **Pro Max**, and the highly anticipated foldable **"iPhone Ultra,"** while holding the standard iPhone 18, lower-end 18e, and iPhone Air 2 back until early 2027. Leaks from screen protectors confirm that the folding iPhone Ultra features a unique **asymmetric design** with square corners near the hinge and rounded outer corners. However, due to production hiccups, display durability testing, and supply bottlenecks, the complex \$2,000+ foldable will follow a country-by-country rollout starting strictly in the U.S. market, forcing international buyers to wait.

**Waging War on the Memory Crisis: Supply Chains Squeezed by a "100-Year Flood"** · [Source](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/08/12/iphone-18-pro-lower-oled-display-cost-report/)
To combat a severe global DRAM shortage that is driving up the upcoming iPhone 18 Pro bill of materials by an estimated 38%, Apple is aggressively squeezing its supply chain and has successfully negotiated **OLED panel price cuts** from Samsung and LG down to \$68 per display for the Pro Max. These component negotiations became crucial as the memory supply crisis—which CEO Tim Cook likened to a **"100-year flood"**—previously forced summer price increases on Macs and iPads and reportedly forced the cancellation of Apple’s radically redesigned all-glass 20th-anniversary iPhone in favor of a more refined, "glass-centric" 2027 design. Meanwhile, Apple's avenues for relief are limited by geopolitical friction, as bipartisan U.S. pressure builds to blacklist Chinese memory manufacturer CXMT, whose production lines are already running at maximum capacity.

**The App Store Anti-Steering Battle: Link-Out Fees Proposed Amid Settling Efforts** · [Source](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/08/13/app-store-fees-apple-link-outs/)
Following a series of rapid-fire legal developments where U.S. District Judge Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers denied a stay of proceedings and Supreme Court Justice Elena Kagan granted a brief 24-hour emergency reprieve, Apple officially submitted its court-ordered commission fee structure for off-App Store link-outs in the United States. Under the proposed framework, developers will be **taxed 15% for external transactions**, dropping to 10% for subscriptions and 5% for participants in the Small Business Program. Simultaneously, Apple has petitioned the court to order a supervised settlement conference with Epic Games in an attempt to resolve the multi-year battle, though Epic has yet to consent to the talks.

**Passing the Torch: Tim Cook’s Final Weeks and the Ternus Transition** · [Source](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/08/11/apple-pay-chief-retiring/)
As John Ternus prepares to officially take over as Apple’s CEO on September 1, a major wave of executive departures is reshuffling Cupertino's top ranks, including the upcoming retirement of Apple Pay pioneer Jennifer Bailey and the exit of legal chief Katherine Adams. Reflecting on his legacy during CBS News interviews and the opening of Apple's new **20,000-square-foot Advanced Manufacturing Center in Houston**, outgoing CEO Tim Cook expressed a humble desire to simply be remembered as a "good and decent man". In a symbolic closing of a chapter, Apple also declared the historic 2017 iPhone X—the device that introduced the Face ID era under Cook's tenure—officially obsolete this week.

## Also Worth Knowing

*   **Alibaba Partners with Apple for China-Specific AI Model**: Apple has broken from its typical strategy of relying purely on domestic models in China by training its own custom large language model with assistance from Alibaba, securing approval from local authorities as the first foreign firm authorized to operate a proprietary model in the region.
*   **"Apple Reference Image" Targets AI-Generated Deepfakes**: Hidden within the fifth developer beta of iOS 27 is a hardware-backed image provenance system called "Apple Reference Image," which uses Private Cloud Compute to verify if a photo was shot with a genuine, unaltered iPhone camera sensor.
*   **iCloud+ 2TB Tiers Locked for Premium Siri AI Features**: Apple is planning to lock advanced server-side AI features, such as generated video summaries for HomeKit Secure Video cameras, behind premium iCloud+ tiers (at least 2TB of storage) to help offset the steep hardware and server costs of running powerful server models.
*   **M6 Chips Skipped to Accelerate M7 Debut**: To secure a dominant position in on-device AI computing, Apple is skipping the high-end M6 Pro, Max, and Ultra chips to accelerate the development of the M7 processor family for a spring 2027 debut.
