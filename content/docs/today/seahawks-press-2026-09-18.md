# Seahawks Press — 2026-09-18

## Top Takeaway

## Press Conferences & Interviews

**[Head Coach Mike Macdonald — Final Updates Ahead Of Week 2](https://www.youtube.com/watch)** · Seahawks Press Pass
Head Coach Mike Macdonald delivered a candid, highly analytical press conference ahead of Week 2, striking an optimistic yet process-driven tone as the Seahawks prepare for a 1:00 p.m. away game. The big personnel news is the return of wide receiver Tory Horton, who Macdonald noted had a great week of practice and is excited to get back on the field. When asked about managing target depth with an influx of wideouts and tight ends, Macdonald dismissed any notion of roster friction, labeling it a "good problem" and emphasizing that the offense must execute seamlessly regardless of who takes the field to prevent opposing defenses from anticipating plays. 

On the injury front, Macdonald provided notable optimism regarding quarterback Sam Darnold, stating his rehab is progressing "great" as he works his way back, while revealing running back Zach Charbonnet is nearing a target date to resume practice. Conversely, the injury report added a hip designation for Nick, rendering him questionable after a limited Friday session, though Macdonald affirmed that Christian Haynes is ready as the next man up if Anthony cannot suit up at guard. 

Schematically, Macdonald offered deep insight into Seattle's defensive edge philosophy and "shell" coverages. Responding to veteran edge defender Dante Fowler’s observation that Seattle plays edges unlike anywhere else, Macdonald explained that in light front structures, edge defenders must restrict space and play physical in the C-gap to maintain gap integrity before earning pass-rush opportunities. Additionally, Macdonald highlighted how the coaching staff integrates sub-packages for players like "T9"—describing the transition as "less expensive" in year three of the scheme because players require fewer reps and lower investment to execute role-specific calls cleanly. Throughout the session, Macdonald praised backup execution—referencing Rodney’s strong play in Taio’s absence and rookie running back Jadarian Price’s process-oriented approach—reiterating his core philosophy that front and coverage units must operate in complete unison as "12 as one".

## Injury & Roster Updates
Wide receiver Tory Horton completed a full, productive week of practice and is locked in to make his game return. Quarterback Sam Darnold continues to make encouraging strides in his rehab program, and running back Zach Charbonnet is closing in on a concrete date to return to practice. Meanwhile, Nick was added to the injury report with a hip condition and is officially questionable after being limited in practice, while guard Christian Haynes is slotted as the direct backup behind Anthony.

## Reading Between the Lines
Macdonald’s explanation of "less expensive" package adjustments indicates that the coaching staff has reached a comfort level with the roster where sub-personnel packages can be deployed on the fly without wasting valuable practice reps on wholesale installation. Furthermore, the heavy emphasis on edge discipline—demanding that outside linebackers set strict C-gap boundaries rather than chasing raw sack numbers—signals that Seattle’s defensive engine relies on suffocating run-fit control on early downs to unleash its pass rush on third down. Finally, Macdonald’s quick praise for Rodney and Jadarian Price demonstrates a firm team-wide standard: injuries will not alter game-plan expectations, and backup players are expected to deliver immediate, process-driven performance.
