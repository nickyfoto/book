## Top Stories

**[Fox Acquires Roku for $22 Billion](https://www.theverge.com/column/951850/fox-roku-predictions)** · The Verge
Traditional media is aggressively consolidating around ad-supported streaming, with Fox buying Roku in a massive $22 billion deal. The acquisition gives Fox a dominant streaming platform to pair with its Tubi service, instantly allowing it to surpass Netflix's US viewership when combined with its traditional TV networks. It's a stark reminder that the future of streaming looks remarkably similar to old-school cable television.

**[Midjourney Pivots to Hardware and Medical Scanning](https://science.slashdot.org/story/26/06/18/1939203/midjourney-pivots-from-ai-image-generation-to-body-scanning-medical-spa)** · Slashdot
AI image generation startup Midjourney is stepping far outside its software roots, unveiling a water-based, full-body ultrasonic scanner. The massive hardware prototype uses hundreds of thousands of tiny sensors to reconstruct detailed, MRI-like images of human body composition. The company intends to open a San Francisco scanning "spa" in late 2027, provided it can successfully navigate the inevitable massive regulatory hurdles and FDA approval process.

**[Apple and Intel Finalize Domestic Chip Deal](https://www.engadget.com/2197065/trump-claims-apple-intel-deal-is-done/)** · Engadget
According to President Donald Trump, Apple and Intel have successfully struck a deal to design and manufacture Apple silicon domestically in the United States. While the political victory lap surrounding Intel's foundry business is loud, Apple CEO Tim Cook separately warned that severe memory chip shortages—driven largely by the AI boom—are making imminent hardware price increases "unavoidable" for consumers.

**[Tesco Flees VMware Over Broadcom's "Abusive Conduct"](https://yro.slashdot.org/story/26/06/17/2357242/tesco-moving-40000-server-workloads-off-vmware-amid-broadcoms-abusive-conduct)** · Slashdot
Broadcom's aggressive monetization of VMware continues to alienate major enterprise clients, with UK retail giant Tesco moving 40,000 server workloads off the virtualization platform. Tesco is currently suing Broadcom in the UK High Court for breach of contract, alleging the company refused to honor perpetual licenses and demanded exorbitant subscription fees for existing software. 

**[Amazon Steps Up to Challenge Nvidia's AI Chip Monopoly](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/18/amazon-hopes-to-challenge-nvidia-more-directly-by-selling-its-ai-chips/)** · TechCrunch
Amazon Web Services is no longer content just powering its own servers; the company is in talks to sell its custom-made AI chips directly to other data centers. AWS CEO Andy Jassy views the initiative as a $50 billion opportunity, setting the stage for a brutal infrastructure war as Big Tech attempts to break its heavy reliance on Nvidia's expensive hardware.
