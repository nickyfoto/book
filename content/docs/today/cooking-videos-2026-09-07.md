### Food Science & Tips
* **Ice-Chilling for Firm Seafood:** Martin shares a brilliant science-backed tip for Teochew frozen crab (潮汕凍蟹). By steaming the crab, wrapping it tightly, and **freezing it for exactly one hour**, you suppress any muddy seafood odors and force the delicate muscle fibers to contract. This locks in the natural sugars and creates a firm, sweet, and clean-tasting cold meat.
* **No-Stick Claypots & Slime-Free Snails:** To prevent delicate small fish from sticking to the bottom of a dry claypot, Martin recommends **lining the bottom of the pot with thick ginger slices** over rendered pork lard. For baked flower snails (鹽焗花螺), he highlights two essential prep steps: soaking the snails in **warm salted water to force them to spit out their bitter slime**, and splashing **rice wine along the outside of the hot pot lid** to let the steam strip away any muddy odor.
* **Instant Flash-Cooking for Surf Clams:** When cooking delicate surf clams (貴妃貝/西施舌), Martin explains that the line between tender and rubbery is a matter of seconds. His genius solution is to slice the raw clam meat thin and **pour boiling-hot, defatted chicken broth directly over the clams in the serving bowl** rather than boiling them on the stove, instantly flash-cooking the meat to a sweet, crisp perfection.
* **Raw Flour as a Steam Binder:** In Lao Fan Gu’s steamed mooncakes, the master chefs share a crucial tip for keeping a dry nut filling together: **adding raw flour to the nut mixture**. When steamed, the raw flour cooks in the pastry's moisture, creating a beautifully dense, sticky, and unified filling that keeps the nuts from spilling out when sliced.
