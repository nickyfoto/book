# YouTube — 2026-08-25

## Watch First
In this remarkably candid exit interview, **[António Guterres on the future of the UN and the limits of superpowers | FT Interviews](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Gof_4ybio8I)** on *Financial Times*, the UN Secretary-General reflects on how geopolitical divides have paralyzed the Security Council. It is an essential, thoughtful post-mortem of our fraying post-war architecture.

## Highlights by Theme

### News & Business
*The Wall Street Journal* explores **[The Rise of the Unstoppable American Tourist](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p7eZ13T3AHw)**, analyzing how a supercharged US economy and wealthy Boomers are flooding Europe with cash while driving up local living costs and straining local housing infrastructure. For those looking to escape high domestic interest rates, the *Financial Times* explains **[Why the ‘dirt cheap’ yen is proving hard to fix | FT #shorts](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-5SxEqHe5nc)**, pointing to Japan's massive 200% debt-to-GDP ratio which makes rapid rate hikes highly risky. Meanwhile, on the ground in China, Tencent Video’s gut-wrenching Chinese-language documentary series **[【前浪 第2季​ The Silver Tide S2】EP06 | 永不停歇](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LiJmTURwjHQ)** follows an elderly couple delivering food in Shenzhen's Seg Plaza after losing 400,000 RMB of their life savings to an online scam. It is a raw, unforgettable look at the relentless grit and crushing pressure faced by China's aging working class.

### Learning & Ideas
Leading the intellectual pack is a brilliant historical lecture from LIFEANO CLUB, **[袁Sir聊古代中日关系：中国正统文化在日本？](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Rgykwoea9P8)**, which tracks how the "Little China" (小中华) ideology historically bloomed in ancient Japan, convincing Japanese intellectuals that they had preserved the pure cultural legacy of the Tang and Song Dynasties while China itself fell under Mongol and Manchu rule. On a parallel track of historical critique, Gao Xiaosong on the channel *松言松语* dissects the dark, expansionist side of the 1860s in **[#高晓松|1860 年代|社会达尔文主义|物种起源|第二次鸦片战争](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=g__R-upcnjw)**, arguing that Western democracy's high internal cost drove powers to colonize low-cost territories under the guise of Social Darwinism. Finally, Jon Alexander’s inspiring talk **[How Citizens (Not Politicians) Are Building the Future | Jon Alexander | TED](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tXZs2NuLFRA)** on *TED* urges us to transition from passive "subjects" and "consumers" into active "citizens" who build local community resilience in times of crisis rather than waiting for politicians to solve our problems.

### Tech & AI
The *Hoover Institution* hosts a fascinating discussion in **[Can Palantir and AI Transform the UK For The Better?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8aMl0CZ1ako)**, detailing how software integrates legacy IT systems to streamline the UK's National Health Service, expose corrupt police officers, and run military targeting on the frontlines in Ukraine. The channel also explores **[The Future of Quantum Technology | Stanford Emerging Technology Review](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kZn_j_ILf9M)**, mapping how quantum computing and sensing could revolutionize drug development and biological imaging, though it warns of severe hardware scaling bottlenecks and a lack of specialized algorithms. For a more immediate physical deployment of tech, Tesla's **[Deploying Megapack At Scale](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=32C0_8_296k)** shows how modular battery storage is rapidly scaling up to meet the massive 50 GW data center load projected to hit the US grid over the next five years.
