# Seahawks Press — 2026-09-17

## Top Takeaway
Drew Lock is officially taking over under center for Week 2 against the Arizona Cardinals following Sam Howell's departure, with Offensive Coordinator Brian Fleury declaring total confidence and zero hesitation in handing him the reins. Fleury emphasized that Lock will execute the full offensive game plan rather than a scaled-back playbook, backed by extensive contingency calls designed specifically for starter transitions.

## Press Conferences & Interviews

**[Drew Lock On Preparing To Start Week 2 vs. Cardinals](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=btba4k2yTuo)**
Drew Lock broke down the fundamental shift from being a backup—where a QB might only see 5 to 10 scout-team reps per week—to running live first-team reps through full Wednesday-to-Friday practice installs against Arizona. Lock made it clear that his primary directive is ball security and getting the football into the hands of Seattle's playmakers, noting the team's immaculate 14-0 record under head coach Mike Macdonald when winning the turnover margin. He also reflected warmly on his family roots in Seattle, where both of his children were born, while reaffirming that his competitive drive to be an NFL starter remains as fierce as ever. Lock displayed a candid, relaxed, and highly confident demeanor, radiating absolute comfort with Brian Fleury's play calling and his teammates in the huddle.

**[Brian Fleury On The Team's Confidence In Drew Lock](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CDtQz76KFoE)**
Offensive Coordinator Brian Fleury dismissed any notion of panic regarding the quarterback change, pointing to his past experience managing backup QB transitions during his coaching tenure in San Francisco. Fleury revealed that he prepares an extensive array of situational contingency calls ("if A, B, and C happen") and confirmed Lock has full command of the game plan. On the personnel front, Fleury clarified that rookie running back Jarian Price's limited Week 1 workload was a planned developmental ramp-up after missing the preseason, confirming Price earned an expanded role moving forward. Fleury maintained a calm, cerebral, and methodical tone throughout, offering deep insight into play sequencing, defensive technique analysis, and Jaylen Milroe's growth as the new backup QB.

**[Uchenna Nwosu: "The Developmental Process Here Is Great"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3REXKJVeEH4)**
Veteran edge rusher Uchenna Nwosu addressed an urgent operational issue from Week 1, revealing that late-game defensive line communication got rattled by crowd noise at Lumen Field and must be corrected before facing Arizona. Nwosu noted he feels significantly stronger and more agile compared to last season, having enjoyed a full healthy offseason of preparation after missing OTAs and training camp a year ago. He stressed that holding opponents under 100 rushing yards—a streak Seattle has maintained for 30 consecutive games—is an absolute "non-negotiable" cultural standard for Macdonald's defense. Nwosu brought an energetic, highly accountable veteran presence, praising the organization's "next man up" developmental pipeline and highlighting veteran edge Dante Fowler Jr.'s elite athleticism.

**[Aden Durde Talks Depth On Defense, Looks Ahead To Week 2](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5Kk5e_uXRXg)**
Defensive Coordinator Aden Durde lauded his defense for its "one play at a time" mindset during Week 1, praising the group's mental resilience and unselfish front-seven chemistry. Durde highlighted how interior pressure and coverage work hand-in-hand as a "north star," pointing to Byron Murphy II forcing a pocket collapse that directly gifted Leonard Williams a sack. He warned that facing the Cardinals' heavy 13 personnel and pre-snap motion will severely test edge setting, tackling, and eye discipline to eliminate explosive plays. Durde was thoughtful and grounded, speaking candidly about his close working relationship with Mike Macdonald and the shared pressure of building a championship defense.

**[Julian Love: "Everyone Is Appreciated Here For Who They Are"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TqW5BYDnd_Y)**
Pro Bowl safety Julian Love detailed the defensive game plan required to bottle up Cardinals tight end Trey McBride, labeling him one of the NFL's premier complete tight ends at the point of attack and downfield. Love praised the coaching staff's situational education—highlighting weekly drills from analyst Brian Eysgens—that trains every defender to operate like a field quarterback with complete awareness of game clocks and timeouts. He gave strong praise to backup safety Rodney Thomas II and cornerbacks Nehemiah Pritchett and Josh Jobe for stepping into starting roles without the defense missing a single beat. Love delivered a sharp, authentic, and passionate interview, emphasizing that Seattle's locker room culture thrives on mutual trust, personal appreciation, and relentless accountability.

## Injury & Roster Updates
With Sam Howell forced out in Week 1, Drew Lock officially steps into the starting quarterback spot, while rookie Jaylen Milroe moves up to primary backup duties. Rookie running back Jarian Price is set for an increased workload after being deliberately kept on a pitch count in Week 1 following a missed preseason. Across the secondary, safety Rodney Thomas II and cornerbacks Nehemiah Pritchett and Josh Jobe continue to log heavy starter snaps, with players and coaches treating them as established core starters rather than temporary backups.

## Reading Between the Lines
The Seahawks' coaching staff is instilling an elite level of situational football, turning defenders into pre-snap "field quarterbacks" who recognize clock management and opponent tendencies without needing sideline calls. Additionally, Lock and Nwosu's repeated references to Seattle's 14-0 record under Mike Macdonald when winning the turnover margin signal that ball protection and opportunistic takeaways remain the top organizational directive. Finally, Nwosu calling out late-game communication breakdowns at Lumen Field indicates that pre-snap verbal and non-verbal checks will be heavily scrutinized during practice this week.
