# The Ternus Era Begins, Intel Gets its Final Notice, and iOS 27 Breathes New Life into Old Hardware — 2026-09-02

## Highlights
**The transition to John Ternus as Apple's new CEO officially commenced on September 1, 2026, marking a pivotal moment as Tim Cook transitions to Chairman of the Board**. Ahead of next week's highly anticipated "Surprise and shine" event, Apple is clearing out legacy technologies by **ending developer support for Intel-based Macs on the App Store**. However, the future looks incredibly bright for software, as **iOS 27 beta testing reveals dramatic performance speedups and cooler operations even on older supported devices**.

## Top Stories
- **John Ternus Officially Assumes CEO Role as Apple Prepares for "Phenomenal" Launch Event**: On September 1, 2026, **John Ternus officially took over as Apple's Chief Executive Officer**, ending Tim Cook's 15-year tenure as Cook transitions to Chairman. In a welcome memo to employees, Ternus teased Apple's highly anticipated fall event on September 9, promising a **"huge launch that's going to be phenomenal"**. Tagged with the slogan **"Surprise and shine,"** the showcase is expected to unveil **eight new products**, including Apple's first-ever foldable smartphone (rumored as the **"iPhone Ultra"**), the **iPhone 18 Pro series, Apple Watch Series 12, Apple Watch Ultra 4, and AirPods 5**. ([Source](https://www.macworld.com/article/3223918/apples-loaded-month-set-to-kick-off-with-huge-launch-september-9.html))

- **Apple Commands End to Intel Era: Mac App Store Developers Can Now Drop Support for Legacy Hardware**: Apple has notified developers that **universal macOS applications on the Mac App Store requiring macOS 13 or later can now completely drop support for Intel-based Macs**. Rebuilding apps to target Apple Silicon (**"arm64" only**) allows developers to simplify codebases and drastically optimize download and on-device storage sizes. This change aligns with **macOS 27 Golden Gate dropping Intel-powered Mac support entirely**, with Apple confirming that macOS 27 will be the **final release to support the Rosetta 2 translation layer**. ([Source](https://www.macworld.com/article/3225932/apple-to-mac-developers-its-time-to-let-go-of-intel.html))

- **Optimization Shock: iOS 27 Beta Dramatically Speeds Up and Cools Down Older iPhones**: While Apple's WWDC presentation focused heavily on hardware-restricted Siri AI and Apple Intelligence, real-world testing of the first beta on an iPhone 11 Pro Max demonstrates **massive optimization gains for older hardware**. On an iPhone 11 Pro Max, the **Camera app launch time is cut from 0.98 seconds to 0.65 seconds**, and the **Maps app loads in just 1.36 seconds** compared to a sluggish 3.01 seconds on iOS 26. System **animations feel significantly smoother**, and the operating system **runs cooler during everyday tasks**, suggesting that Apple's under-the-hood CPU scheduling and efficiency optimizations are highly effective. ([Source](https://www.macworld.com/article/3185415/ios-27-will-actually-speed-up-your-old-iphone-heres-the-proof.html))

- **Apple Pay Checkout Gets a Complete Interface Overhaul to Resolve Card-Switching Pain Points**: In iOS 27, Apple Pay receives its most intuitive checkout design in years, streamlining transactions in apps and on the web. The updated interface **places card previews side-by-side, allowing users to easily swipe between payment cards** instead of wrestling with confusing nested menus. Additionally, the screen now utilizes **distinct, colorful icons to present billing, shipping, and address information clearly**, enabling users to verify details at a glance before double-clicking to pay. ([Source](https://9to5mac.com/2026/09/02/ios-27-gives-apple-pay-its-best-upgrade-in-years-with-redesign/))

- **Oklahoma Becomes the 16th U.S. State to Support Secure Digital IDs in Apple Wallet**: Residents of Oklahoma can now **add digital versions of their driver's licenses and state IDs to the Apple Wallet app** on iPhone and Apple Watch. Tapping the device on identity readers **releases only the specifically requested data after biometric authentication via Face ID or Touch ID**, protecting user privacy without unlocking the phone. While physical IDs remain legally required for traffic stops, these digital IDs are **accepted at TSA checkpoints across more than 250 U.S. airports** and at select businesses. ([Source](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/09/02/apple-wallet-id-feature-16th-us-state/))

- **Siri AI Data Center Demands Pose a "Defining Test" for Apple’s Environmental Commitments**: Environmental organization Greenpeace has warned that Apple's upcoming rollout of Siri AI and Apple Intelligence next month represents **a major test of its climate record**. While Apple has led the industry by utilizing renewable energy for its data centers, the **extreme power and water consumption of large-scale AI processing** will test its net-zero goals. Greenpeace highlights that Apple must ensure its AI growth is met with **absolute emission reductions, new renewable investments, and longer-lasting hardware**. ([Source](https://9to5mac.com/2026/09/02/ai-will-be-the-defining-test-of-apples-environmental-commitments-says-greenpeace/))

## Articles Worth Reading

**An iPhone Ultra Stylus Would Be a Colossal Mistake** ([Source](https://www.macworld.com/article/3225539/an-iphone-ultra-stylus-would-be-a-colossal-mistake.html))
With Bloomberg's Mark Gurman reporting that **Apple has prototyped and tested Apple Pencil compatibility for its upcoming foldable "iPhone Ultra,"** this opinion piece argues that introducing a stylus would be a severe misstep. Grounded in Steve Jobs' historic aversion to phone styluses, the author notes that accessories introduce unnecessary friction and are easily lost. Practical complications like **awkward magnetic storage**, potential **damage or unsightly marks on sensitive folding displays**, and **over-complicating muscle memory** during a major hardware transition are highlighted. Ultimately, the article suggests Apple should resist over-engineering its premium folding phone and stick to its touch-based design philosophy.

**Siri AI Won't Be Your Friend, and Why That Really Matters** ([Source](https://9to5mac.com/2026/09/02/siri-ai-wont-be-your-friend-and-heres-why-that-really-matters/))
This article examines a concerning Washington Post survey revealing that **over a quarter of Americans interact with AI chatbots as close friends or therapists** to combat loneliness. It praises Apple executives Craig Federighi and Greg Joswiak for **explicitly designing Siri AI to reject parasocial relationships and sycophantic behavior**. By keeping Siri strictly focused on utility, world knowledge, and task completion, Apple is taking a responsible ethical stance against emotional substitution and social isolation. This is a must-read for anyone interested in AI ethics, illustrating why Apple's decision to limit Siri's emotional engagement is a necessary boundary.

**Wavlink Thunderbolt 5 Dock Review: Affordable, Fast — Now with SSD Slot Option** ([Source](https://www.macworld.com/article/3009184/wavlink-thunderbolt-5-dock-review.html))
Wavlink's new Thunderbolt 5 Docking Station receives a highly positive review for offering **impressive 80Gbps speed, 2.5Gb Ethernet, and 140W power delivery** at a sub-\$300 price point. The premium UTD58M version (\$349.99) **adds a built-in PCIe Gen 4 x4 M.2 SSD slot under its base**, enabling high-speed local storage expansion without external enclosures. While the review notes an awkward front-facing upstream port, the dock's robust 230W power supply and versatile port selection beat out more expensive competitors. It provides a compelling, cost-effective alternative for users looking to **bypass Apple's steep built-in storage upgrade fees**.

**iOS 27 basically turns your iPhone and AirPods into an Apple Watch at the gym** ([Source](https://9to5mac.com/2026/09/02/ios-27-basically-turns-your-iphone-and-airpods-into-an-apple-watch-at-the-gym/))
This piece reviews a fascinating new fitness feature in iOS 27 that **brings the Apple Watch GymKit experience to users without requiring a smartwatch**. By tapping an iPhone to a compatible treadmill, the machine **receives heart-rate tracking data directly from AirPods Pro 3**, while the iPhone's Fitness app captures the treadmill’s precise speed, distance, and incline. The review highlights how this clever integration of mobile and audio hardware serves as an **exceptional backup for forgotten or dead smartwatches**. It’s an essential read for fitness enthusiasts, illustrating how Apple is utilizing its broader hardware ecosystem to complete its athletic tracking ecosystem.
