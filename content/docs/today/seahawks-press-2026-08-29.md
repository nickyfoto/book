# Seahawks Press — 2026-08-29

## Top Takeaway
Head coach **Mike Macdonald confirmed that newly acquired cornerback Trevon Diggs is immediately "viable" to suit up for Week 1** after Diggs grabbed an interception across three budgeted series in the preseason finale. Diggs backed up that timeline at the podium, declaring himself completely healthy and ready to roll in whatever role the staff requires after seven months away from live game action.

## Press Conferences & Interviews

**[Mike Macdonald: "I Think We're In A Great Place Going Into The Season"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EvN07crddMw)** · [Seahawks Press Pass](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EvN07crddMw)
Macdonald was measured yet quietly upbeat following the exhibition finale, emphasizing that the team's operational foundation is in a "great place" even if vanilla preseason tape did not showcase everything the starters built during camp. The head coach confirmed Diggs' readiness for the regular season opener while praising quarterback Jalen Milroe's poise and decision-making behind an emergency offensive line featuring two offensive tackles acquired roughly 72 hours before kickoff. On the schematic side, Macdonald delivered clear football reality over coach-speak, noting that Seattle cannot drop back 50 times a game in the NFL and will rely heavily on moving the pocket, boot action, and improved run-game efficiency to keep the offense on schedule. When questioned on running back Emanuel Wilson's health, Macdonald was evasive, offering only that Wilson would return "at some point this year".

**[Trevon Diggs: "It's Been A Blessing To Be A Part Of The Seahawks"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6VXIovXBeT8)** · [Seahawks Press Pass](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6VXIovXBeT8)
Diggs was visibly energized and relieved after recording an interception in his Seattle debut, calling the opportunity a "blessing" after being sidelined for seven months. He explained that getting live exhibition reps was essential for shedding rust and shaking off early jitters, especially after logging only two padded practices with the team. Diggs highlighted the immediate locker-room brotherhood when veterans sprinted down the sideline to celebrate his turnover, adding that executing calls on the grass makes absorbing Macdonald's complex system far easier than staring at playbook diagrams. Showing total buy-in, Diggs stated he is fully healthy and ready for any assignment asked of him—even joking he would line up at wide receiver if called upon.

**[Jalen Milroe On His Performance In The Preseason Finale](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l-9BGhDa8SU)** · [Seahawks Press Pass](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l-9BGhDa8SU)
Milroe handled his postgame availability with impressive accountability, identifying "decisiveness" as both the coaching staff's primary directive and his biggest personal improvement from Year 1 to Year 2. He walked through his 40-yard strike to Cody White out of his own end zone, praising his protection and wideout execution while highlighting the offense's ability to stay poised in hostile territory. Milroe acknowledged his deep frustration over the previous week's sloppy outing in Nashville, noting that his sole priority remains winning, whether operating a two-minute drive or bleeding the clock in four-minute situational mode. Crucially, the young passer expressed heartfelt appreciation for the trust Macdonald and general manager John Schneider showed by giving him the entire game to navigate adjustments and grow.

**[Nehemiah Pritchett: "I've Done A Pretty Good Job Of Taking The Next Step"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qo0t4TG8QmM)** · [Seahawks Press Pass](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qo0t4TG8QmM)
Pritchett brought a calm, businesslike focus to the podium, breaking down his critical third-down red zone pass breakup as the direct fruit of dedicated film study and diagnosing route concepts. Asked about Seattle trading for a star like Diggs at his position, Pritchett did not blink, stating his mindset remains entirely on controlling his own progress and competing every day. The cornerback spotlighted tackling as his primary offseason priority, detailing the extra work he puts in before and after practice in assistant coach CP's "EAT" drill circuit. That effort has clearly registered with the coaching staff, as Macdonald separately praised Pritchett's maturity, tackling reliability, and consistent camp performance.

## Injury & Roster Updates
With roster cutdowns arriving over the next few days, Macdonald noted the organization faces pivotal roster decisions to assemble the initial 53-man squad. The offensive line remains an active construction zone after Seattle had to play the finale with two reserve tackles who had collectively spent barely 72 hours with the team, one of whom Macdonald only met at the team hotel on the way to the stadium. On the injury front, the timeline for running back Emanuel Wilson remains vague, with Macdonald declining to provide specifics beyond stating Wilson will be back "at some point this year".

## Reading Between the Lines
Macdonald’s frank comment about protecting his quarterback by avoiding 50 traditional dropbacks signals that Seattle's regular-season offense will lean heavily into bootlegs, designed movement, and early-down rushing to mask offensive line vulnerability. Holding out the core starters for the entire preseason while strictly scripting a three-drive tune-up for Diggs confirms Macdonald already has his starting defensive framework locked in place. Pritchett's prominent role on special teams and his lockdown third-down play show he has likely solidified his roster spot, even as Diggs' rapid integration reshuffles the outside cornerback rotation.
