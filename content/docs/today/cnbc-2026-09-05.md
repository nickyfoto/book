# CNBC — 2026-09-05

## Lead Story
U.S. military forces launched a coordinated missile strike Saturday disabling three Iranian crude oil carriers near Kharg Island and the Gulf of Oman, a major escalation following an Iranian ballistic missile attack on Navy warships that has effectively closed the strategic Strait of Hormuz. This direct confrontation has injected massive geopolitical risk into global energy markets, threatening to choke off a vital shipping lane and driving fears of a broader oil supply shock.

## Markets & Economics
U.S. stock indexes kicked off September on shaky footing, with the S&P 500 up just 0.1% for the week and the Nasdaq gaining 0.4%. Bond yields rose sharply after a stronger-than-expected August jobs report showed 162,000 payroll additions, prompting markets to price in a 58% probability of a quarter-point Federal Reserve rate hike on September 15-16. This looming rate decision has triggered an unusually aggressive public pressure campaign from the Trump administration, highlighted by Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent expressing serious economic concerns about rate hikes during a supply shock in a **[CNBC Interview](https://www.cnbc.com/video/2026/08/31/watch-cnbcs-full-interview-with-treasury-secretary-scott-bessent.html)**. Adding to severe inflationary pressures, global crude jumped back over \$90 a barrel, sending national average gasoline prices to a record Labor Day high of \$4.03 per gallon, while diesel prices reached an all-time peak of \$5.85 a gallon.

In a featured Squawk Box appearance, Berkshire Hathaway CEO Greg Abel detailed his AI playbook, emphasizing BHE's massive data center energy opportunity and recounting how Berkshire completed a \$10 billion direct stock purchase of Alphabet shares at a 6.5% discount to fund Google's computer infrastructure; watch the **[Greg Abel Full Interview](https://www.cnbc.com/video/2026/09/02/watch-cnbcs-full-interview-with-berkshire-hathaway-ceo-greg-abel.html)** for more. Meanwhile, Walmart is aggressively challenging food delivery platforms with its new "Walmart Restaurant Delivery" service, expanding home delivery of Dunkin' products to nearly 10,000 U.S. locations after a successful Express Delivery pilot with Subway. In aviation, Etihad Airways is doubling down on fleet expansion and targeting a break-even year despite Middle East flight disruptions and soaring jet-fuel costs, as explained in the **[Etihad CEO Interview](https://www.cnbc.com/video/2026/09/05/etihad-ceo-iran-war-break-even.html)**. Finally, General Motors and Ford are leveraging their unused EV battery plants to pivot into the grid-scale energy storage system (ESS) and U.S. defense markets, seeking to tap into the data center boom and offset slowing domestic vehicle sales.

## Investing & Commentary
Jim Cramer's Charitable Trust went more defensive this week, locking in gains by trimming Palo Alto Networks and exiting Corning to redeploy capital into less cyclical names like BNY and high-yielding Kimberly-Clark. On the research front, Goldman Sachs is urging investors to buy the dip in five high-conviction ideas, identifying **[Alibaba Group](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/05/buy-the-dip-in-these-five-stocks-before-its-too-late-goldman-says.html)**, Ulta Beauty, and cruise operator Viking Holdings as heavily undervalued plays with strong operational catalysts ahead. Additionally, the Trump administration took an unprecedented step in "state capitalism," with the Pentagon securing a 35% equity stake in North American Blue Energy Partners (NABEP) to control 65 billion barrels of Venezuelan oil reserves, bypassing major oil producers that have labeled Venezuela "uninvestable".
