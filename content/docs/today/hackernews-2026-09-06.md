**[The car industry A/B tested selling a car with and without CarPlay](https://a.wholelottanothing.org/the-car-industry-a-b-tested-selling-the-same-car-with-and-without-carplay-and-the-results-are-not-shocking/)** · [A Whole Lotta Nothing](https://a.wholelottanothing.org/the-car-industry-a-b-tested-selling-the-same-car-with-and-without-carplay-and-the-results-are-not-shocking/)
When General Motors controversially decided to dump Apple CarPlay and Android Auto in favor of its own proprietary infotainment subscription model, it unwittingly set up a perfect market experiment. Lacking its own EV research and development, Honda partnered with GM to build the Honda Prologue—which is fundamentally a Chevy Blazer EV with Honda badging—but Honda insisted on keeping CarPlay, whereas GM stripped it out. The sales figures are a devastating blow to GM's strategy: the CarPlay-equipped Honda Prologue outsold its identical GM sibling by 42.8% in 2024, 73.1% in 2025, and a staggering 165.5% in early 2026, demonstrating that consumers will aggressively penalize brands that hold their smartphone integration hostage.

**[Any Nix package, live in the browser](https://fzakaria.com/2026/09/04/any-nix-package-live-in-your-browser)** · [fzakaria.com](https://fzakaria.com/2026/09/04/any-nix-package-live-in-your-browser)
In a tour de force of systems hacking, developer Farooq Zakaria built 'trynix', a browser-based runner that lets you execute any of the 310,000+ historical packages in the Nix ecosystem instantly inside a browser tab. By booting a real x86_64 Linux kernel using QEMU compiled to WebAssembly (qemu-wasm) and pulling pre-built store-path binaries directly from static file caches, trynix turns the browser into a fully sandboxed client with zero native installation steps. The community is buzzing over the practical implications, which range from running "Works on my machine" URLs for bulletproof bug reproductions to reviewing pull requests by running the compiled software directly in a browser tab without cloning.

**[The revolt of the reader](https://bcantrill.dtrace.org/2026/09/05/the-revolt-of-the-reader/)** · [Bryan Cantrill's Blog](https://bcantrill.dtrace.org/2026/09/05/the-revolt-of-the-reader/)
Bryan Cantrill delivers a sharp, beautifully written critique of the flood of AI-generated prose cluttering professional feeds and technical blogs. Pointing to a survey of 668 developers where 78% admitted they "stop reading immediately" upon detecting LLM-authored text and 71% resolve to avoid that author entirely in the future, Cantrill argues that outsourcing writing to an LLM violates the fundamental social contract between writer and reader. Commenters strongly resonate with the call to arms, agreeing that while LLMs are superlative editors, forcing readers to parse sentences that the author themselves didn't bother to work to create is both lazy and ineffective.

**[Following legal advice, the Nitter project will continue](https://github.com/zedeus/nitter)** · [GitHub](https://github.com/zedeus/nitter)
Despite receiving cease and desist letters from X Corp. demanding a permanent takedown of the project and its repository, the open-source alternative Twitter frontend Nitter has announced it is continuing development after consulting with legal counsel. Operating as a lightweight, JavaScript-free, and privacy-respecting gateway, Nitter prevents IP and fingerprint tracking while remaining roughly 15 times lighter than X's bloated web client. The community is rallying behind the developer's defiance, viewing the decision as a rare and critical victory for open-web alternatives in an era of aggressive corporate platform lockdowns.

**[Chrome again exempts Google from user site data settings](https://lapcatsoftware.com/articles/2026/9/1.html)** · [Lapcat Software](https://lapcatsoftware.com/articles/2026/9/1.html)
Developer Jeff Johnson has discovered that Google Chrome is once again quietly exempting Google-owned sites from the browser's automatic 'delete site data on close' settings. This replicates a notorious "bug" from six years ago that was supposed to have been fixed after widespread public backlash. While Johnson generously attributes the repeat failure to Hanlon's razor, the Hacker News comment section is far less forgiving, pointing out that Google's recurring, highly profitable QA oversights always seem to break in a direction that preserves their own tracking capability.

**[A/I shuts down – Stay human](https://keepitfree.ai/announcements/a/i-shuts-down-stay-human/)** · [Keep It Free](https://keepitfree.ai/announcements/a/i-shuts-down-stay-human/)
The Autistici/Inventati (A/I) collective has announced it is shutting down and discontinuing all digital services after 25 years of defending secure, non-commercial, and privacy-friendly digital infrastructure. Commenters are lamenting the closure as a sobering milestone, marking the rapid erosion of independent, politically committed internet sanctuaries in a hostile global climate.

## Discussion & Debate
AI hype and evaluation metrics took a beating in a combined discussion surrounding **[Recreating Minecraft Is Not a Benchmark](https://kuber.studio/blog/Reflections/Recreating-Minecraft-is-Not-a-Benchmark)** and the physical robot performance of **[GPT-6 Astra on robot arms](https://openai.robocurve.org/gpt-6-astra/)**. Technical commenters are noting that while new models can easily generate flashy, social-media-friendly "demo-benchmarks"—such as writing Minecraft in one prompt or animating a pelican riding a bicycle—they still stall and fail at the exact same physical precision limits, like inserting a puzzle piece into a matching board groove. The consensus is that public, static, and famous test sets are severely leaking into training data, and the community is calling for robust holdout evaluations like LiveBench over manufactured, overfitted marketing spectacles.
