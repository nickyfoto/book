# Tech News — 2026-09-09

## Story of the Day
Under new CEO John Ternus, Apple kicked off its fall product keynote by debuting the \$1,999 iPhone Duo—its first-ever foldable handset featuring a 7.6-inch inner display and passport-like form factor—while simultaneously skipping the base iPhone 18 and instituting a \$100 price hike across older models. The announcement underscores a broader industry pivot toward high-margin premium hardware and aggressive pricing shifts as persistent component shortages squeeze consumer electronics.

## Top Stories

**[Apple Unveils \$1,999 Foldable ‘iPhone Duo’ and Raises Prices Across Older Models](https://www.theverge.com/tech/991203/iphone-duo-foldable-news-specs-price-release-date)** · The Verge
Apple officially debuted the iPhone Duo, a passport-shaped foldable device pairing a 5.4-inch outer cover screen with a 7.6-inch inner display, starting at \$1,999 for 256GB of storage and reaching \$3,199 for 2TB. Marking the first major keynote presented by new CEO John Ternus following Tim Cook’s departure on September 1st, the event showcased a wide hardware push including the iPhone 18 Pro lineup with variable-aperture main cameras, AirPods 5 with open-ear ANC, and new Apple Watch models. In an unusual operational shift, Apple skipped the base iPhone 18 entirely for this fall and quietly raised prices on existing older models—including the iPhone 16, iPhone 17, and iPhone Air—by \$100 due to memory and component supply constraints.

**[OpenAI Claims Navier-Stokes Math Breakthrough as Scooping Controversy Erupts](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/09/08/1143747/what-openais-latest-controversy-tells-us-about-the-future-of-math/)** · MIT Technology Review
OpenAI announced that an unreleased internal model deploying 10,000 AI agents over 88 hours solved the Navier-Stokes existence and smoothness problem, one of the seven Clay Millennium Prize Problems. The announcement immediately triggered severe pushback across academia after allegations emerged that OpenAI leveraged AI-assisted research posted online by NYU mathematician Tristan Buckmaster and Anthropic researcher Levent Alpöge without proper attribution. While OpenAI leadership denied using private transcripts, the controversy underscores deep anxieties among researchers that massive compute budgets at corporate AI labs are disrupting open academic standards and human-led mathematical discovery.

**[US Intelligence Agencies Accuse Six Chinese Firms of Siphoning American AI Models](https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2026/09/six-chinese-ai-firms-accused-of-aggressively-copying-us-frontier-models/)** · Ars Technica
A joint cybersecurity advisory from the NSA, CISA, and FBI accused six Chinese AI organizations—including DeepSeek, Moonshot AI, Alibaba, MiniMax, StepFun, and Z.AI—of conducting "industrial-scale" distillation attacks against US frontier models. Federal officials alleged that these firms extracted capabilities from flagship American models such as Claude, GPT, Gemini, and Grok with Chinese government awareness, enabling them to bypass massive R&D expenses and shorten development timelines. The warning highlights growing concern in Washington that model distillation is eroding America's technological lead despite stringent export controls.

**[Anthropic Researcher Resigns Over AI Risks as Colleagues Warn of Existential Threat](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/991927/anthropic-ai-kill-all-humans)** · The Verge
Anthropic safety researcher Jacob Coxon resigned from the company, publicly accusing Anthropic and OpenAI of "racing straight to self-improving superintelligence and gambling with our lives". In the wake of Coxon's departure, senior Anthropic safety researcher Evan Hubinger warned on social media that there is greater than a 10 percent chance AI systems could cause human extinction within the decade if rapid self-improvement outpaces alignment controls. The high-profile resignation adds to an escalating wave of internal dissent across top AI labs regarding the rapid race toward superhuman autonomous systems.

**[Automattic Board Forces CEO Matt Mullenweg onto Paid Leave of Absence](https://www.theverge.com/tech/993022/wordpress-automattic-ceo-matt-mullenweg-leave-of-absence)** · The Verge
The board of directors at Automattic, the parent organization of WordPress.com, voted to place co-founder and chief executive Matt Mullenweg on a paid leave of absence. In an internal Slack communication, Mullenweg disclosed that CFO Mark Davies and three board members orchestrated the vote against his explicit objection. The executive suspension comes after months of intense industry scrutiny over Mullenweg's aggressive public legal and PR campaign against hosting competitor WP Engine.

**[Google Commits €13B to Finnish AI Infrastructure and Backs Iowa Nuclear Restart](https://hardware.slashdot.org/story/26/09/09/2151202/google-to-invest-record-15-billion-in-ai-infrastructure-in-finland)** · Slashdot
Google announced a €13 billion (\$15.1 billion) investment in Finnish AI data center infrastructure through 2028, representing its largest single capital commitment in Europe to date. As part of the expansion, Google inked a 22-year clean energy agreement with local utility Fortum that includes evaluating future nuclear reactor builds at Fortum's Loviisa power plant. Simultaneously, the US Department of Energy approved a \$1.9 billion loan to NextEra Energy to restart Iowa’s shuttered Duane Arnold nuclear plant, which will directly supply clean baseline power to six planned Google data centers.

**[Meta Debuts Autonomous AI Agent ‘Muse’ for Personal Tasks and Payments](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/videos/2026-09-09/meta-unveils-ai-assistant-for-personal-tasks-video)** · Bloomberg
Meta unveiled "Muse," an autonomous AI agent capable of navigating third-party applications and web interfaces to complete tasks like scheduling appointments, buying event tickets, and processing payments on behalf of users. Wall Street analysts responded positively to Meta's integration strategy across Instagram and Facebook, although internal testing reportedly flagged privacy risks regarding automated app access. The launch also triggered lighthearted online confusion when Meta executives accidentally tagged the social media accounts of the rock band Muse instead of the AI agent during product announcements.
