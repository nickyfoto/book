# Apple Ecosystem Digest: Pre-Keynote Leaks, Foldable Ambitions, and Neural Sensing Breakthroughs — 2026-09-08

## Highlights
On the eve of Apple's "Surprise and Shine" September 9 keynote, last-minute leaks have revealed extensive details regarding the foldable "iPhone Ultra," iOS 27 pro camera software additions, and watchOS 27 health tracking capabilities. Strategic moves across the ecosystem also highlight Apple's future hardware roadmap, headlined by the acquisition of neural-sensing startup Sonera and developer-focused tooling releases like Logitech's MX Keypad. Meanwhile, Apple released iOS 26.6.2 and iPadOS 26.6.2 to resolve a cellular update bug ahead of next week's major operating system rollouts.

**[Logitech Releases the Developer-Focused MX Keypad for AI Automation](https://www.macstories.net/news/logitech-releases-the-developer-focused-mx-keypad-for-ai-automation/)** ([MacStories](https://www.macstories.net/news/logitech-releases-the-developer-focused-mx-keypad-for-ai-automation/))
Logitech introduced the \$99 MX Keypad, a compact accessory featuring nine customizable color LCD keys aimed at developers and automators. Derived from the keypad portion of Logitech's MX Creative Console, the standalone unit removes the creative dialpad to offer an affordable desktop control center for AI workflows. Connecting via USB-C, the keypad supports Logi Options+ Smart Actions, custom macros, and a dedicated Logi Actions SDK for developers building agentic tools. The device launches alongside an official GitHub Copilot plugin, permitting coders to trigger saved prompts, launch agents, and monitor status dynamically. It is an essential read for software engineers and power users seeking tactile control over automated coding tools.

**[BASF Subsidiary trinamiX Sues Apple Over Alleged Face ID Patent Infringement](https://9to5mac.com/2026/09/07/basf-subsidiary-sues-apple-over-alleged-face-id-patent-infringement/)** ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/09/07/basf-subsidiary-sues-apple-over-alleged-face-id-patent-infringement/))
Biometric sensor developer trinamiX, a spin-off of BASF, filed a lawsuit against Apple in Texas federal court alleging infringement of seven patents. The lawsuit claims that modern Face ID systems on recent iPhones and iPad Pros incorporate trinamiX's optical skin-detection and material-analysis technologies. These technologies project light patterns to verify physical skin properties, preventing authentication bypasses using 3D masks or photos. TrinamiX is seeking an injunction to halt the importation and sale of accused Apple hardware, as well as monetary damages. It is a compelling read for legal observers following intellectual property disputes surrounding consumer biometric security.
