### Food Science & Tips
* **Thermal Conduction & Residual Steam**: Chef James shares a crucial tip to prevent dry, rubbery salmon: turn off the burner when the thick fillet reaches **70% doneness**, keep the lid closed, and let internal heat conduction gently steam the interior to perfection. This retains essential juices without overcooking the protein or burning the skin.
* **Sauce Reduction Chemistry**: Chef James explains that *teriyaki* (照燒) literally describes a reflective, glossy shine created by caramelizing sugars and mirin. The sauce must be simmered in rendered fish fat until large, viscous bubbles cover the pan; pouring it too thin or too early will soak into the rice and make it soggy.
* **Starch Velveting & Acidity Balance**: Chef Chen breaks down the culinary mechanics of meat velveting (*上浆*): massaging pork with starch, liquid seasoning, and an oil seal keeps the meat from shrinking during stir-frying, yielding plump, tender slices. Additionally, introducing a splash of broth when flashing vinegar tempers the sharp acidic punch—a technique in traditional Chinese wok cooking called **"folding the vinegar mouth" (折醋口)**.
