# Seattle Local — 2026-09-05

## Top Story

## Local News

**[Sound Transit Probes Thousands of Fish Deaths Near Floating Light Rail Line](https://bit.ly/4yf20I5)** · KOMO News
Sound Transit is scrambling to find solutions after approximately 2,000 dead fish, primarily nonnative perch and bass alongside some salmon, were discovered in Lake Washington near the newly opened I-90 floating bridge light rail line. Officials suspect the deaths may have been caused by an electrical grounding or protection system on the Crosslake Connection line, which opened on March 28. Dive teams, biologists, and engineers have been deployed to investigate the issue and implement urgent fixes.

**[Aurora Avenue Shooting Leaves Woman Critically Injured Just One Day After Safety Forum](https://www.king5.com/article/news/crime/we-need-to-clean-it-up-woman-shot-along-aurora-avenue-after-safety-meeting/281-3ee2e9dc-144f-4b00-850a-bb9f3f889a46)** · KING 5
A 20-year-old woman remains in critical condition after being shot on Aurora Avenue North near North 125th Street in North Seattle's Bitter Lake area on Thursday night. The shooting took place just 24 hours after a heated community safety forum held by the North Precinct Advisory Council to address crime and violence in the neighborhood. According to police, the suspect shot the woman from a black SUV following an earlier altercation at a nearby hotel and fled the scene.

**[The Seattle Times Sues OpenAI and Microsoft for Copyright Infringement](https://www.seattletimes.com/business/local-business/seattle-times-sues-openai-microsoft-over-copyright-infringement/?utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=owned_echobox_tw_m&utm_source=Twitter#Echobox=1788567025-3)** · The Seattle Times
The Seattle Times Company has filed a federal lawsuit against OpenAI and Microsoft, alleging the tech companies violated copyright law by using the newspaper's high-quality local articles without permission to train their artificial intelligence models. The lawsuit joins a growing list of complaints from publishers, authors, and musicians seeking to protect intellectual property rights against generative AI developers. This litigation represents a significant stand by local journalism against unauthorized AI content scraping.

**[Tulalip Tribes Enter Discussions to Back Bid for Sonics Return in Marysville](https://www.kiro7.com/news/local/tulalip-tribe-is-talks-bid-sonics-return-washington/WBJMIPISIJG5TKW6TY2JU3MQOI/?utm_term=Autofeed&utm_medium=Social&utm_source=Twitter#Echobox=1788580956)** · KIRO 7
The Tulalip Tribes confirmed they are in active discussions regarding a private equity firm’s bid to bring the Supersonics NBA franchise back to Western Washington. The proposal outlines constructing a professional basketball arena on or near tribal land in Marysville. Tribal leaders expressed strong support, noting the project would help diversify the local economy while creating community connections and expanding entertainment options.

**[South Seattle Neighbors Plead for City Action Over Beacon Avenue Encampment](https://bit.ly/4yiPmYE)** · KOMO News
South Seattle residents are banding together to push the city for immediate intervention at a rapidly growing homeless encampment near 42nd Avenue South and Beacon Avenue South. The camp has ballooned to several acres, spreading across multiple public and private properties. Frustrated neighbors have organized community efforts, calling the current conditions a "humanitarian crisis" and warning of imminent tragedy if the city does not clear the site.

**[Washington State Employees Stage Second Walkout Amid Pay Disputes](https://www.seattletimes.com/seattle-news/politics/wa-state-workers-walk-out-again-over-pay-negotiations-with-governor/?utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=owned_echobox_tw_m&utm_source=Twitter#Echobox=1788650519-3)** · The Seattle Times
Washington state workers staged a second walkout on Saturday to protest ongoing salary negotiations and demand better compensation from the governor's office. Employees claim that understaffing has led to unsustainable workloads that are hurting public service delivery. This follows a similar walkout earlier in the week as state employee unions increase pressure on administrators.

**[Washington State Law Requires Student Vaccinations as Back-to-School Season Resumes](https://komonews.com/news/back-to-school/child-vaccines-back-to-school-students-washington-state-law-proof-of-immunization-or-an-official-exemption-flu-shot-updated-covid-booster)** · KOMO News
As local school districts prepare to resume classes next week, state health officials are reminding parents that Washington law strictly requires proof of immunization or an official exemption before children can attend school or childcare. Local clinics are experiencing a rush of families catching up on pediatric vaccines. Health officials advise parents to review updated federal guidance on pediatric immunizations, including flu shots and updated COVID-19 boosters, to ensure students are ready for class.
