# Seattle Local — Week of 2026-08-28 to 2026-09-04

## Top Story
After intense contract negotiations and a looming teacher strike that threatened to delay the school year for **50,000 students**, **Seattle Public Schools** and the **Seattle Education Association** reached a tentative agreement late Tuesday night. The last-minute compromise successfully averted a districtwide walkout, allowing classes to begin on schedule on Wednesday, September 2. The agreement resolved a high-stakes standoff over class sizes, special education staffing, and cost-of-living pay raises.

King County has officially finalized the **\$14 million purchase** of industrial property along the Cedar River near Renton, permanently ending a long-running community fight against a proposed Lakeside Industries asphalt plant. County officials plan to permanently preserve the riverfront acreage for public open space, recreational trails, and habitat restoration.

Thousands of public employees represented by the **Washington Federation of State Employees** walked off the job at more than 100 regional worksites. Organized under the **"Keep Washington Running"** campaign, the workers are protesting wage proposals from Governor Bob Ferguson's administration, which they argue do not keep pace with escalating living costs. The ongoing labor dispute has threatened to disrupt essential government services throughout Western Washington.

**[Seattle Police Pauses Hiring Incentives Amid Stark Recruiting Disparities](https://komonews.com/news/local/seattle-police-to-pause-hiring-incentives-sept-1-amid-budget-forecast-concerns-detective-hire-officer-employment-jobs-economy-katie-wilson-shone-barnes-sayles)** · [KOMO News](https://komonews.com/news/local/seattle-police-to-pause-hiring-incentives-sept-1-amid-budget-forecast-concerns-detective-hire-officer-employment-jobs-economy-katie-wilson-shone-barnes-sayles)
The **Seattle Police Department** has frozen all recruitment and lateral hiring incentives starting September 1 to assess its long-term budget forecast. The suspension has drawn sharp criticism from the Seattle Police Officers Guild, which called the decision premature given that the department is operating hundreds of officers below its staffing targets. This pause highlights a regional recruiting disparity as neighboring **Bellevue** continues to advertise **\$30,000 signing bonuses** to attract lateral transfers.

**[Sound Transit Approves \$87 Million Fare Gate Pilot to Curb Evasion](https://www.seattletimes.com/seattle-news/transportation/seattle-light-rail-inches-closer-to-installing-fare-gates/?utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=owned_echobox_tw_m&utm_source=Twitter#Echobox=1787873269-1)** · [The Seattle Times](https://www.seattletimes.com/seattle-news/transportation/seattle-light-rail-inches-closer-to-installing-fare-gates/?utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=owned_echobox_tw_m&utm_source=Twitter#Echobox=1787873269-1)
Sound Transit’s governing board has approved an **\$87 million pilot program** to install and test physical fare gates at up to 14 high-traffic Link light rail stations. The retrofit program represents a significant departure from the agency's traditional, barrier-free "proof-of-payment" system. Designed to improve fare compliance and enhance security, the new gates are slated to launch at stations like Westlake, Capitol Hill, and the University of Washington between 2029 and 2030.
