# CNBC — 2026-04-03

## Lead Story
Energy markets went parabolic as the U.S.-Iran conflict escalated, with physical Brent crude surging past $141 a barrel and President Trump threatening to systematically destroy Iranian infrastructure. 

## Markets & Economics
The U.S. labor market delivered a major upside surprise, with the [U.S. economy adds 178K jobs in March, unemployment rate dips slightly to 4.3%](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/04/03/jobs-report-march-2026-.html) blowing past consensus estimates of 59,000. Wage growth remained tepid at 0.2% for the month, representing the lowest annual increase since May 2021 and keeping the Federal Reserve on hold. However, positive macroeconomic data was entirely overshadowed by geopolitical panic in the energy sector. U.S. crude futures spiked nearly 12% to $112.06 a barrel after Iran shut down tanker traffic in the Strait of Hormuz, prompting a defiant response from the White House in [Watch Pres. Trump's full address on Iran from the White House](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/04/03/trump-iran-threats-un-resolution-blocked-strait-of-hormuz-f35-shot-down.html).

## Business & Earnings
Big Tech is facing a severe legal reckoning after a landmark jury verdict pierced the 30-year-old Section 230 shield, seeing [Meta shares plunge 8% for worst day since October after back-to-back child safety losses in court](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/04/03/meta-google-under-attack-court-cases-bypass-30-year-old-legal-shield.html). Meanwhile, OpenAI is confusing Wall Street with its M&A strategy following the purchase of tech media network TBPN, though leadership maintains they are playing the long game in [OpenAI sees more opportunity in enterprise, coding AI than consumer side: Big Technology's Kantrowitz](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/04/03/chasing-vibes-openai-ma-strategy-gets-more-confusing-with-tbpn-.html). In the broader economy, corporate America continues to wrestle with sweeping global supply chain disruptions, as explored in [How industries are faring one year after Trump's tariffs](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/04/03/trump-tariffs-trade-war-impact.html).

## Investing & Commentary
Despite geopolitical headwinds, the structural AI narrative continues to present opportunities in the semiconductor space. Morgan Stanley's Joe Moore argues that [Memory stock fundamentals will remain strong if AI capex continues](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/04/03/chinese-chip-firms-record-revenue-ai-boom-us-curbs.html), identifying clear tailwinds for hardware components. On the software side, however, concerns are growing over Microsoft's AI monetization timeline, with Deepwater's Gene Munster warning that a ['Seat decline' in workforce a looming risk for Microsoft](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/04/02/microsoft-executive-touts-copilot-traction-after-analyst-pressure.html) could threaten the tech giant's long-term recurring revenue.
