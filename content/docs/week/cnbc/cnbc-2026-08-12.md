# CNBC — 2026-08-12

## Lead Story
**July’s consumer price index came in right down the plate to ease immediate Fed rate-hike anxieties, while a blowout, obsolescence-shattering earnings beat from neocloud powerhouse CoreWeave sent AI infrastructure stocks on a massive run. Together, these twin drivers broke a two-day market slide, reinforcing Wall Street’s secular bet on high-performance compute.**

## Markets & Economics
**The S&P 500 snapped a two-day slide as July headline CPI moderated to 3.4% and core inflation came in at 2.5% year-over-year, matching consensus estimates. This in-line report pushed down bond yields, prompting traders to slash the probability of a September Federal Reserve rate hike to a modest 38%-42%. However, commodity markets flashed supply-side warnings as Brent crude surged 1.24% to \$90 per barrel after deadly Houthi attacks killed six in the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, leaving shipping transits through the Strait of Hormuz 90% below prewar averages. These geopolitical bottlenecks prompted the IEA to raise its 2026 demand destruction forecast to a steep 1.6 million barrels per day. Meanwhile, central bank watchers face a newly-shorn, low-guidance communication regime under Fed Chair Kevin Warsh, even as hawkish commentators warn the Fed must tighten further in an exclusive **[CNBC Interview](https://www.cnbc.com/video/2026/08/11/fed-will-need-to-hike-rates-to-tackle-inflation-says-fmr-trump-special-assistant-joe-lavorgna.html)**.**

## Business & Earnings
**In corporate earnings, neocloud champion CoreWeave hijacked center stage by reporting that second-quarter revenue surged 112% to \$2.58 billion, dispelling chip-obsolescence fears by showcasing active contracts for six-year-old Nvidia A100 chips extending through 2029. In contrast, recently public chipmaker Cerebras Systems tumbled 14% in extended trading after its \$180 million core revenue missed the Street's consensus, while Cisco Systems slid 3% as its fourth-quarter gross margin of 66.3% only narrowly beat expectations. In dealmaking, Wendy's jumped 13% midday on reports that activist Nelson Peltz's Trian Fund Management is preparing a takeover bid to take the burger chain private, and Disney CEO Bob Iger partnered with Joshua Kushner to purchase a majority stake in the Los Angeles Lakers at a colossal \$12.5 billion valuation. Furthermore, retail giant Home Depot announced that CEO Ted Decker is taking a temporary medical leave, placing store operations and finance chiefs in charge ahead of next week's earnings.**

## Investing & Commentary
**Jim Cramer urged investors to load up on the AI data center trade, declaring that CoreWeave’s stellar print proves Nvidia’s graphics cards are "like fine jewelry" rather than depreciating assets. Conversely, "The Big Short" investor Michael Burry warned that the market's record-setting 182 trading sessions without an 80%-plus downside-volume day is an unprecedented technical red flag. Burry also compared Nvidia’s massive new \$500 billion financing initiative with Wall Street's largest asset managers—which was debuted in an exclusive **[CNBC Roundtable Interview](https://www.cnbc.com/video/2026/08/10/pro-watch-cnbcs-full-interview-with.html)**—to "shades of Enron," warning that unnatural credits are being structured late in the market cycle to prolong momentum.**
