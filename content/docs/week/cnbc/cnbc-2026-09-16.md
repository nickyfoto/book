# CNBC — 2026-09-16

## Lead Story
In a unanimous 12-0 decision, the Federal Reserve raised its benchmark interest rate by 25 basis points to a target range of 3.75%–4.00%, marking its first interest rate hike since July 2023 as Chairman Kevin Warsh cited stubborn inflation and Middle East supply shocks. The hawkish decision and indication of further tightening triggered a sharp late-day sell-off on Wall Street, drawing an immediate rebuke from President Donald Trump demanding rates be slashed to 1% or lower.

## Markets & Economics
Wall Street sold off following the Fed's announcement, with the Dow Jones Industrial Average dropping 631 points (1.2%), the S&P 500 falling 0.5%, and the 2-year Treasury yield spiking 7 basis points to 4.736% as the 10-year yield held near 5.0%. Economic data released earlier showed August **[Retail sales up 1.2% in August, more than expected](https://www.cnbc.com/video/2026/09/16/retail-sales-up-1-point-2-percent-in-august-more-than-expected.html)**, while headline inflation rose to 3.4% and crude oil hovered above \$107 per barrel due to Middle East supply disruptions. Record-high diesel prices of \$6.31 per gallon and surging 30-year mortgage rates of 7.22% caused homebuyer mortgage applications to plunge 19% from a year ago. Globally, central banks remained under pressure as the Bank of Japan prepared for a potential 25-basis-point rate hike and U.K. August inflation accelerated to 3.1%.

## Business & Earnings
J.B. Hunt stock plummeted nearly 13% after CFO Brad Delco warned that Q3 earnings could drop 5% to 10% due to record diesel costs, while Boeing fell over 5% as CEO Kelly Ortberg reported slower-than-expected 737 Max production stabilization. In tech infrastructure, Amazon acquired warrants to purchase up to \$340 million of Generac stock in a \$2.4 billion backup generator deal for data centers, causing Generac shares to surge over 40%. On the executive front, **[Salesforce CEO Marc Benioff on AI risks and enterprise growth](https://www.cnbc.com/video/2026/09/15/dont-want-ai-companies-to-run-into-the-same-issues-as-social-media-companies-says-salesforce-ceo.html)** highlighted \$63 billion in fiscal 2030 revenue targets at Dreamforce, while Arm Holdings CEO Rene Haas expressed increased confidence on *Mad Money* in reaching \$2 billion in data center chip revenue. Meanwhile, Intel jumped 5% following reports of preliminary talks to manufacture SK Hynix memory chips in Ohio.
