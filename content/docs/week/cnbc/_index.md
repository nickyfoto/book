# CNBC — Week of 2026-08-22 to 2026-08-28

## Story of the Week
Nvidia’s blockbuster second-quarter performance—delivering \$96.22 billion in revenue—and an unprecedented 70% revenue growth projection for fiscal 2028 decisively shattered the prevailing thesis that the artificial intelligence infrastructure buildout is peaking. CEO Jensen Huang mounted an aggressive defense of surging capital expenditures, noting that sub-one-year payback periods on major data center deployments prove enterprise demand is accelerating well beyond core hyperscalers. The blowout print ignited an explosive relief rally across enterprise software and cloud cybersecurity names, fundamentally re-energizing the broader technology sector and overpowering mounting macroeconomic headwinds.

## Markets & Economics
* **Fed Chair Warsh Signals Rate Hikes as Sticky Inflation Rattles Bonds** · [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/28/treasury-yields-jackson-hole.html): At Jackson Hole, Federal Reserve Chairman Kevin Warsh delivered an unexpectedly hawkish maiden address, discarding forward guidance and warning that stubborn price pressures—backed by July core PCE holding at 3.3% and 54% of subcomponents rising above 3%—may force interest rates higher. The remarks sent the 2-year Treasury yield surging to 4.298% and pushed implied September rate-hike odds toward 60%, capping a week where Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack called for immediate hikes while Kansas City's Jeffrey Schmid questioned whether current policy was truly restrictive.
* **U.S.-Canada Trade War Escalates with 50% Retaliatory Tariffs** · [CNBC Video](https://www.cnbc.com/video/2026/08/25/canadian-minister-leblanc-on-retaliating-against-trump-tariffs-were-not-waiting-by-the-phone.html): Bilateral trade negotiations collapsed as the U.S. imposed 50% duties on \$20 billion worth of Canadian imports, prompting Ottawa to suspend talks and enact immediate "dollar-for-dollar" retaliatory tariffs on \$20 billion of American goods. Tensions intensified further when President Donald Trump threatened a 50% tariff on Canadian vehicles and auto parts effective January 2027, with U.S. trade officials blaming Ottawa for eleventh-hour demand shifts.
* **Treasury Debt Buybacks Supercharge the "Debasement Trade"** · [CNBC Video](https://www.cnbc.com/video/2026/08/24/bessent-could-tap-near-1-trillion-treasury-general-account-to-fund-bond-buybacks-sources-said.html): Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent moved to more than double long-dated government bond buybacks—with reports indicating the Treasury may tap its near-\$1 trillion General Account to finance purchases—sparking criticism from Stanley Druckenmiller over artificial yield manipulation. The liquidity measures accelerated currency debasement trades, propelling gold to a three-month high of \$4,677.19 per ounce and driving Bitcoin up toward \$80,000.
* **Crude Oil Retreats Despite Middle Eastern Sanctions and Chokepoint Risks** · [CNBC Video](https://www.cnbc.com/video/2026/08/26/the-strait-of-hormuz-is-just-one-of-many-choke-points-being-squeezed-right-now-says-jeff-currie.html): Even as the U.S. initiated secondary sanctions and maritime blockades that drove Iranian oil exports down over 80%, Brent crude dropped from above \$94 per barrel to below \$87 as the State Department resumed Middle East diplomatic staffing and Oman coordinated independent navigation channels. The energy pullback provided equity markets with reflationary relief, while agricultural commodities surged as corn hit multi-year highs on tightening harvest projections.
* **Market Breadth Expands as Volatility Hits Year-to-Date Lows** · [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/28/stock-traders-warm-up-to-warsh-as-volatility-index-touches-year-to-date-low.html): The Cboe Volatility Index (VIX) dropped to a year-to-date low of 14.1 as the three-month S&P 500 intra-stock correlation plunged toward zero, signaling that individual corporate fundamentals are superseding macro fears. Capital rotated into equal-weight products, driving the Invesco S&P 500 Equal Weight ETF (RSP) above \$100 billion in assets for the first time as it maintained a roughly 3% year-to-date outperformance over the market-weighted index.

## Business & Earnings
* **Enterprise Software Crushes "SaaSpocalypse" Concerns**: Salesforce surged more than 20% on Q2 revenue of \$11.35 billion, buoyed by a \$2.6 billion Anthropic investment gain, \$1.5 billion in annualized Agentforce revenue, and its new "Claudeforce" launch as CEO Marc Benioff declared software obsolescence fears "nonsense". Concurrently, cybersecurity platforms CrowdStrike and Okta soared 19% and 27% respectively, reporting record top-line results driven by post-breach demand and automated agentic AI defenses.
* **Semiconductor Guidance Diverges Amid Skyrocketing Component Costs**: While Nvidia signaled price hikes exceeding 15% on Blackwell and Vera Rubin systems due to soaring DRAM costs before delivering a beat-and-raise quarter with a 2-million GPU AWS partnership, Marvell Technology plunged 8% to 10% after its \$18 billion fiscal 2028 sales guidance fell short of elevated expectations. Meanwhile, Alibaba sank over 8% after raising \$10.2 billion in a share placement to support its full-stack AI push following a 75% quarterly profit drop tied to aggressive capital spending.
* **Antitrust Pushback and Broken Deals Stall Mega-Mergers**: California Attorney General Rob Bonta abruptly canceled settlement negotiations over Paramount Skydance's \$110 billion buyout of Warner Bros. Discovery, accusing Paramount of breaching confidentiality and leaking discussions in bad faith. Deal friction expanded across financials and materials as PayPal dropped 16% after buyout talks involving Stripe and Advent stalled, and Solstice terminated its \$14.5 billion acquisition of Element Solutions.
* **Retail Displays Stark Bifurcation Across Consumer Tiers**: Dick's Sporting Goods endured its worst session on record, plunging 27% after Foot Locker integration headwinds caused Q2 revenue of \$5.59 billion to miss targets. Conversely, Gap surged 15% after beating quarterly earnings and hiring former Target executive Michael Francis as Old Navy's CEO, while Affirm gained on \$14.1 billion in gross merchandise volume despite CEO Max Levchin cautioning that \$4.09 gasoline continues to pressure disposable income.
* **Big Tech Clears Regulatory Overhangs as Physical Infrastructure Expands**: Meta Platforms settled multi-state youth addiction lawsuits for \$16.7 billion, resolving a pivotal legal threat to its advertising operations. Simultaneously, industrial-scale investments accelerated as SpaceX announced plans for a \$100 billion spaceport in Louisiana and Micron advanced a \$100 billion domestic DRAM manufacturing expansion across Idaho and New York.

## Investing & Commentary
* **AI Infrastructure Pushback Creates Moats for Cash-Rich Hyperscalers**: Jim Cramer noted that intensifying community and political opposition to data center electricity consumption in states like Pennsylvania and Texas has created severe bottlenecks for hardware suppliers like Broadcom and Corning. However, analysts argued this regulatory barrier is a distinct advantage for balance-sheet giants like Microsoft and Alphabet that already secure power, while Stephanie Link highlighted natural gas equities as an undervalued play to capture rising data center energy demand.
* **Extreme Market Concentration Triggers Rotations into Value and International Equities**: With technology and communication services commanding nearly 50% of the S&P 500, asset managers advised rotating into equal-weight index funds, cash-generative dividend ETFs like SCHD, and physical gold to hedge portfolio concentration. On CNBC's "ETF Edge," portfolio managers stressed that large-cap international equities trading at forward price-to-earnings multiples of 10 to 12 times offer superior risk-reward profiles as potential dollar softening favors overseas industrials and materials.
* **Edge Computing Challenged as the Antidote to Hyperscaler Capex Excess**: Panmure Liberum strategist Joachim Klement warned that hyperscalers are wasting tens of billions overbuilding centralized data centers, arguing that Apple and Dell will emerge as the ultimate structural beneficiaries as enterprise AI processing transitions toward localized, on-device computing.
