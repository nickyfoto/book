# CNBC — 2026-07-30

## Lead Story
A hawkish pause from the Federal Reserve sparked a massive sell-off that pushed 30-year Treasury yields to their highest levels since 2007, but blowout cloud earnings from Microsoft and Amazon helped tech stocks engineer a sharp rebound. The contrasting earnings from Big Tech highlight a growing divide in the artificial intelligence trade as investors heavily scrutinize massive capital expenditures.

## Markets & Economics
The Federal Reserve voted 9-3 to hold rates at 3.5% to 3.75%, prompting Chairman Kevin Warsh to acknowledge a "family fight" over persistent inflation. U.S. Q2 GDP slowed to an annualized 1.5% growth rate, while June core PCE inflation stubbornly held at 3.3%, reinforcing the "higher for longer" narrative. The Dow Jones Industrial Average suffered a 1,153-point plunge on Wednesday following the Fed's decision and rising geopolitical tensions. Oil markets whipsawed, with [Brent crude](https://www.cnbc.com/quotes/@LCO.1/) topping $92 a barrel after the U.S. launched a "heavy wave" of retaliatory strikes against Iranian military targets. Meanwhile, the Bank of England mirrored the Fed, holding its benchmark rate at 3.75% in a 6-3 split decision as energy supply risks keep inflation skewed to the upside.

## Business & Earnings
[Microsoft](https://www.cnbc.com/quotes/MSFT/) shares surged 15% after reporting 43% Azure cloud growth and keeping its calendar year 2026 capital expenditure expectations unchanged. [Amazon](https://www.cnbc.com/quotes/AMZN/) also soared in extended trading, driven by its AWS unit posting nearly 37% revenue growth, its fastest expansion since 2021. Conversely, [Meta](https://www.cnbc.com/quotes/META/) tanked 9% as its free cash flow plunged 91% and the company increased the lower bound of its massive AI spending forecast. [Apple](https://www.cnbc.com/quotes/AAPL/) topped Q3 estimates with a 22% jump in iPhone sales, but shares slid after incoming CEO John Ternus and Tim Cook issued weak current-quarter guidance due to a severe memory chip shortage. [Starbucks](https://www.cnbc.com/quotes/SBUX/) knocked it out of the park, popping 6% in premarket trading after CEO Brian Niccol's turnaround strategy delivered 7.9% global comparable store sales growth.

## Investing & Commentary
CNBC's Jim Cramer called the collapse and liquidation of Leopold Aschenbrenner's $24 billion AI-focused hedge fund, Situational Awareness, a "clearing event" that could signal a bottom for battered AI semiconductor stocks. After weeks of forced selling to meet margin calls, chips like [Micron](https://www.cnbc.com/quotes/MU/) and [Advanced Micro Devices](https://www.cnbc.com/quotes/AMD/) ripped higher by double digits. Ritholtz Wealth Management's Josh Brown highlighted New England utility Eversource Energy (ES) as a breakout candidate, noting that regulatory cleanups and surging electricity demand from data centers make the stock an attractive under-the-radar utility play.
