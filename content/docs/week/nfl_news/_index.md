# NFL News — Week of 2026-09-05 to 2026-09-11

## Read First
The 2026 NFL regular season opened with historic flair as the San Francisco 49ers dismantled the Los Angeles Rams 27-7 in Melbourne, Australia for the league's first regular-season game Down Under, while the defending champion Seattle Seahawks survived a 13-10 Super Bowl LX rematch against New England despite losing quarterback Sam Darnold to an opening-drive hip injury. Between megadeals for franchise pillars and a sudden wave of starting quarterback injuries across the league, Week 1 immediately proved that depth and adaptive coaching will dictate the 2026 title race.

## Week in Review
Long-form beat coverage throughout the week centered on aggressive front-office spending, capped by Tampa Bay securing Baker Mayfield to a franchise-record extension and Baltimore landing Trey Hendrickson on the richest contract ever awarded to an outside free agent in franchise history. At the same time, immediate trial-by-fire starts for rookie offensive linemen and late-week injury reports—most notably Tua Tagovailoa's oblique issue in Atlanta and Darnold's hip scare in Seattle—forced multiple coaching staffs into emergency scheme adjustments on the eve of Sunday's full slate. Off the field, league coverage reflected a historic week dominated by global outreach in Australia and European markets alongside solemn 25th-anniversary remembrances for 9/11 across multiple franchises.

### Roster Moves & Free Agency
Quarterback valuations hit new peaks heading into Week 1 as Tampa Bay locked up Baker Mayfield on a franchise-record multi-year extension through 2029, while New England made Christian Gonzalez the highest-paid defensive back in league history with a four-year deal worth up to \$135 million. Defensive line reshaping triggered massive blockbusters, led by the Baltimore Ravens signing edge rusher Trey Hendrickson to a record four-year, \$112 million deal and the Cincinnati Bengals trading the No. 10 pick to the Giants for All-Pro nose tackle Dexter Lawrence II alongside extensions for Boye Mafe and Bryan Cook. Roster maintenance continued across contenders, with the Dallas Cowboys naming newly acquired defensive tackle Quinnen Williams a captain after a massive extension, securing Brevyn Spann-Ford and Ryan Flournoy, and clearing nearly \$20 million in cap space for trade flexibility. Ancillary transactions saw New York release veteran receiver Darius Slayton, Minnesota re-sign 15th-year safety Harrison Smith, and Chicago acquire veteran center Garrett Bradbury from New England for a 2027 fifth-round pick.

### Team Beats
Quarterback availability drastically reshaped Week 1 game plans, as Atlanta ruled out Tua Tagovailoa due to an oblique injury—thrusting Cooper Rush into the starting role over Michael Penix Jr.—while Seattle lost Sam Darnold on the opening drive to a hip injury before Drew Lock rallied a 13-10 win over New England. Front seven availability hit several contenders hard, with Los Angeles Rams defensive end Aaron Donald ruled out of the Melbourne trip, New Orleans losing Cam Jordan to a hamstring injury, and San Francisco suffering a season-ending patellar tendon tear for Alfred Collins. Conversely, offensive line continuity was tested early, forcing Dallas to rely on backup T.J. Bass following Tyler Smith's thumb surgery while Baltimore named Jovaughn Gwyn starting center after Danny Pinter's season-ending injury. Play-calling evolution also dominated practice reports, highlighting Justin Herbert's adaptation to Mike McDaniel's quick-strike offense in Los Angeles and Lamar Jackson operating under center with modified footwork in Declan Doyle's new scheme.
