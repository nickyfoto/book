# Bloomberg — 2026-08-12

## Lead Story
Underlying US inflation remained subdued in July, with the core Consumer Price Index rising a modest **0.2%** on a monthly basis and **2.5%** from a year earlier, matching the slowest annual pace since March 2021. The benign reading, driven by falling monthly prices for energy, gas, and groceries, has significantly eased market pressure on the Federal Reserve to raise interest rates. Consequently, global stocks and bonds rallied as traders backed off bets of imminent near-term rate hikes despite elevated oil prices.

## Markets & Economics
* **[IEA Sees Wider Oil Supply Deficit Despite Hit to Demand From War](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-12/iea-sees-wider-oil-supply-deficit-despite-hit-to-demand-from-war)**: The International Energy Agency warned that global oil inventories will tumble this quarter at more than twice the previously estimated rate, leaving a shortfall of **1.8 million barrels a day**. Renewed hostilities and maritime disruptions in the Middle East are undermining production recovery, overshadowing a drop in demand caused by high prices.
* **[Norway \$2.3 Trillion Fund Returns 9.4% as Tech Holdings Gain](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-12/norway-2-3-trillion-fund-returns-9-4-in-first-half)**: Norway's massive sovereign wealth fund returned **9.4%** in the first half of the year, gaining approximately **\$185 billion**, primarily on the strength of its US technology stock holdings. Despite the stellar return, CEO Nicolai Tangen voiced caution about future risks, citing stretched artificial intelligence valuations, persistent inflation, and geopolitical conflicts.
* **[Yen Intervention Put China’s Yuan Under More Pressure, BE Says](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-12/yen-intervention-puts-yuan-under-even-more-appreciation-pressure)**: Coordinated efforts by US and Japanese authorities to strengthen the yen have inadvertently piled appreciation pressure on the Chinese yuan, according to Bloomberg Economics. This currency strengthening threatens to drain support for China's sluggish economy, even as domestic bond yields fall and commodity markets face persistent deflationary pressures.
* **[Jane Street Repaying \$5.5 Billion Loan in Overhaul of Debt Load](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-12/jane-street-repaying-5-5-billion-loan-in-overhaul-of-debt-load)**: Proprietary trading giant Jane Street is repaying **\$5.5 billion** of its floating-rate loans as part of a strategic restructuring of its \$11 billion total debt portfolio. This massive deleveraging move coincides with a broader credit market landscape where overall interest rates, rather than credit spreads, are driving corporate borrowing costs and corporate behavior.
