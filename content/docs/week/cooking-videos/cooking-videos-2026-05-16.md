### Food Science & Tips
When working with tough, gelatin-heavy cuts like beef feet, proper preparation is key to a clean-tasting final dish; the chef strongly recommends buying them pre-cut from the butcher, giving them a two-hour cold water soak, and executing a cold-water blanch with cooking wine before you ever start braising. Additionally, for the perfect textural finish and a restaurant-quality glaze, the cook suggests pressure cooking the meat for 45 minutes to achieve fall-off-the-bone tenderness, but then transferring it to a separate pot to reduce the sauce uncovered for 10-15 minutes.
