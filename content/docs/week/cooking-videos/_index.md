# Cooking Videos — Week of 2026-08-28 to 2026-09-04

## Watch First
The absolute triumph of the week is **[Classic Lu Cuisine: Sweet and Sour Carp](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZQVzz6toxP4)** by **鲁菜老厨陈宗明**, which showcases the spectacular, rare **'two curls and four openings' (两翘四开)** frying technique to sculpt a whole carp into an upright crescent moon silhouette. It is an essential watch for its masterful lessons on precise 45-degree wheat-ear knife cuts, a bubbly beer-aerated starch batter, and a properly balanced sugar-to-vinegar glaze.

Over on **鲁菜老厨陈宗明**, the master guides his apprentice through **[Four Happiness Meatballs](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CrzO2pL0Xfw)**, demonstrating meticulous knife-work and seasoning mechanics. ### Food Science & Tips
This week's science tips unlocked professional-grade results, starting with Master Chen's reminder that mixing beer into starch batter aerates the coating for long-lasting crispness, while thoroughly scraping a fish's inner dark mucosal membrane is the true key to neutralizing gamey odors. For pork prep, **尚食厨房 ShangshiKitchen** explains that dry-searing pork belly forces out trapped moisture to achieve a gelatinous braised skin, while Master Chen reveals that folding crumbled steamed bun into meat mixtures creates air pockets that keep meatballs incredibly soft. Finally, **老饭骨** teaches us to never cook curry in an iron wok to avoid turning the sauce black, and always blanch octopus in boiling salted water as a 'seafood lock' to prevent a rubbery texture.

### Restaurant & Travel
On the cultural and travel front, **詹姆士官方專屬頻道** explores the culinary folklore of *Nikujaga* (Japanese potato and meat stew) as a domestic benchmark, while **老饭骨** hosts an engaging cross-cultural exchange pairing French wines with fermented Chinese ingredients. Furthermore, **老饭骨** details Bohai Bay's maritime heritage, revealing that the rustic 'Cha' technique originated as a slow-stewed cold snack prepared by fishermen directly on their boats.

## Skippable
While there was no outright bad video, you can safely skip the popular culinary myth of pulling a fish's lateral sensory line to eliminate gamey odors. Additionally, avoid using bitter IPAs in your pork belly braises, as concentrated hop bitterness will quickly ruin the dish's savory balance.
