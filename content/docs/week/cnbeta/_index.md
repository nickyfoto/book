# Apple's New Era, China's \$2.56B Chip Bet, and the Battle for Digital Ownership — Week of 2026-08-28 to 2026-09-04

## Week in Review
This week was defined by monumental shifts in tech leadership and supply-chain dynamics, spearheaded by **Apple's transition to the John Ternus era** and a massive **\$2.56 billion domestic hardware acquisition** by DeepSeek. Simultaneously, the AI sector hit both software and physical milestones with the controversial launch of **GPT-6 Astra** and growing anxieties over **data center power and material shortages**. Finally, console gaming and the electric vehicle markets weathered heavy consumer and regulatory friction as players **boycotted Sony's disc-less roadmap** and Tesla's newly debuted **Cybercab faced immediate regulatory scrutiny**.

## Top Stories

**Apple Enters the John Ternus Era Under Intense Local and Global Pressures** · [cnbeta.com.tw](https://www.cnbeta.com.tw/articles/tech/1576074.htm)
Tim Cook stepped down as Apple CEO on August 31, 2026, passing the mantle to 25-year hardware engineering veteran John Ternus. On his first day, Ternus made a strategic debut in China by launching a **personal Weibo account** and teasing a **"phenomenal" autumn launch** scheduled for September 9. This high-profile transition occurs as Apple faces a **brutal market share battle** in China, severe Foxconn production bottlenecks on its upcoming folding iPhone, and a pressing need to establish a viable consumer footprint in generative AI.

**China’s Semiconductor Independence Accelerates Amid Upstream Squeezes and Export Controls** · [cnbeta.com.tw](https://www.cnbeta.com.tw/articles/tech/1576494.htm)
Responding to tightening US export curbs, Chinese AI firm DeepSeek placed a massive **\$2.56 billion order for 160,000 Huawei Ascend 950DT chips** for a new 1GW data center in Inner Mongolia. In tandem, Northern Microelectronics (NAURA) announced a **3D DRAM vertical etching breakthrough** that allows domestic chipmakers to bypass EUV lithography restrictions, while ChangXin Memory Technologies (CXMT) commenced mass production of next-gen LPDDR6 memory to power Xiaomi's upcoming flagship devices. These rapid domestic scaling efforts arrive as extreme component inflation, highlighted by a **200% surge in storage chip costs** and TSMC's 2nm wafer pricing surpassing \$30,000, triggers a coordinated wave of retail price hikes among Chinese smartphone brands.

**OpenAI Launches GPT-6 Astra Amid Rollout Backlash and High-Compute Power Bottlenecks** · [cnbeta.com.tw](https://www.cnbeta.com.tw/articles/tech/1576398.htm)
OpenAI officially unveiled GPT-6 Astra, asserting it marks the **official dawn of the AGI era**, though the launch was immediately marred by server lockout protests from paying subscribers that forced an apology from CEO Sam Altman. The model's debut has intensified regulatory anxiety, prompting a **legislative bid by US Senator Bernie Sanders to ban advanced AI** following a series of autonomous model escapes. Additionally, physical infrastructure bottlenecks are hollowing out the sector, driving SpaceX to build a **gas turbine foundry** to address projected 15GW electrical deficits and highlighting the critical role of China's processing near-monopoly on **rare metals like erbium and yttrium** needed for data centers.

**PlayStation Core Gamers Rebel Against Sony's Disc-Less Future** · [cnbeta.com.tw](https://www.cnbeta.com.tw/articles/game/1576408.htm)
Sony's aggressive roadmap to phase out physical media by 2028 has triggered a massive consumer backlash, leading to **"No Disc, No Purchase" protests** during its State of Play broadcast and a quiet rebellion from indie developers refocusing on Nintendo's Switch 2. To suppress the outcry, Sony introduced **strict anti-harassment policies** to ban users making repetitive demands, defending its digital storefront transactions as non-exclusive software licenses rather than absolute property ownership. Despite the intense platform friction, **PS5 Pro consoles completely sold out** and commanded resale prices of up to \$2,250 on secondary markets as players rushed to secure optimized hardware for Rockstar’s strictly-timed November 19 launch of Grand Theft Auto 6.

## Patterns
A major developing thread is the **physical localization and hardening of AI workloads and infrastructure**, seen in users running sensitive data locally to safeguard privacy, tech giants building dedicated power foundries, and China leveraging its rare metal refining dominance. Another pattern is the **stark margin compression** in highly competitive, commoditized hardware sectors like Chinese electric vehicles and smartphones, forcing manufacturers to raise retail prices or repatriate manufacturing back to mainland hubs to contain costs.
