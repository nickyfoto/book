# CNBeta — 2026-07-30

## Top Story
According to a **[cnbeta report on GPT-5.6 pricing](https://www.cnbeta.com.tw/articles/tech/1571028.htm)**, OpenAI has drastically slashed the API costs for its newly released GPT-5.6 Luna and Terra models. This aggressive price cut is a direct response to fierce competition from Chinese AI labs like Moonshot AI (Kimi) and Zhipu AI (Z.ai), which have been offering highly capable reasoning models at incredibly disruptive price points. The move highlights how the rapidly advancing Chinese AI ecosystem is now actively shaping global AI pricing and forcing established Western giants to adjust their strategies to remain competitive.

## Tech & AI

This recursive self-improvement has driven a 20% drop in OpenAI's end-to-end service costs and a 15% boost in token generation efficiency. Meanwhile, Apple's leadership transition is officially underway, as detailed in **[Apple's Q3 earnings call summary](https://www.cnbeta.com.tw/articles/tech/1571018.htm)** where Tim Cook made his final appearance before handing the reins to John Ternus in September. A key driver of Apple's recent 28.7% surge in Mac revenue was the **[John Ternus-led MacBook Neo](https://www.cnbeta.com.tw/articles/tech/1571050.htm)**, a $599 A18 Pro-powered laptop that is aggressively replacing Windows and Chromebooks in the education sector. In geopolitical developments, several US senators have penned a joint letter **[pressuring Apple to abandon Chinese memory chips](https://www.cnbeta.com.tw/articles/tech/1570938.htm)** from suppliers CXMT and YMTC. Lawmakers warn that incorporating these components could trigger a US Department of Defense procurement ban on Apple products, complicating the tech giant's supply chain strategy. Finally, a **[new analysis on single-person startups](https://www.cnbeta.com.tw/articles/tech/1570948.htm)** shows AI tools are dramatically lowering the barrier to entry, with many solo founders turning to Chinese open-source models to reduce overhead and relying on AI for everything from coding to customer service.

## Consumer & Devices
The wholesale price of NVIDIA's RTX 50 series has surged in Chinese retail channels, according to a **[report on GPU pricing](https://www.cnbeta.com.tw/articles/tech/1570912.htm)**. The RTX 5080 is reportedly starting at 12,000 RMB in the channel—a massive 44.6% premium over its MSRP—driven by NVIDIA's supply adjustments and memory manufacturers shifting capacity toward high-profit HBM server memory. In the EV space, scalpers are already **[selling priority delivery slots](https://www.cnbeta.com.tw/articles/tech/1570946.htm)** for Xiaomi's upcoming N70 and N90 SUV models for up to 15,000 RMB. However, Xiaomi retail staff have warned buyers that these "internal quotas" and "F-codes" are entirely fake and represent a recurring scalper scam. On the mobile front, foldables remain the "kings of depreciation," with a **[recent study showing](https://www.cnbeta.com.tw/articles/tech/1571006.htm)** they lose up to 70% of their value in the first year of ownership. These premium devices are depreciating 10% to 15% faster than standard flagship smartphones, cementing their status as luxury purchases rather than long-term investments.

## Gaming

The user is storing pirated game installation files on custom-designed USB drives housed in classic CD and DVD jewel cases to reclaim the feeling of a physical collection. Meanwhile, the highly anticipated **[Switch 2 remake of The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time](https://www.cnbeta.com.tw/articles/game/1570954.htm)** has received an "E10+" rating from the ESRB. Notably, the "suggestive themes" descriptor from the 3DS era has been removed, hinting at potential design or character alterations for the 2026 release.

## Science & Space

These "cotton candy" planets are massive but incredibly fluffy, featuring a density significantly lower than Jupiter's. Closer to home, high-resolution 3D computer modeling **[suggests that Venus is still geologically active](https://www.cnbeta.com.tw/articles/science/1570988.htm)**. Researchers found evidence that the planet's giant rift valleys are still expanding at rates of 3 to 10 centimeters per year, which is comparable to tectonic plate movements on Earth.
