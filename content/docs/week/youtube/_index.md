# YouTube — Week of 2026-08-28 to 2026-09-04

## Watch First
For the ultimate weekend watch, look no further than **Luo Yonghao’s candid 1.5-hour conversation with village welder Handy Geng** (**[手工耿×罗永浩！“村口的五金店里，出现了一个达达主义艺术家”](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=URrlmE0E75s)**). It is a deeply reflective and surprisingly serious discussion that highlights Geng's journey from a village welder to a self-made modern artist whose viral creations challenge our very definitions of **"useless" art**.

## Week in Review
This week’s content was dominated by the shifting tectonic plates of global supply chains, from the high-stakes race to **break China's rare-earth monopoly** to **Apple’s transition under John Ternus** as the AI gold rush accelerates. Interspersed with these structural crises was a profound undercurrent of civic and scientific accountability, exemplified by **high-resolution satellite exposés of national park encroachment** and **Veritasium’s public \$10,000 physics wager**. Whether analyzing global debt worries or the cultural philosophy of "useless" art, creators across English and Chinese-language channels delivered **deeply reflective commentary**.

## Highlights by Theme

### News & Business
The macroeconomic landscape felt heavy as a brutal global bond sell-off pushed **10-year yields to 4.8%**, ending the era of cheap capital. Geopolitical flashpoints simmered, from **Tibetan flood devastation** largely suppressed in Chinese state broadcasts to the economic distortions of **Russia's massive military payout policies**. Meanwhile, Chinese-language channel 小Lin说 explained how the US Treasury intervened to stabilize the plummeting yen via the **FIMA repo facility** to avoid destabilizing selloffs of US Treasuries.

### Learning & Ideas
In historical discussions, Gao Xiaosong juxtaposed Victoria and Empress Dowager Cixi to **contextualize late-Qing systemic inflation and modernization**, while Yuan Tengfei analyzed how idealistic early 20th-century Japanese socialists **pivoted toward radical militarism**. On the scientific front, Carnegie Mellon University researchers highlighted materials science breakthroughs, transitioning from accidental inventions like Silly Putty to **cutting-edge polymers that can repair their own severed connections in seconds**. Finally, Saloni Dattani's TED talk cut through medical optimism, arguing that **broken institutional incentives and market failures**—rather than scientific bottlenecks—stall the distribution of cures.

### Tech & AI
The tech space was defined by OpenAI's breathtaking release of its **autonomous GPT-6 model, Astra**, which marks a paradigm shift to agents capable of directly controlling computer interfaces. To counteract the threat of closed-source tech giants, NVIDIA executed a **\$12.93 billion acquisition of Hugging Face** to entrench the open-source software ecosystem around standard GPUs. Additionally, Apple's transition under newly designated CEO John Ternus presents immediately steep hurdles in catching up with generative AI while managing **skyrocketed component costs**.

### Everything Else
In culture and arts, The New York Times paid tribute to avant-garde artist Yayoi Kusama following her passing at age 97, honoring her life of **turning hallucinations into world-famous polka dots**. A breathtaking documentary series on oyster sauce traced the cultural obsession with umami, from **high-altitude wild mushrooms to Henan’s rich, slow-simmered mutton broths**. Additionally, a charming look at London's "mudlarks" followed individuals **searching the anaerobic Thames mud** to uncover perfectly preserved Roman shoes and medieval pins.
