# YouTube — 2026-08-11

## Watch First
For anyone fascinated by the convergence of artificial intelligence and deep science, Tudor Achim's riveting TED Talk, **[The Path to Mathematical Superintelligence | Tudor Achim | TED](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HSxytYCWVow)** · TED, is an absolute must-watch that elegantly describes how modern AI can transcend its current brute-force, hallucination-prone limitations by shifting from ambiguous human language to formal programming languages like Lean, unlocking a true collaborative partnership in mathematical discovery. Achim shows how our 4,000-year-old system of peer-reviewed mathematical proofs is collapsing under the sheer volume of new papers, and convincingly argues that AI-generated Lean proofs can bypass the human verification bottleneck entirely, transforming computers into ultimate partners for validating absolute truths.

### Learning & Ideas
Leading our intellectual exploration is the exceptional 45-year personal retrospective **[Thomas Sowell Legacy: A Conversation With Justice Clarence Thomas and Peter Robinson](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GVr5KwpeJRo)** · Hoover Institution, in which Supreme Court Justice Clarence Thomas shares deeply candid, moving memories of his childhood in Georgia and his path from student radical to finding intellectual liberation in Sowell's writings, which offered "water in the desert" by prioritizing hard evidence and self-reliance over narratives of systemic hopelessness. Expanding on this ideological framework, historian Victor Davis Hanson in **[Victor Davis Hanson on Thomas Sowell’s Tragic View of Human Nature](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KKVRlHbVRGo)** · Hoover Institution unpacks Sowell's core thesis: a tragic view of life that recognizes human nature as inherently constrained and flawed, which forces us to focus on practical, real-world trade-offs rather than chasing idealistic and ultimately disastrous expert-engineered solutions. For a practical application of this focus on cognitive capacity, TED's short lecture **[Let this be your last Short for the day #TEDTalk](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gU_7tuSo7AU)** · TED explains how constant smartphone distraction destroys our higher brain function, urging viewers to embrace structured boredom because default-mode daydreaming is actually the critical subconscious engine required to solve complex problems and foster genuine creativity.

### Tech & AI
In **[Can AI Libel You? The Robby Starbuck Case](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sRFZhDwC9D4)** · Hoover Institution, law professors Jane Bambauer and Eugene Volokh analyze the fascinating legal ramifications of *Starbucks v. Google*, a landmark Delaware state court decision that denied Google's motion to dismiss and allowed a defamation lawsuit over Gemini's hallucinations to proceed. The discussion brilliantly highlights the tension between Delaware's low "conceivability" procedural standard—which opens up massive corporate databases to expensive discovery based on an AI's fictional output—and the chilling effect this litigation might have on the development of generative AI technologies.
