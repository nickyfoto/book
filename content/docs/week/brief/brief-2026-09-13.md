# The Brief — 2026-09-13
## Top Story
A 370-year-old cipher that survived every cryptanalyst who tried it fell in 44 minutes on September 13, and the way it fell is less a story about machine intelligence than about machine patience. Claude Fable 5.1 recovered the plaintext of Sir Thomas Urquhart's Cyphral Distich — 64 numbers printed at the end of *Logopandecteision* (1653), unsolved since the 17th century, posed as an open problem in *Notes and Queries* in 1899, and listed by Klaus Schmeh among his Top 50 unsolved encrypted messages.

The answer is a royalist prayer in rhyming couplet: "O GOD UPHOLD KING CHARLS THE SECOND AND / MAKE HIM THE SUPREME RULER OF THIS LAND."

The rule, once stated, is almost insulting: the *i*-th number in a cipher line indexes a word in the *i*-th of Urquhart's 32 Proquiritations, and you take that word's first letter. Thirty-two paragraphs, 32 numbers per line, and a poem that promises the reader will find there "his own heart's wishes" while each Proquiritations entry ends with "is the desire," "wish," or "hope of." The key was never an external cipher alphabet. The key was the book.

This matters because of what previous solvers did. They ran frequency analysis, monoalphabetic and homophonic substitution — the standard toolkit — and got nothing, because they had assumed the key lived outside the text. Fable 5.1 noticed the numerical coincidence, connected it to the repeated closing formulas, and stopped looking outward. That is the whole trick.

The same method then cracked a harder target: the Cyphral Octastich in *The Jewel* (1652), 285 numbers against a book of exactly 284 numbered pages, so that number *k* indexes a word on page *k*. The result is an ottava rima prayer written in London in March 1652 — "GREAT LORD, MANTAINE THAT REGAL FAMILIE / WHEREOF KING CHARLS THE SECOND IS THE HEAD" — with a decagram reading "AMEN, SO BE IT." The solver reports 231 of 275 readable positions as exact first-occurrence hits on the page, the other 44 off by one to three words for identifiable transcription reasons: hyphenated words at page tops, ampersands, paragraph numbers, an untranscribed Greek phrase.

The caveats are stated, not buried, and they matter. Line 4 of the octastich comes out I-R-S-H where IRISH is meant, which may be a slip or a deliberate ten-syllable contraction. Letters 4–12 of line 5 decode to C-O-N-E-R-T-H-T-O — eight of nine are exact first-occurrence hits, so that is genuinely what the EEBO-TCP text yields, and it is not English; page-shift, dropped-letter, misprinted-number and dictionary-lattice hypotheses were all tried and failed. From position 159 on, every number keys to page *k*−1, suggesting one page was used twice or that a printed "5.5" is a dittography. Nine letters of the octastich remain unread, and confirming the ±1 positions requires a physical copy or the 1983 Jack & Lyall edition, since no free page images of a 1652 *Jewel* exist. The Distich solve is self-verifying in a way the Octastich is not: exactly 32 letters per line, ending *and* / *land*, consistent with the promised two-line verse.

Two things about the provenance deserve scrutiny. The result comes from Vals, which ran the elicitation, and no independent cryptanalyst has yet reproduced it — though the Distich rule is short enough that anyone with the 1834 Maitland Club edition and an afternoon can check it. And the article is candid that this was not a feat of cryptanalysis: Fable 5.1 did not out-reason the field, it out-persisted it, trying approaches that a human researcher would have abandoned for lack of payoff. The author says months of attempts with other frontier models produced no verified solve, while this one arrived at the clue "almost immediately" after surveying candidate problems — including a deliberate decision to skip Kryptos K4 as likely too hard.

That is the honest framing, and it is more useful than a benchmark number. The bottleneck on a large class of unsolved problems — historical ciphers, forgotten conjectures, archival puzzles — was never raw reasoning power. It was the willingness to spend days reading obscure material and testing ideas that might go nowhere. If a model can do that cheaply and know when to stop, the population of tractable-but-ignored problems shrinks.

The next thing to watch is not another solve but a replication: whether an independent cryptographer takes the 64 numbers and the Proquiritations and confirms the Distich by hand. If that holds, the Octastich's nine missing letters are the interesting residue — either Urquhart's error or a printing error, and only a physical 1652 quire will say which.

## Also Today

**Homebrew 7.0.0** · [Source](https://brew.sh/2026/09/13/homebrew-7.0.0/)
Homebrew 7.0.0 is the release where the package manager stops trusting the things it installs. Formula and cask setup now runs as signed structured data inside a sandbox instead of arbitrary Ruby; sandboxed builds can no longer read your home directory by default; downloads are migrating to a `fetch` phase that has network and a writable cache, after which `install` runs with networking off and those caches read-only. Eight advisories are fixed, including a High in 6.0.12 where unsigned cask-removal metadata could run commands under `sudo`. The costs are real: macOS 10.15 is dropped, Sonoma 14 demoted to Tier 3, and Intel Macs get no new bottles from 2027-09-01 with MacPorts named as the exit. Homebrew is becoming a supply-chain tool rather than a convenience script, and the people who pay for that are on old hardware.

**JetKVM Mini** · [Source](https://jetkvm.com/blog/introducing-jetkvm-mini)
JetKVM Mini puts a KVM at $39 wired, $42 wireless, $33 in a three-pack, shipping October 26. The interesting decision is architectural: an ESP32-P4X does video capture, H.264 encode, USB and firmware on one chip, with no Linux, no DRAM, no eMMC — which is how the price fell. The Mini W adds an ESP32-C5 for 2.4/5 GHz and Bluetooth setup; 4K capture comes only via the host-side OS Services release, not the device. Firmware is open source from day one, and an eFuse digest lets you lock the unit to JetKVM-signed firmware permanently. Validated against nothing but the vendor's own claims so far. A KVM you can afford per machine changes remote-hands habits more than the specs suggest.

**Revolut confirms customer data breach through fake government requests** · [Source](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/12/revolut-confirms-customer-data-breach-through-fake-government-requests/)
Revolut disclosed customer data — birth dates, addresses, phone numbers, passport and licence scans, possibly verification selfies and transaction histories — to an unauthorized party that simply emailed from a legitimate government agency domain and asked. Revolut calls the number "limited," won't say how many, won't name the agency, won't say which market; ZachXBT says the targets skew high-net-worth. The company blocked the address after the fact. There is no exploit here in any technical sense: the control that failed was a human deciding that a request looked official. That is the worst kind of finding for a firm seeking a conditional US banking licence and reportedly weighing a listing at up to $200 billion, because it cannot be patched.

**Registration without a phone number on Signal will use zero-knowledge proofs** · [Source](https://community.signalusers.org/t/registration-without-a-phone-number/2222?page=10)
Signal's long-running phone-number thread has moved from wishlist to mechanism: registration without a number will use zero-knowledge proofs, the same primitive already behind groups, usernames and donation badges. The client already assumes the server is untrusted, so ZKP fits the existing architecture rather than bolting onto it. Unresolved, and visible in the thread: whether existing accounts can unlink a number without re-registering, what remnants persist locally and server-side, and how to stop a spammer registering, unlinking and repeating for unlimited free accounts. A cooldown is the obvious answer and probably not sufficient. This is a hard trade between abuse resistance and anonymity, and Signal has not yet published the numbers that settle it.

**CUDA for AMD on Windows** · [Source](https://github.com/Speedstu/CUDA-for-AMD-Windows)
A reproducible Windows stack now runs CUDA-targeted applications on AMD GPUs via ZLUDA over ROCm/HIP, with no private DLLs: ZLUDA v6-preview.69, HIP SDK 6.4, LibTorch 2.3.0+cu118, verified by hash. cuBLAS, cuBLASLt, cuSPARSE, cuFFT and `nvcuda` all pass `cuda_check` on an RX 9060 XT (gfx1200); a 2.2M-parameter PPO network completed training, 65,536 timesteps. cuDNN is absent from the stable Windows HIP SDK, so convolution-heavy work needs a nightly stack or extra effort. A controlled A/B put upstream at 13,278 median SPS against 12,876 for a recovered custom overlay. One validated GPU is one data point, not a porting strategy.
