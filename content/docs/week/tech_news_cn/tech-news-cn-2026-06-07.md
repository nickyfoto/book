# Chinese Tech Daily — 2026-06-07

## Top Story
Dewu (Poizon) is showcasing its "AI Harness" architecture at AICon Shanghai, demonstrating how to safely deploy AI-generated code in production recommendation systems. This is a critical development as AI agents move from experimental "wild code" to highly-controlled, reliable engineering pipelines with robust safety guardrails.

## Engineering & Dev
The transition of AI from labs to production takes center stage as InfoQ highlights an upcoming AICon 2026 presentation on **[Dewu's AI Harness: From "Wild Code" to "Production by Objective"](https://www.infoq.cn/article/pOwoNlmEL9zV0aodLVB9)**. Dewu's senior technical expert, Bai Zhongwei, will detail a 7-stage guardrail system that ensures AI-written code can be safely integrated into complex recommendation architectures. The system utilizes a hybrid agent architecture consisting of "Highways" for highly deterministic tasks and "ATV" modes for dynamic problem-solving, providing a robust model for large-scale AI engineering. On the security front, researchers disclosed a severe **[FumaCRM ProductDetail.aspx SQL Injection Vulnerability](https://mrxn.net/jswz/fumacrm-Dingding-ProductNewVersion-ProductDetail-sqli.html)**. The flaw allows unauthenticated remote attackers to steal administrative credentials or inject trojans, stemming from an unfiltered `empId` parameter within the application's cookies.

## Products & Digital
For hobbyists looking to explore the skies, a fascinating guide on **[Catching Signals from the Space Station: How to Receive SSTV with a Handheld Radio](https://sspai.com/post/110108)** explains the intricacies of "Slow Scan Television". Using basic UHF/VHF handheld radios and smartphone decoding apps like Robot36 or SSTV Pad, amateur radio operators can tune into 145.800 MHz to capture and decode images broadcasted by the International Space Station. Back on Earth, an iFixit teardown featured in the **[sspai Morning Report](https://sspai.com/post/110847)** reveals the alarming interior of counterfeit Apple products from Shenzhen's Huaqiangbei. The teardown of sub-$100 knockoff Apple Watch Ultra 3, AirPods Max 2, and AirPods Pro 3 units exposed glued-in batteries, fake sensors, steel counterweights, and fire-hazard wiring, emphasizing that these devices are little more than instant e-waste.
