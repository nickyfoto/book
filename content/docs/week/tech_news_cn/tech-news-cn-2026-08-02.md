# Chinese Tech Daily — 2026-08-02

## Top Story
**[Remix 3.0 Completely Rewrites Its Architecture, Dropping React](https://www.infoq.cn/article/s8IA8KgdrizgCEsQAOXr)**
Remix, the popular full-stack web framework originally built around React, has shocked the frontend ecosystem with its 3.0 beta release by shifting away from the React runtime entirely. The new version relies on Web standard APIs and a Preact fork, signaling a massive pivot from functional programming conventions back to an imperative, web-native model. This radical rewrite positions Remix 3 as a fresh, highly opinionated bet on pure web standards over framework abstractions.

## Engineering & Dev
The frontend community is deeply divided over **[Remix's departure from React in its 3.0 rewrite](https://www.infoq.cn/article/s8IA8KgdrizgCEsQAOXr)**, a framework maintained by Shopify since 2022. Moving beyond just routing and rendering, Remix 3 is repositioned as a comprehensive full-stack ecosystem that utilizes Fetch API-based routing and Server-Driven UI through "frames," drawing immediate comparisons to HTMX. While some developers praise its alignment with Web standards and "unbundling" philosophy as ideal for AI-assisted coding, critics on Reddit and Hacker News argue the frequent breaking changes have made the framework completely unrecognizable. 

Meanwhile, on the enterprise AI front, a technical deep-dive on **[Building Enterprise Data Agents with CyberData](https://www.infoq.cn/article/5KYVt93chdBrtDsvTEcK)** at AICon Shenzhen highlights the shift from simple Copilots to autonomous Agents. Xu Xibin, Chief Architect at ShuXin Intelligent, introduces the SRW (Skill, Role, Workflow) and GPME (Goal, Policy, Memory, Eval) frameworks to solve the enterprise dilemma of "data sleep and AI suspension". This engineering paradigm enables AI to safely navigate core data development and governance loops autonomously, shifting the human role from micromanagement to setting robust risk policies and review gates.

## Products & Digital
For an analog escape, a comprehensive **[spring travelogue to Tibet](https://sspai.com/post/112843)** offers a deep dive into high-altitude travel logistics, capturing stunning vistas from Lhasa and the Potala Palace to Yamdrok Lake and the Karola Glacier. The author details the physiological challenges of the Qinghai-Tibet railway, noting that even with diffuse oxygen supply systems, altitude sickness remains a major hurdle that requires careful acclimatization. Culturally, the trip explores the deep contrasts of the region, from devout pilgrims at the Jokhang Temple to the rapid infrastructure developments driven by state-owned enterprises like the Yarlung Zangbo River Group. Shot entirely on the Panasonic LUMIX S9, the author highly praises the camera's built-in LUT features for travel photography.

## News & Commentary
The landscape of digital ownership is rapidly eroding, as highlighted in **[SSPAI's commentary on the death of physical media](https://sspai.com/post/113014)**. Drawing on preservation data from DoesItPlay, the report notes that 27% of tested physical console games cannot run without internet patches, rendering them effectively useless as digital heritage if servers ever go offline. Furthermore, Sony's CFO confirmed during a recent earnings call that despite massive community backlash, the company will "cautiously" proceed with plans to completely cease production of PlayStation physical game discs by January 2028. The piece frames this transition not merely as a commercial shift, but as a critical crisis for video game preservation.
