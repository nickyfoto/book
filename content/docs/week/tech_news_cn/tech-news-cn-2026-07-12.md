# Chinese Tech Daily — 2026-07-12

## Top Story
**[Apple sues OpenAI over trade secrets](https://sspai.com/post/112215)** — In a major escalation between the two tech giants, Apple has filed a lawsuit in California accusing OpenAI and its executives of systematically stealing commercial hardware secrets to build rival consumer devices. The suit specifically targets former Apple employees Tang Tan (a 24-year hardware veteran) and Chang Liu, alleging they downloaded confidential hardware files and funneled supplier information to OpenAI, an act Apple claims is part of a broader corporate culture of intellectual property theft. This legal battle highlights the rapid deterioration of their relationship, coming just a month after Apple announced it would use Google's Gemini for new Siri AI features.

## Engineering & Dev
In the realm of AI infrastructure and architecture, the Alibaba Qwen team is preparing to share their high-performance optimization practices for linear attention at the upcoming AICon Shenzhen 2026 conference. According to a preview in **[InfoQ's AICon Shenzhen Coverage](https://www.infoq.cn/article/wg5h3JBMvs5ZkoP0azha)**, algorithms engineer Zhangchengruidong will detail the development of FlashQLA, a TileLang-based operator library designed to drastically reduce the memory and compute overhead of the Gated Delta Network (GDN) in Qwen-3.5 models. By splitting the forward and backward passes into fused operators and utilizing dynamic sequence parallelism, FlashQLA achieves a 2-3x speedup on Hopper GPUs, solving critical bottlenecks in long-sequence and small-batch scenarios. 

Meanwhile, the latest issue of **[Transparent Daily](https://daily.xlab.app/d/20260712/)** offered a sharp economic commentary comparing the underlying business models of modern AI labs to the Hong Kong MTR transit system. The author points out that just as the MTR relies heavily on real estate development rather than ticket sales to offset the massive capital expenditures of railway construction, AI infrastructure companies are pouring billions into systems where the core service is priced near marginal cost, raising existential questions about how these generational investments will ultimately achieve profitability .

## Products & Digital
For digital productivity, consumer tech outlet ifanr published an in-depth review of **[Tencent's WorkBuddy](https://www.ifanr.com/1671739)**, framing it as a highly localized and more accessible alternative to tools like Codex or Claude Code for Chinese users. The local AI agent integrates seamlessly with WeChat, allowing users to remotely search their PC files while commuting, automate messy folder organization, and extract structured "action items" from messy meeting transcripts. Notably, WorkBuddy allows non-programmers to build custom, reusable mini-tools—like an automated essay grader that processes local text files against a rubric and outputs a neat HTML report. 

On the digital lifestyle and culture front, sspai released its **[June Indie Music Roundup](https://sspai.com/post/112035)**, highlighting 17 standout albums for audiophiles. Notable mentions include Taiwanese indie band Shallow Levée's (*浅堤*) ten-year milestone album *The Silent Masterpiece*, which explores the unspoken emotions of city life, and Acid Brain's (*酸脑*) *The Decameron*-inspired intricate instrumental rock journey. The roundup reflects a strong trend of blending modern rock and electronic sounds with traditional roots.

## News & Commentary
In current events, **[Typhoon Bavi Makes Landfall in Eastern China](https://cn.nytimes.com/china/20260713/typhoon-bavi-china/)**, bringing severe weather to Zhejiang and surrounding provinces over the weekend. The New York Times Chinese edition reports that the typhoon, with wind speeds reaching nearly 145 kilometers per hour, triggered massive evacuations of over 1.7 million people in Zhejiang and 180,000 in Fujian. The sheer scale of the storm—which temporarily spanned an area roughly the size of France—caused widespread flooding along the Nanxi River, forced the cancellation of more than 1,200 flights, and suspended public transit and schools across multiple cities before downgrading to a severe tropical storm.
