# Tech News — 2026-09-02

## Story of the Day
OpenAI has delayed the release of its next-generation AI model, Astra, after internal safety testing rated the model as a "critical" cyber risk capable of autonomously executing cyberattacks. The delayed release has also triggered alarm among security researchers because Astra reportedly hides its internal chain of thought using a technique called "recurrent depth," making it dangerously difficult for third parties to monitor or audit.

## Top Stories

**[Google Dodges Ad-Tech Breakup in Landmark Antitrust Decision](https://www.theverge.com/policy/988316/google-ad-tech-antitrust-remedies-decision)** · The Verge
US District Judge Leonie Brinkema rejected the Department of Justice's request to force Google to spin off its online advertising exchange (AdX), representing a massive symbolic victory for the tech giant. Instead of a forced breakup, the court accepted milder behavioral remedies that require Google to make its ad-tech tools interoperable with competitors, a decision that critics argue may fail to curb Big Tech's unprecedented market power. The decision marks the third consecutive defeat for federal antitrust regulators trying to break up major technology firms in court.

**[Nvidia in Advanced Talks to Buy AI Hub Hugging Face for \$14 Billion](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-02/nvidia-nears-14-billion-hugging-face-deal-this-week)** · Bloomberg
Nvidia Corp. is in advanced negotiations to acquire Hugging Face, the central open-source collaboration hub for the AI developer community, in a deal estimated at approximately \$14 billion. If completed, this transaction would stand as Nvidia’s largest AI acquisition to date, allowing the semiconductor giant to consolidate its grip over the software platforms developers use to share, fine-tune, and run AI models. The move highlights Nvidia's broader strategy to expand its market dominance beyond hardware chips and solidify its footprint in the foundational software layers of AI.

**[Hackers Leak 153 Million North American Driver's Licenses on the Dark Web](https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/09/my-drivers-license-is-one-of-153-million-for-sale-on-a-new-dark-website/)** · Ars Technica
A dark-web identity theft marketplace called Nexus listed high-resolution scans of more than 153 million US and Canadian driver's licenses before abruptly going offline. Security researchers traced the breach back to Louisiana-based idscan.net, which had its systems silently exfiltrated for over a year, exposing IDs scanned at Hertz rental counters, cannabis dispensaries, and other major commercial hubs. Crucially, the leak contains advanced infrared and ultraviolet scans that could allow counterfeit physical IDs to pass hologram security tests, triggering an active FBI inquiry into the breach.

**[Uber Cuts 10% of Global Staff While Launching Robotaxis in London](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/02/uber-is-laying-off-10-of-staff-or-3300-people/)** · TechCrunch
Uber Technologies has announced a major corporate overhaul that will lay off 3,300 employees—amounting to 10% of its global workforce—while cutting management layers, eliminating remote positions, and merging its core engineering, science, and delivery divisions. Simultaneously, the company beat Waymo to become the first to launch a commercial robotaxi service in London, partnering with British autonomous driving startup Wayve to deploy self-driving vehicles on the city's complex roads. CEO Dara Khosrowshahi is aggressively prioritizing operational efficiency to funnel savings back into Uber's core ridesharing, delivery, and self-driving ventures.

**[Voters Recall Missouri Official as Data Center Backlash Intensifies](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/09/01/technology/data-center-recall-vote-independence.html)** · The New York Times
Bipartisan public outrage over the rapid buildout of generative AI infrastructure has escalated into a central political issue, culminating in Missouri residents overwhelmingly voting to recall a city official who approved massive data center tax breaks. The political fallout matches a growing crisis in Loudoun County, Virginia—home to the world's densest concentration of servers—where residents are actively protesting noise pollution from natural gas turbines and 185-foot transmission lines slicing through residential backyards. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent publicly rebuked major tech companies for failing to engage with local communities as grid strain and rising cost-of-living concerns fuel widespread resistance.

**[1Password Embroiled in Customer Outrage Over \$300,000 Linux Donation](https://www.theverge.com/tech/988536/1password-dhh-linux-controversy)** · The Verge
Password manager 1Password is facing severe customer backlash and internal employee dissent after pledging \$300,000 to the Omacom Foundation, which oversees David Heinemeier Hansson’s (DHH) Omarchy Linux distribution. The controversy centers on DHH's history of sharing overtly racist and anti-immigrant blog posts, which led critics to accuse 1Password of indirectly funding white nationalist rhetoric. 1Password CEO David Faugno defended the decision as a purely practical move to support their second-most popular Linux user base, insisting that the donation goes to the Omacom nonprofit rather than DHH himself.
