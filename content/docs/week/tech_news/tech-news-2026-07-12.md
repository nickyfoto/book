# Tech News — 2026-07-12

## Story of the Day
SK Hynix has issued a grim warning that 2027 will be the memory industry's "worst-ever" year for supply shortages, driven almost entirely by the relentless demand for AI hardware. The crisis means commodity memory is taking a backseat to premium HBM chips, which will inevitably drive up prices across consumer PCs, smartphones, and consoles through 2030.

**[AI-driven Datacenter Builds Increased Microsoft's Emissions 25% In One Year](https://slashdot.org/story/26/07/11/1944220/ai-driven-datacenter-builds-increased-microsofts-emissions-25-in-one-year)** · Slashdot

**[Meta Removes Controversial AI Feature On Instagram After Backlash](https://tech.slashdot.org/story/26/07/12/0150219/meta-removes-controversial-ai-feature-on-instagram-after-backlash)** · Slashdot

**[Apple’s failed self-driving car program left a legacy of powerful AI chips](https://www.theverge.com/tech/964519/apple-silicon-self-driving-car-ai-m7-ultra)** · The Verge
While Apple's self-driving car program was famously scrapped, the immense on-device processing power it required led directly to the creation of the Neural Engine. This foundational work now serves as the backbone for Apple's on-device AI and directly influenced the development of its powerful new M-series chips.

**[Facial Recognition in UK Shops Will Soon Instantly Alert Police About Offenders](https://news.slashdot.org/story/26/07/12/0259226/facial-recognition-in-uk-shops-will-soon-instantly-alert-police-about-offenders)** · Slashdot
Over 100 UK businesses are deploying "Facewatch," an AI surveillance system that will now alert police in real-time when suspected repeat offenders enter a retail store. Civil liberties groups are sounding the alarm over this opaque escalation of retail surveillance, pointing to numerous instances of innocent shoppers being falsely flagged and ejected.

**[Linus Torvalds on AI, Junk Patches, Humans, and Godzilla](https://linux.slashdot.org/story/26/07/12/2053201/linus-torvalds-on-ai-junk-patches-humans-and-godzilla)** · Slashdot
The creator of Linux hasn't bought into the AI coding hype, noting that LLMs have generated far more "junk" patches than useful code for kernel-level development. Torvalds warned that AI often spits out "mindless band-aid" fixes that obscure deeper issues, maintaining that human oversight remains strictly necessary for core infrastructure.
