# Tech News — 2026-09-06

## Story of the Day
German aerospace startup Isar Aerospace successfully launched its "Spectrum" rocket into low Earth orbit from Andøya Spaceport in northern Norway, marking Europe's first-ever entirely commercial orbital rocket launch. This critical milestone establishes a sovereign launch alternative for commercial and institutional customers, providing a crucial escape route from the global satellite launch bottleneck while strengthening European security.

**[How a Blacklisted Chinese Tech Giant Kept Buying America’s Best A.I. Chips](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/09/06/technology/ai-chips-china-blacklist.html)** · [The New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/09/06/technology/ai-chips-china-blacklist.html)
Despite being placed on a U.S. blacklist due to its collaborations with the Chinese military, technology giant Inspur has successfully bypassed federal sanctions to purchase Nvidia's most advanced AI chips. The firm utilized an unsanctioned subsidiary to procure and funnel the high-performance hardware directly to China's leading AI companies. This loophole underscores the immense difficulty of enforcing export controls in deeply integrated global tech supply chains.

**[Seattle Times and Newsday sue OpenAI and Microsoft for infringement](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/990932/seattle-times-newsday-lawsuit-openai-microsoft)** · [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/990932/seattle-times-newsday-lawsuit-openai-microsoft)
The Seattle Times and Newsday have filed a joint lawsuit against OpenAI and Microsoft, alleging that the companies used their copyrighted journalism without permission to train generative AI models. The plaintiffs argue that OpenAI's chatbots frequently reproduce entire passages of their proprietary reporting, and they named Microsoft as a co-defendant because its Copilot assistant relies directly on OpenAI's infrastructure. The publishers now join a wave of nearly 400 local newspapers seeking legal remedies for copyright infringement.

**[Flock Offered Webinar Teaching Cops How to Surveil 'No Kings’ Protesters](https://yro.slashdot.org/story/26/09/05/0659243/flock-offered-webinar-teaching-cops-how-to-surveil-no-kings-protesters)** · [Slashdot](https://yro.slashdot.org/story/26/09/05/0659243/flock-offered-webinar-teaching-cops-how-to-surveil-no-kings-protesters)
A webinar hosted by Flock Safety revealed that the company has been teaching law enforcement agencies how to use its FlockOS software platform to surveil political protests and other routine public gatherings. FlockOS aggregates automatic license plate readers, drones, gunshot detectors, and third-party cameras into a single interface, which police have used to monitor First Amendment-protected activity. This training directly contradicts Flock's public positioning of its hardware as noninvasive, static tools reserved strictly for solving severe crimes.

**[FreeCORE: TrueNAS Fork Maintaining Deeply Integrated Virtualization, Jails, and OpenZFS on FreeBSD](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/09/freecore-truenas-fork)** · [InfoQ](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/09/freecore-truenas-fork)
Following iXsystems' decision to deprecate the FreeBSD-based TrueNAS CORE in favor of the Debian Linux-based TrueNAS SCALE, community developers have launched "FreeCORE" to preserve the legacy architecture. The community-driven fork upgrades the platform to FreeBSD 15.0 and restores deeply integrated virtualization technologies like FreeBSD Jails, which engineers argue offer vastly superior kernel-level security isolation compared to Linux containers. However, long-term sustainability is a core concern, as the project is currently managed by a single developer heavily leveraging AI coding agents.

**[How US Campaigns Are Already Using AI to Try to Sway Voters](https://politics.slashdot.org/story/26/09/05/223250/how-us-campaigns-are-already-using-ai-to-try-to-sway-voters)** · [Slashdot](https://politics.slashdot.org/story/26/09/05/223250/how-us-campaigns-are-already-using-ai-to-try-to-sway-voters)
U.S. campaign finance disclosures reveal that at least 39 congressional candidates and 30 political action committees have paid for OpenAI subscriptions to quietly draft voter outreach emails, ad copy, and campaign scripts. This widespread adoption violates OpenAI's safety guidelines, which prohibit candidates from using its tools to generate advertising materials or target specific demographics. Despite these official policies, testing shows that OpenAI's enforcement is highly inconsistent, frequently complying with banned political prompts.

**[UN Votes To Encourage Map Projections More Accurately Reflecting the True Size of Continents](https://yro.slashdot.org/story/26/09/06/0017242/un-votes-to-encourage-map-projections-more-accurately-reflecting-the-true-size-of-continents)** · [Slashdot](https://yro.slashdot.org/story/26/09/06/0017242/un-votes-to-encourage-map-projections-more-accurately-reflecting-the-true-size-of-continents)
The United Nations General Assembly voted 164 to 1 to endorse a resolution urging governments, schools, and tech platforms to transition away from the 16th-century Mercator map projection. The African-led campaign argued that the Mercator projection's severe polar distortion—which dramatically minimizes equatorial landmasses like Africa while magnifying northern landmasses—perpetuates a historically skewed worldview. The United States cast the sole opposing vote, dismissing the cartographic modernization initiative as a "radical ideological project".
