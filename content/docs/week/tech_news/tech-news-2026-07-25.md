## Top Stories

**[SpaceX's Starship Nails Flight 13 Splashdown](https://arstechnica.com/space/2026/07/spacex-eyes-tower-catch-for-next-starship-after-auspicious-end-to-13th-flight/)** · Ars Technica
SpaceX successfully executed an intact splashdown of its Starship megarocket in the Indian Ocean during its 13th test flight. The spacecraft's heat shield survived the extreme temperatures of reentry, providing crucial data for the company's reusable rocket program. Elon Musk indicated that the next test flight will attempt to catch the returning ship using mechanical arms on the launch tower.

**[AI Data Centers Drive Up Consumer Hardware Prices](https://www.theverge.com/tech/971041/google-confirms-pixel-11-price-hike)** · The Verge
The explosion of AI data centers has created a severe RAM supply shortage, forcing major consumer tech companies to hike their hardware prices. Google essentially confirmed the upcoming Pixel 11 will cost more, joining a wave of similar price increases from Apple, Nintendo, Microsoft, and Roku. Meanwhile, Qualcomm is also preparing to raise its processor prices before the end of the summer.

**[Anthropic Moves Toward Custom Silicon](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-25/sk-chair-says-anthropic-asked-for-supplies-to-make-its-own-chips)** · Bloomberg
Anthropic has reportedly approached memory chip giant SK Hynix for supplies to manufacture its own custom semiconductors. The move signals the AI developer's strategic intent to reduce its reliance on Nvidia and take greater control over the hardware infrastructure powering its frontier models.

**[China Slaps Trip.com With $765 Million Antitrust Fine](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-25/china-fines-trip-com-765-million-for-abusing-market-dominance)** · Bloomberg
Chinese regulators have fined the country's largest travel booking platform, Trip.com, 5.18 billion yuan ($765 million) following a months-long antitrust investigation. The government concluded that the company abused its market dominance, marking another major regulatory strike in China's ongoing oversight of its massive domestic tech sector.

**[Amazon Mandates Labels for AI-Generated Images](https://slashdot.org/story/26/07/25/0545246/amazon-cracks-down-on-use-of-ai-images-by-sellers)** · Slashdot
Amazon will now require third-party sellers to flag product images and videos containing photorealistic AI-generated people, placing a visible indicator on those listings for shoppers. The policy shift is a direct response to a new New York law mandating transparency around synthetic performers in advertising. This is highly impactful, given that outside sellers currently account for over 60% of all goods sold on the retail platform.

**[Instagram Bans Harassment Using Meta Smart Glasses](https://tech.slashdot.org/story/26/07/24/2212248/instagram-is-now-banning-pickup-artists-pranksters-who-use-meta-glasses)** · Slashdot
Instagram is actively removing videos filmed with Meta's smart glasses that feature the harassment of service workers and women in public. According to Instagram head Adam Mosseri, the platform will not tolerate content that surreptitiously records and takes advantage of people for engagement. Several large accounts belonging to "pickup artists" with millions of followers have already been deactivated under the new policy.

**[Trump Threatens Tariffs Over EU's Google Fine](https://yro.slashdot.org/story/26/07/24/2111219/trump-threatens-new-tariffs-against-eu-over-google-fine)** · Slashdot
Following Brussels' decision to fine Google over $1 billion for alleged illegal trade practices, President Trump threatened a "substantial" new tariff against the European Union. The retaliation could jeopardize an existing trade deal that capped US tariffs on EU exports at 15 percent, with the President warning that the US is not Europe's "piggybank".

**[Warner Bros. Sues Amazon Over Poaching Spree](https://www.theverge.com/business/971011/warner-bros-suing-amazon-poaching-employees)** · The Verge
Warner Bros. Discovery has filed a lawsuit accusing Amazon of illegally inducing its executives to breach their employment contracts. The complaint claims Amazon engaged in a "lawless employee shopping spree," targeting key figures like Pia Barlow, the former senior VP for originals marketing, as well as HBO's head of drama series. The case could have significant implications for the enforceability of term employment agreements under California law.
