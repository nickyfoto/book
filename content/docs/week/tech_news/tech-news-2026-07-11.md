# Tech News — 2026-07-11

Apple has filed a major lawsuit against OpenAI, accusing the AI giant of systematically poaching employees to steal trade secrets for its nascent hardware division. The suit claims former Apple engineers brought proprietary technical documents and unreleased device prototypes to OpenAI, exposing deep fault lines in the escalating battle for AI and hardware dominance.

## Top Stories

**[Flock Safety's AI Surveillance Nightmare Leads to Wrongful Arrest](https://yro.slashdot.org/story/26/07/11/0556236/how-flock-cameras-wrongly-tracked-a-journalist-for-days-then-sent-police-to-arrest-him)** · Slashdot
A minor typo in a police report entered into Flock Safety's nationwide AI camera network resulted in a journalist being wrongfully identified as a car thief and surrounded by armed police in Minnesota. Flock's system failed to register non-standard plate characters, highlighting the profound dangers of automated surveillance systems when edge cases trigger aggressive police action. Separately, the controversial company is facing criticism for sending a cease-and-desist letter to a lecture series that hosted a debate regarding its technology.

**[Chinese AI Firms Caught "Distilling" Anthropic's Claude](https://slashdot.org/story/26/07/11/040239/chinas-ai-companies-may-be-distilling-americas-ai-models)** · Slashdot
Anthropic uncovered a campaign in which Chinese tech giants, including Alibaba, used tens of thousands of fraudulent accounts to funnel millions of queries through Claude to improve their own models. The technique, known as "distillation," allows smaller AI systems to cheaply learn from advanced ones, significantly accelerating China's AI catch-up. However, Anthropic faced severe backlash after it deployed tracking software to spy on users in order to catch the offenders, eventually forcing the company to remove the surveillance code.

**[FCC Greenlights Controversial Space Mirror Satellite](https://science.slashdot.org/story/26/07/10/2230254/fcc-approves-reflect-orbitals-space-mirror-satellite-that-astronomers-hate)** · Slashdot
The FCC has approved Reflect Orbital's Earendil-1, a demonstration satellite featuring a 60-by-60-foot mirror designed to beam sunlight back to Earth after dark. Despite receiving nearly 2,000 public complaints from astronomers and environmental groups worried about extreme light pollution, the FCC ruled that environmental harms are unlikely from a single test. The approval paves the way for Reflect Orbital's ultimate vision of deploying 50,000 space mirrors by 2035 to power solar farms at night.

**[Meta Faces Potential $1.4 Trillion Penalty in Youth Safety Lawsuit](https://tech.slashdot.org/story/26/07/11/0614250/meta-says-us-states-seek-14-trillion-in-penalties-in-augusts-youth-safety-trial)** · Slashdot
In a new court filing, Meta revealed that four US states are seeking an unprecedented $1.4 trillion in penalties over allegations the company intentionally designed Facebook and Instagram to addict children. Meta insists the demands are "outlandish" and lack legal basis, noting the requested sanction nearly eclipses the company's entire market capitalization. The high-stakes trial, brought by California, Colorado, Kentucky, and New Jersey, is slated to begin in August.

**[OpenAI's Head of Safety Exits Amid Reorganization](https://www.wired.com/story/openai-head-of-safety-leaving/)** · Wired
Johannes Heidecke, OpenAI's head of safety, is departing the company as part of an ongoing internal restructuring. The exit comes as the AI giant attempts to formally merge its research and safety teams, with a new executive expected to oversee both divisions moving forward.

**[Meta Pulls Instagram AI Deepfake Feature After Immediate Backlash](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/07/10/technology/meta-muse-images-instagram-removal.html)** · The New York Times
Meta has officially deactivated "Muse Image," a controversial capability that allowed users to generate AI deepfakes of any public Instagram account simply by tagging them. The rapid reversal follows intense pushback from users, creators, and Hollywood agencies over glaring privacy and copyright violations.
