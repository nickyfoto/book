# Tech News — Week of 2026-06-13 to 2026-06-19

## Story of the Week
In an unprecedented escalation of government intervention, the US forced Anthropic to pull the plug on its new Fable 5 and Mythos 5 frontier AI models worldwide over cybersecurity fears. Reportedly spurred by Amazon CEO Andy Jassy directly alerting the White House, the chaotic export control directive paralyzed one of Silicon Valley's top labs and set a chilling new precedent for sovereign AI regulation.

## Top Stories
**SpaceX Ascends to the Public Markets and Buys Cursor** · TechCrunch / Bloomberg
SpaceX successfully executed a record-breaking $85.7 billion IPO, instantly propelling its market capitalization to $2.7 trillion and eclipsing Amazon. Flush with capital, Elon Musk immediately flexed the company's financial muscle by dropping $60 billion to acquire AI coding startup Cursor, strategically signaling an ambition to close the developer capability gap with frontier AI rivals.

**Traditional Media Strikes Back: Fox Buys Roku** · The Verge
Fox Corporation is swallowing streaming hardware giant Roku whole in a massive $22 billion acquisition, abruptly shifting the media landscape by combining its Tubi platform and traditional networks with 100 million streaming households. The aggressive play proves that the future of streaming looks remarkably like legacy cable television, as Fox aggressively prioritizes raw distribution leverage and advertising dominance over traditional content licensing.

**Local Resistance and the Data Center Reckoning** · Slashdot / The New York Times
The physical toll of the AI boom is triggering massive grassroots backlash, with community opposition derailing 75 data center projects worth $130 billion in the first quarter alone. The tension between hyperscale infrastructure and the environment became painfully explicit when the DOJ formally intervened to defend xAI's unpermitted, polluting gas turbines in Memphis strictly on national security grounds.

**Coordinated State Action Targets OpenAI** · Bloomberg / The New York Times
A powerful coalition of state attorneys general has launched a sweeping subpoena and investigation into OpenAI, heavily scrutinizing the generative AI giant’s handling of consumer data, health information, and interactions with minors. This probe follows recent legal action from Florida and underscores a rapid escalation in coordinated, state-level legal scrutiny targeting the business practices behind the AI boom.

**Global Governments Move to Shield Children from Tech** · The Verge / Bloomberg
The UK announced an aggressive, total ban on social media and companion AI chatbots for children under 16, mirroring similar legislation proposed in Australia. Concurrently, Norway restricted generative AI tools in elementary schools to protect foundational learning, highlighting a growing international consensus to forcibly regulate Big Tech's access to young, vulnerable audiences.
