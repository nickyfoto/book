# Tech News — Week of 2026-08-08 to 2026-08-14

## Top Stories

**Wall Street Mobilizes \$500 Billion for Nvidia-Backed AI Infrastructure** · [New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/08/10/business/ai-nvidia-lenders-500-billion.html)
Six major investment firms, including BlackRock, Blackstone, and Goldman Sachs, have partnered with Nvidia to launch a monumental \$500 billion financing platform to fund AI data centers. Under this alliance, Nvidia-based "AI factories" will be treated as repeatable, productive infrastructure assets rather than risky tech ventures, with Nvidia putting up up to 25% of its own capital on the line to backstop residual-value risk. This massive financial mobilization represents a critical macroeconomic shift as the physical reality of building data centers and power grids evolves into an unprecedented Wall Street asset class.

**SpaceX Finalizes \$60 Billion Acquisition of AI Coding Sensation Cursor** · [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-14/spacex-completes-its-60-billion-cursor-acquisition)
SpaceX has completed its landmark \$60 billion acquisition of Cursor, the red-hot AI developer tool, in one of the largest technology acquisitions in history. The massive buyout serves as a core pillar of Elon Musk's aggressive push to rival OpenAI and Anthropic in generative AI by securing a premier developer-centric asset. Integrating Cursor's localized AI capabilities directly into SpaceX's operations underscores how mission-critical software automation has become to the aerospace giant's grander engineering and operational ambitions.

**Judge Orders Google to Eliminate Play Store Friction Within One Week** · [Slashdot](https://tech.slashdot.org/story/26/08/14/1733213/judge-orders-google-to-make-rival-app-store-installs-easier)
Following Google's landmark antitrust defeat by Epic Games, Android users in the US can now download Aptoide, a games-focused storefront, directly from Google Play. However, US District Judge James Donato has ordered Google to immediately strip away "anticompetitive friction" from these rival storefront installations. The judge's rapid seven-day deadline came after Epic demonstrated that Google's multi-step, warning-laden installation process was intentionally designed to discourage consumers, signaling deep judicial impatience with Google's stalling tactics.

**US AI Giants Slit API Pricing to Battle Chinese Rivals** · [Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/ai/2026/08/openai-and-anthropic-in-price-war-as-chinese-ai-rivals-gain-ground/)
Facing rising enterprise bills and intensifying competition from cheap, highly capable Chinese open-weight models like DeepSeek, Moonshot, and Z.ai, US AI leaders have launched an aggressive price war. OpenAI slashed API pricing for its GPT-5.6 Luna model by a staggering 80%, while Anthropic introduced Claude Opus 5 at half the price of its predecessor, and Google offered a temporary 50% discount on its rapid-fire Gemini 3.7 Flash release. This race to the bottom highlights how rapidly frontier models are being commoditized as labs prioritize developer lock-in over profit margins.

**Meta and TikTok Denied Section 230 Immunity in \$1.4 Trillion Youth Addiction Lawsuit** · [Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2026/08/meta-cant-stop-states-1-4-trillion-lawsuit-from-going-to-trial/)
The US Court of Appeals for the 9th Circuit issued a landmark 3-0 ruling rejecting claims from Meta and TikTok that they are legally immune to state-led youth addiction lawsuits under Section 230. The decision clears the way for a massive trial brought by a bipartisan coalition of state attorneys general seeking an astronomical \$1.4 trillion in damages. Scheduled to start immediately following jury selection on August 19, the trial will determine if the platforms' underlying recommendation algorithms were intentionally designed to hook minor users.

**The Backlash Against Flock Safety’s Surveillance Grid Reaches a Boiling Point** · [The New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/08/10/us/flock-cameras-can-track-every-car-in-america-police-love-them-citizens-dont.html)
Automated license plate reader developer Flock Safety faced severe backlash this week after multiple police officers were arrested for using the system to stalk partners, and audits revealed error rates of 32.3% in Los Angeles and 71% in Ohio. Tensions escalated further following Littleton, Massachusetts canceling its contract after discovering Flock secretly reactivated disabled public road cameras, alongside leaked documents revealing partnerships to turn rideshare drivers into roaming surveillance units. In response, Flock mandated criminal case numbers for all law enforcement searches and slashed its standard video retention window from 30 days to seven, though civil rights advocates warn these guardrails are easily bypassed.

The piloted flight drew over 1 MW of power but consumed a mere \$5 in electricity, demonstrating highly promising operating economics.
*   **Connecticut Court Catches Prompt Injection**: A Connecticut judge sanctioned a self-represented plaintiff after discovering hidden instructions written in a tiny, 3-point white font inside official court filings. The hidden text instructed any reviewing AI system to ignore previous denials and rule in his favor.
*   **npm 12 Disables Lifecycle Scripts**: In a major security overhaul, GitHub's newly released npm 12 package manager has disabled automatic preinstall, install, and postinstall scripts by default. These automatic scripts accounted for 53% of malicious npm supply chain attacks over the past year.
*   **Nvidia AI Hardware Inside Russian Missile**: Ukrainian military intelligence discovered an Nvidia Jetson Orin computer module inside Russia's newly deployed S-71 Monochrome air-launched cruise missile, suggesting the integration of automated AI guidance.
