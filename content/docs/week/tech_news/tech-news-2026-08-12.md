## Top Stories

**[First End-to-End Autonomous AI Cyberattack Hits Taiwan](https://it.slashdot.org/story/26/08/12/1544250/china-linked-hackers-used-ai-to-run-first-ever-autonomous-cyberattack-on-taiwan)** · Slashdot
**Suspected Chinese state-linked hackers deployed a multi-agent system built on open-source frameworks Hermes and OpenClaw to conduct a highly adaptive, four-day intrusion against Taiwanese systems, compromising 85 accounts and stealing over 2,500 personnel records.** Rather than following static scripts, the agents dynamically mapped networks, prioritized attack vectors, and searched the web to build alternative exploits when blocked. **This marks the first observed end-to-end autonomous cyberattack against a government, raising alarm that highly sophisticated offensive tools can now be assembled entirely from free, off-the-shelf developer libraries.**

**[Model Context Protocol Goes Stateless with Centralized Auth](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/08/mcp-stateless-gateway/)** · InfoQ
**The Model Context Protocol (MCP) has released a major specification update that deprecates stateful sessions in favor of stateless JSON-RPC over HTTP, introducing mandatory `Mcp-Method` and `Mcp-Name` headers to enable standard web infrastructure to route and throttle agent traffic.** Alongside statelessness, the ecosystem is addressing security headaches with the arrival of a centralized authentication plugin, correcting a glaring vulnerability where agents previously ran with the full permissions of the user who initialized them. **This double-whammy of scaling and governance maturity signals MCP's transition from early-stage experimentation into a robust enterprise-grade API standard as monthly SDK downloads surge past 400 million.**

**[Scientists Create First Female Clones of Male Mice Using CRISPR](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/08/12/1141768/scientists-just-created-female-clones-of-male-mice/)** · MIT Technology Review
**Reproductive biologists in Japan have successfully eliminated the Y chromosome from male mouse embryos using a CRISPR-based method called Y-CUT, resulting in healthy and fertile female pups with an XO chromosomal profile.** By scaling this technique to cryopreserved male cells, the team demonstrated they could create female clones of adult male mice, which subsequently mated with normal males to produce healthy offspring. **This biological breakthrough challenges the paradigm that both sexes are required for mammalian reproduction, offering a powerful new "frozen zoo" tool to rescue endangered species where only a few male survivors remain.**

**[LiteLLM Supply Chain Leak Exposes Terabytes of Enterprise Secrets](https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/08/terabytes-of-credentials-leaked-in-massive-supply-chain-attack/)** · Ars Technica
**Security firms CloudSEK and Hudson Rock revealed a massive supply chain breach of LiteLLM, a popular open-source tool used to simplify multi-model AI software development.** Attackers compromised the package's official PyPI repository during a 40-minute window in March, allowing them to harvest a 195-terabyte cache of SSH keys, cloud access tokens, and Kubernetes secrets from over 2,500 organizations. **The affected entities include industry heavyweights Microsoft, Amazon, Cisco, and Salesforce, illustrating the profound risks of integrating third-party AI orchestration middleware without strict credential auditing.**

**[Neurotech Frontier Prompts Calls for 'Neurorights' to Protect Thoughts](https://www.wired.com/story/book-excerpt-the-vanishing-earth-james-crawford-brain-mining/)** · Wired
**Non-invasive wearable brain scanners are rapidly transitioning from academic research labs into consumer products developed by tech giants like Apple, Meta, and Snap.** Thanks to major leaps in machine learning, researchers can now decode fMRI scans to reconstruct watched video clips, map complex emotions, and even translate a subject's thoughts into coherent text after training on spoken-word podcasts. **Fearing that mental privacy will become the next corporate advertising battleground, neuroscientists are calling for legal frameworks establishing "neurorights" to protect cognitive agency and identity.**
