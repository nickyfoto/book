## Top Stories

**Anthropic’s landmark $1.5B copyright settlement is approved** · [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/20/anthropics-landmark-1-5b-copyright-settlement-is-approved/)
A federal judge has signed off on Anthropic's historic $1.5 billion class-action settlement with authors whose copyrighted books were pirated from shadow libraries to train its Claude AI models. While the agreement delivers roughly $3,000 per book to affected authors, it neatly skirts the larger legal question of whether training AI on copyrighted material inherently constitutes infringement. The payout ranks as the largest copyright recovery in history, though many creators remain concerned about the precedent it sets.

**America needs to stop getting shocked by Chinese AI** · [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/968136/chinese-ai-models-another-sputnik-moment)
Chinese startup Moonshot's release of the open-source Kimi K3 model has shocked US markets and sparked panic in Washington, proving Beijing can match cutting-edge systems from OpenAI and Anthropic. The model's free availability threatens to undercut Silicon Valley's massive data center investments, prompting the Trump administration to weigh potential sanctions over alleged IP theft. It is a stark wake-up call that China's aggressive bet on open-source AI is rapidly eroding American dominance.

**Google releases three new Gemini models — but no 3.5 Pro** · [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/21/google-releases-three-new-gemini-models-but-no-3-5-pro/)
Google rolled out Gemini 3.6 Flash alongside a new security-focused model dubbed 3.5 Flash Cyber, explicitly deprecating its recently launched 3.5 Flash system in response to developer complaints over code generation. Flash Cyber is built to integrate with Google's CodeMender agent for low-cost, high-speed vulnerability patching. However, the continued absence of the delayed flagship Gemini 3.5 Pro model is raising pointed questions about the company's deployment timeline.

**Apple’s rumored ‘Upgrade’ program brings lease-to-own pricing for iPhones, Macs, and iPads** · [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/tech/968750/apple-upgrade-program)
Apple is partnering with Klarna to launch "Apple Upgrade," a fundamental shift in how the hardware giant sells its premium devices. Operating like a car lease with a soft credit check, the program will allow customers to rent iPhones, iPads, Macs, and Apple Watches with options to return, keep, or upgrade early. Launching July 28, the financial pivot aims to spur sluggish hardware sales as component shortages drive retail prices higher.

**France Passes Bill to Ban Social Media for Children Under 15** · [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-20/french-lawmakers-clear-way-for-social-media-age-limit-afp-says)
France has become the first major European country to pass legislation completely banning social media access for children under 15, alongside a total ban on mobile phones in schools. The aggressive legislative move sets a new benchmark in the global pushback against youth social media addiction. It is expected to severely strain relations with US technology giants and the Trump administration.

**Judge Pauses Paramount-Warner Bros Merger** · [Slashdot](https://yro.slashdot.org/story/26/07/21/069239/judge-pauses-paramount-warner-bros-merger)
A federal judge slapped a 14-day restraining order on the $111 billion megamerger between Paramount and Warner Bros. Discovery following an antitrust lawsuit led by a 12-state coalition. The delay pauses operations while the court evaluates a preliminary injunction that could permanently kill the deal, setting up a massive battle over market consolidation in the film and television industry.
