# Tech News — 2026-08-08

## Story of the Day
To fuel the ongoing AI arms race, tech giants Microsoft, Google, Amazon, Meta, and Oracle are projected to bleed a combined \$125 billion in negative free cash flow next year as infrastructure and development costs balloon. This massive capital expenditure has already pushed Google and Amazon to an ignominious milestone, with both losing more cash over the past quarter than any other major U.S. companies.

## Top Stories

**[Amazon’s New Texas Data Center Fueling One of the Nation's Most Polluting Power Plants](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/977124/amazon-data-center-worst-polluting-power-plant)** · The Verge
Amazon is investing heavily in a massive 7.65-gigawatt on-site gas-burning power plant to power its new data center in Pecos County, Texas. Because the facility will not initially connect to the state's power grid, its 35 natural-gas turbines will feed the data center directly, potentially creating one of the largest single greenhouse gas emitters in the country. The move highlights a growing tension between Big Tech's insatiable demand for AI-related energy and its public pledges to honor climate commitments.

**[America’s Grid-Scale Battery Infrastructure Pivots to Sodium-Ion Tech](https://hardware.slashdot.org/story/26/08/08/1940232/americas-first-grid-scale-sodium-ion-battery-plant-gets-its-first-big-test)** · Slashdot
Peak Energy selected Sacramento, California, for a \$71 million, 183,000-square-foot facility that will manufacture up to 4 gigawatt-hours of grid-scale sodium-ion battery storage annually. Meanwhile, a commercial partnership between Syntropic Power and UNIGRID aims to deploy proprietary sodium chromium oxide batteries, which independent tests reveal actually absorb heat (endothermic behavior) during charging. These developments signal a major shift toward cheaper, safer, and geopolitically secure grid storage that can operate efficiently without complex mechanical cooling.

**[Trump Administration Pays \$1.2 Billion to Halt U.S. Offshore Wind Projects](https://hardware.slashdot.org/story/26/08/07/2157219/trump-administration-to-pay-german-firm-12-billion-to-halt-us-wind-projects)** · Slashdot
German energy giant RWE agreed to abandon its offshore wind leases along the California, Louisiana, and New York coasts in exchange for a \$1.2 billion payout from the Department of the Interior. Secretary Doug Burgum praised the deal, framing it as a shift away from "costly subsidies" toward "common sense" energy, while RWE plans to reinvest the payout into conventional gas and LNG export projects. This follows similar federal buyouts of French developer TotalEnergies and Charlotte-based Duke Energy to terminate their offshore wind leases earlier this year.

**[GitHub Hardens npm and Actions Defaults, Drawing Debate over Time Cooldowns](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/08/github-npm-actions-defaults)** · InfoQ
GitHub has consolidated multiple security changes across npm and GitHub Actions, altering platform defaults to break critical links in supply chain attacks. While the changes include a 72-hour account freeze for email updates and restricting untrusted workflow forks, developer reactions are highly divided. Critics argue that time-based cooldowns are a low-tech band-aid that fails to address the lack of author-side package signing—a security standard that practitioners have requested for a decade but npm has repeatedly declined to avoid deterring contributors.

**[Cloudflare's Precursor Aims to Detect Bots and AI Agents via Session Behavior](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/08/cloudflare-precursor-detection)** · InfoQ
Cloudflare launched its new client-side engine, Precursor, which continuously monitors user-journey signals like mouse movements and keyboard timing to identify sophisticated bots and AI agents. Rather than relying on one-time challenges like CAPTCHAs, Precursor analyzes real-time behavioral patterns shaped by human physiology and cognition to distinguish automated traffic from legitimate users. While the tool reduces friction for real users, critics raised concerns about privacy and warned that the telemetry data might help bot developers build more realistic human simulations.

**[Google DeepMind’s AI Model Gives Forecasters Extra Lead Time for Cyclones](https://arstechnica.com/science/2026/08/deepminds-hurricane-model-bought-forecasters-an-extra-day/)** · Ars Technica
A study published in Nature reveals that Google DeepMind's WeatherNext AI model can predict cyclones with unprecedented accuracy, providing an average of one additional day of warning time compared to existing models. During October 2025's Hurricane Melissa, WeatherNext successfully predicted five days in advance that the storm would hit Jamaica as a catastrophic Category 5 hurricane, whereas traditional models were conflicted. This breakthrough shows how machine learning can radically improve disaster preparation, where an extra 24 hours can make a massive difference on the ground.

**[Police Misuse and Obfuscation of Flock Camera Systems Spark Alarm](https://yro.slashdot.org/story/26/08/08/0453229/more-police-officers-fired-investigated-or-arrested-for-misusing-flock-camera-systems)** · Slashdot
Sworn police officers across multiple states have been fired or arrested for using Flock Safety license plate readers to stalk ex-wives, partners, and personal acquaintances. This comes as internal policies from an Iowa sheriff's department and guidance from the FBI and DOJ instruct officers to be "as vague as permissible" and explicitly conceal their use of Flock in reports to avoid public records requests. Furthermore, data entry errors in the system led police to pull a Milwaukee woman over at gunpoint twice in one week.
