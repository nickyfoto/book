# Tech News — 2026-05-05

## Story of the Day
Apple is facing a harsh reality check on its AI promises, agreeing to a $250 million settlement for misleading iPhone buyers about its delayed Apple Intelligence features. Forced to adapt, the company will reportedly open iOS 27 to third-party AI models, allowing users to swap out Siri for alternative chatbots system-wide.

## Top Stories

**Coinbase Cuts 14% of Staff in "AI-Native" Pivot** · [The New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/05/05/technology/coinbase-layoffs-ai.html)
Coinbase is laying off roughly 700 employees to restructure the crypto exchange into a "lean, fast, and AI-native" company. CEO Brian Armstrong claims that engineers are using AI to ship code in days instead of weeks, prompting the company to organize around "AI-native talent who can manage fleets of agents". This marks one of the most explicit examples of a major tech firm using AI efficiency as the primary justification for mass workforce reductions.

**US Government Secures Early Access to Frontier AI Models** · [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/924017/google-microsoft-xai-government-review)
Google, Microsoft, and xAI have agreed to let the US Commerce Department’s Center for AI Standards and Innovation (CAISI) evaluate their new AI models before public release. CAISI will perform pre-deployment security evaluations to assess frontier capabilities, joining existing review agreements the center already holds with OpenAI and Anthropic. This signals an escalating regulatory grip on foundational AI development ahead of potential legislative action.

**Book Publishers Slap Meta with Copyright Lawsuit** · [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/tech/924230/meta-publishers-lawsuit-ai-copyright)
Five major book publishers, including Macmillan, McGraw-Hill, and Elsevier, are suing Meta for "massive infringements" of copyrighted materials used to train its Llama AI models. The class action lawsuit alleges Meta knowingly scraped content from notorious pirate sites like LibGen and Sci-Hub. This represents a shift in legal strategy, with heavy-hitting academic and corporate publishers joining the fray instead of just individual authors.

**Meta Deploys AI Bone Structure Analysis to Boot Underage Users** · [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/tech/923564/facebook-instagram-teen-accounts-ai-bone-analysis)
In a novel approach to age verification, Facebook and Instagram parent Meta is now using AI to scan photos and videos for "height and bone structure" to identify and remove users under 13. Meta insists this is not facial recognition and does not identify specific individuals, but rather looks for visual and contextual clues across posts, bios, and comments. The system is currently active in select countries as the company pushes toward a broader global rollout.

**Google DeepMind Staff Unionize Over Military Contracts** · [Wired](https://www.wired.com/story/google-deepmind-workers-vote-to-unionize-over-military-ai-deals/)
Staff at Google DeepMind’s London headquarters have voted to unionize in an attempt to block the company's AI models from being utilized by the US and Israeli militaries. Represented jointly by the Communication Workers Union and Unite the Union, employees are demanding that Google adhere to its own ethical standards and provide transparency over military applications. The labor push marks a rare, organized internal revolt at a premier AI lab over defense sector monetization.

**Anthropic Releases Autonomous "Auto Mode" for Claude Code** · [InfoQ](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/05/anthropic-claude-code-auto-mode/)
Anthropic has launched an "auto mode" for Claude Code, allowing the system to autonomously write, execute, and refine software development tasks without constant human babysitting. To mitigate risks, the system uses a two-stage classification pipeline that pauses for manual human approval only when attempting high-impact or potentially unsafe operations. This shifts the developer's role from a micro-manager to an approver, reflecting the broader industry trend toward asynchronous, agentic workflows.

**Daemon Tools Backdoored in Massive Supply-Chain Hack** · [Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/05/widely-used-daemon-tools-disk-app-backdoored-in-monthlong-supply-chain-attack/)
The popular disk image mounting application Daemon Tools has been compromised in a month-long supply-chain attack that pushed malicious updates to thousands of machines across over 100 countries. Kaspersky researchers suspect Chinese hackers injected a backdoor into officially signed Windows installers, stealing system data and dropping secondary payloads on select high-value targets in government and retail.
