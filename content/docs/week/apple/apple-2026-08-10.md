# Apple Navigates Rising Supply Costs, Watch Rethinks, and Ecosystem Betas — 2026-08-10

## Highlights
Apple is currently grappling with a severe global memory shortage that is driving up hardware production costs and forcing strategic adjustments to its hardware roadmap. At the same time, the company is actively pushing forward on software innovations, releasing its fifth developer beta updates featuring a potential new hardware-based photo authentication system. To bolster its premium device experiences, Apple is also exploring major watch design overhauls and expanding live 3D sports broadcasts for the Vision Pro.

## Top Stories
- **iPhone 18 Pro Manufacturing Costs Surge 38% Driven by DRAM Shortages**: According to TrendForce, soaring memory prices are expected to raise the bill of materials for the upcoming 256GB iPhone 18 Pro by approximately 38% compared to the iPhone 17 Pro. This memory supply squeeze has prompted analyst Ming-Chi Kuo to report that Apple is scaling back its 2026 hardware shipment plans. While price increases are expected across the iPhone lineup, Apple may choose to sacrifice some gross margins to protect consumer demand and shipment volume. ([Source](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/08/10/iphone-18-pro-build-cost-rise/))
- **Apple Explores Sweeping Rethink of Future Apple Watch Designs**: Mark Gurman reports that Apple is evaluating a major design overhaul for future smartwatches, testing directions that include circular screens, screenless fitness bands, and a return of the ceramic case. Although the upcoming Series 12 and Ultra 4 models are expected to be relatively minor refreshes, this broader rethink could represent a massive shift to adapt to changing consumer health tastes and competition from smart rings. Eddy Cue, who leads Apple's health efforts, is reportedly a major fan of smart rings, suggesting a wearable shake-up is highly likely. ([Source](https://www.macworld.com/article/3208976/a-sweeping-apple-watch-rethink-could-bring-new-designs-and-premium-models.html))
- **Apple Releases Developer Beta 5 of iOS 27 with Hidden Photo-Authentication Feature**: Apple has seeded the fifth developer beta of its next-generation operating systems, including iOS 27 and macOS 27 Golden Gate, delivering refined Liquid Glass icons and expanded Siri AI voice controls. Crucially, hidden code inside the iOS 27 beta reveals "Apple Reference Image," an unreleased security tool that sends sensor and camera metadata to Private Cloud Compute to verify whether a photo was taken with genuine, uncompromised iPhone hardware. If activated, the feature would help combat synthetic and AI-manipulated images by validating physical photo provenance. ([Source](https://9to5mac.com/2026/08/10/heres-whats-new-with-ios-27-beta-5/))
- **Apple to Stream Live Friday Night Baseball Games in Immersive 3D Video on Vision Pro**: Starting August 28, Apple and Major League Baseball will stream weekly Friday Night Baseball matchups live in Apple Immersive Video exclusively for Apple Vision Pro users with an Apple TV subscription. The 3D broadcasts will be recorded in 8K with a 180-degree field of view and feature spatial audio, floating stadium stats, and dedicated commentary. This marks Apple’s first regular live sports offering in the immersive format, capitalizing on the popularity of enhanced viewing experiences on visionOS. ([Source](https://www.macstories.net/news/friday-night-baseball-goes-immersive-august-28-on-apple-vision-pro/))

## Articles Worth Reading

**The 20th Anniversary All-Glass iPhone Might Not Happen After All** ([Macworld](https://www.macworld.com/article/3209798/the-20th-anniversary-all-glass-iphone-might-not-happen-after-all.html))
Jefferies analyst Edison Lee reports that Apple has canceled plans for its highly anticipated, radically redesigned all-glass 20th-anniversary iPhone due to poor production yields and soaring memory costs. The quad-curved, liquid-glass device was expected to launch in September 2027 with a blended retail price of \$2,060, serving as a premium average selling price and margin driver for Apple. Following the suspected cancellation, Jefferies downgraded Apple stock to "Underperform," viewing the move as a major setback to Apple's premium pricing power. This article is highly worth reading because it illustrates how complex manufacturing yields and escalating global supply chain costs are directly reshaping Apple’s long-term hardware roadmap and Wall Street outlook.

**Apple Briefly Posts and Removes Qwen AI Support Guide for Mac Users in China** ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/08/10/apple-posts-guide-for-connecting-siri-to-qwen-ai/))
Apple briefly published and then removed a Chinese-language support document explaining how Mac users in mainland China could connect Alibaba's Qwen AI to Siri and Writing Tools on macOS 26.6. The document revealed that users would have to log into their personal Qwen accounts, which suggests that standard Siri AI privacy protections—where neither Apple nor partner servers have access to user interactions—may not apply under strict local regulations. This setup reflects the unique regulatory landscape in China, which requires Apple to partner with approved local providers like Alibaba rather than Google's Gemini models used elsewhere. This piece is an essential read for understanding how regional compliance and geopolitical realities are forcing Apple to make major operational and privacy compromises in one of its largest markets.

**Apple Scrapped "White-Glove" Setup Plan for Leased iPhones** ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/08/10/apple-scrapped-plan-ship-iphones-set-up-for-you/))
Mark Gurman reveals that Apple originally considered a "white-glove" setup experience for its newly launched "Apple Upgrade" device leasing program. Under the proposed plan, replacement devices would have arrived at customers' doors with their personal iCloud data pre-loaded before shipping, eliminating the tedious restoring process on launch day. Despite the technical feasibility, Apple ultimately abandoned the concept because of potential privacy concerns. The company worried that receiving a device with pre-installed personal data would unnerve users and raise concerns about how their data was handled during shipment. This article is worth reading to get a rare glimpse into the internal friction between Apple's customer convenience goals and its protective stance on user privacy optics.
