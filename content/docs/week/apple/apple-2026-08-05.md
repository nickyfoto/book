# Apple's Next Chapter: Private Relay Vulnerabilities, Upcoming Foldables, and the Return of Ted Lasso — 2026-08-05

## Highlights
Today's Apple ecosystem news centers around anticipation and transition, highlighted by upcoming hardware announcements under a landmark leadership shift. Meanwhile, security researchers have exposed a major WebKit flaw in iCloud Private Relay that risks leaking users' real IP addresses through standard passkey requests. Finally, we see real-world testing of Apple's latest generative AI photo tools and modular ecosystem accessories, revealing both the impressive potential and current limits of Apple's platform technologies.

## Top Stories
- **[Vulnerability in iCloud Private Relay Exposes Real IP Addresses](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/08/05/icloud-private-relay-ip-address-leak/)**: Security researchers have disclosed a critical WebKit flaw that allows websites using or pretending to use Passkeys to bypass iCloud Private Relay and expose users' real IP addresses. Because the operating system's credential service issues WebAuthn requests directly outside the browser stack, it completely evades Safari's proxied path. Apple is investigating the issue and planning a software update to address it in Fall 2026. ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/08/05/icloud-private-relay-ip-address-leak/))
- **[Apple Prepares for Landmark September Event as Ternus Era Begins](https://9to5mac.com/2026/08/05/iphone-18-pro-event-date-when-announcement/)**: Under incoming CEO John Ternus, Apple is gearing up for a major September event expected to feature the debut of Apple's first foldable device, the "iPhone Ultra," alongside the iPhone 18 Pro lineup. To support the massive scale of this in-person event, Apple has launched an exclusive lottery program for U.S. retail employees to assist on-site. The presentation is also anticipated to showcase an extensive Siri AI overhaul optimized by the upcoming S11 chip. ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/08/05/iphone-18-pro-event-date-when-announcement/))
- **[Ted Lasso Returns to Apple TV+ for Season 4](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/08/05/ted-lasso-season-4/)**: The award-winning comedy hit has returned after a three-year hiatus, debuting the first episode of a planned ten-episode run. This season sees Ted coaching a second-division women's team, the Lady Greyhounds, introducing a fresh ensemble of female and non-binary athletes. While most of the original men's roster is absent, core cast members like Hannah Waddingham and Juno Temple return to lead the production. ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/08/05/ted-lasso-season-4/))
- **[CarPlay Expands Beyond the Road onto Pontoon Boats](https://9to5mac.com/2026/08/04/apple-carplay-expands-to-select-pontoon-boats-with-on-water-navigation/)**: MasterCraft Boat Holdings is bringing Apple CarPlay and Android Auto standard to its Crest and Balise pontoon brands starting with the 2027 model year. Partnering with Savvy Navvy, the integration will deliver dedicated on-water navigation, including 2D/3D charts, smart routing, nearby boat traffic, and bathymetry directly to the helm display. The first compatible models are scheduled to launch next month. ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/08/04/apple-carplay-expands-to-select-pontoon-boats-with-on-water-navigation/))
- **[Sunbird’s iMessage for Android Relaunches on Google Play Store](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/08/05/sunbird-imessage-for-android/)**: Sunbird has reintroduced its messaging app to Android users, offering "blue bubble" chats alongside RCS, SMS, and MMS in a single interface. After a previous shutdown due to severe security concerns regarding unencrypted Apple ID credentials, the company now claims that messages are fully encrypted and passwords are destroyed immediately after session setup. However, with Apple's recent adoption of RCS, many of Sunbird's original feature advantages have become native across platforms. ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/08/05/sunbird-imessage-for-android/))

**[I Pushed iOS 27’s AI Photo Editing Tools to the Limit](https://www.macworld.com/article/3194029/i-pushed-ios-27s-ai-photo-editing-tools-to-the-limit.html)** ([Macworld](https://www.macworld.com/article/3194029/i-pushed-ios-27s-ai-photo-editing-tools-to-the-limit.html))
In this hands-on test of iOS 27's AI-centric editing features, the author puts the Photos app's new Reframe, Extend, and Clean Up tools to the test with real-world iPhone 17 Pro Max images. While Reframe and Extend offer impressive perspective adjustments, they are prone to AI hallucinations and pattern repetition on complex subjects like airplanes and lighthouses. However, the upgraded Clean Up tool stands out, showing that its new High Quality mode is remarkably precise at reconstructing complex details—such as generating believable kitten bodies—compared to the faster, blemish-focused Fast mode. This review is worth reading for any enthusiast wanting a realistic expectation of Apple's system-wide AI imaging limits.

**[The Utility App Flood Won’t Last](https://www.macstories.net/stories/the-utility-app-flood-wont-last/)** ([MacStories](https://www.macstories.net/stories/the-utility-app-flood-wont-last/))

Although the massive supply of tools initially benefits users, the author argues that the trend is unsustainable because app supply is outstripping demand by orders of magnitude. Furthermore, power users can now easily build custom, simple utilities themselves using command-line agent tools, diminishing the market for independent utility wrapper apps. It is a thoughtful read that contextualizes the long-term impact of AI code-generation tools on the broader App Store economics.
