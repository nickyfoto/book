# Apple’s Autumn Strategy Emerges: App Store Fee Proposals, Foldable Bottlenecks, and Domestic Manufacturing Moves — 2026-08-13

## Highlights
**Apple's operational and hardware roadmap is coming into sharp focus as the company navigates pivotal legal, organizational, and technological transitions. Today's major developments showcase aggressive pushes into domestic smart manufacturing and paid news content partnerships to bolster Siri AI, contrasted by lingering supply-chain challenges for its upcoming folding hardware. Simultaneously, Apple has proposed a new off-App Store fee model in its continuing legal war with Epic Games, while marking a significant transition point for legacy devices by declaring the historic iPhone X obsolete.**

## Top Stories
- **App Store Link-Out Fees Proffered**: Apple has submitted a court-ordered proposal in its ongoing dispute with Epic Games, outlining proposed commission rates for U.S. applications that direct users to external, off-App Store purchasing options. Under this new structure, standard apps would pay a 15% commission, Small Business Program participants would owe 5%, and subscription renewals or partner programs would be taxed at 10%. This remand submission follows the Supreme Court's denial of Apple's request to pause lower-court rate-setting proceedings while the high court reviews its contempt-of-court battle. ([Source](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/08/13/app-store-fees-apple-link-outs/))
- **Staggered Launch for Foldable 'iPhone Ultra'**: Apple's first foldable smartphone, rumored to be named the 'iPhone Ultra,' may see a staggered, country-by-country rollout starting strictly in the U.S. market, mimicking the launch strategy used for the Vision Pro. Australian carriers and Chinese component suppliers have indicated that the book-style foldable will be a "no show" on international shelves when the iPhone 18 Pro lineup debuts in September, owing to production hiccups, display durability testing, and supply bottlenecks. The highly complex device is expected to feature a 7.8-inch unfolded inner display, dual batteries, and a retail price starting above \$2,000. ([Source](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/08/13/iphone-ultra-staggered-country-rollout/))
- **Advanced Manufacturing Center Opens in Houston**: CEO Tim Cook announced the grand opening of a new 20,000-square-foot Advanced Manufacturing Center (AMC) in Houston, Texas, designed to provide free training in smart manufacturing and machine learning automation to small and medium businesses. The high-tech center sits within the same facility that builds Apple's advanced AI servers and is officially slated to begin manufacturing a newly redesigned Mac mini later this year. The massive investment is part of Apple's ongoing pledge to spend \$600 billion supporting advanced manufacturing initiatives within the United States. ([Source](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/08/13/apple-houston-manufacturing-center/))
- **Siri AI Deals for News Content**: Apple is actively negotiating multiyear agreements with major news publishers, budgeting a nine-figure sum to secure Siri AI access to real-time, high-quality news content. Unlike other AI developers who pay broad, flat fees for data scraping, Apple is proposing a compensation model that pays publishers directly when their stories are utilized. This aggressive push is designed to ensure accuracy and prevent the sort of embarrassing, hallucinated summaries that led Apple to pull its notification news summaries for over a year. ([Source](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/08/12/apple-siri-ai-publisher-talks/))
- **Ted Lasso Season 4 Sets Streaming Records**: The season four premiere of the hit comedy series *Ted Lasso* racked up a record-breaking 296.6 million viewing minutes in the U.S. during its August 4–5 launch window, officially registering as the largest television debut in Apple TV+ history. On August 5 alone, the premiering episode was the single most-streamed program in the nation, easily eclipsing major platform rivals and previous seasonal debuts. The underdog series has officially returned to its roots, following Ted as he coaches a second-division women's football team back in Richmond. ([Source](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/08/13/ted-lasso-premiere-record/))
- **iPhone X Declared Obsolete**: In a significant milestone for Apple's legacy hardware, the groundbreaking 2017 iPhone X and the 2018 15-inch MacBook Pro have both been officially demoted from vintage to obsolete status. Moving to the obsolete list means Apple has permanently discontinued all hardware support, and authorized service centers can no longer order replacement parts. The iPhone X was historically Apple's first edge-to-edge smartphone featuring Face ID, and its final major software support officially ended with the iOS 16 release cycle. ([Source](https://9to5mac.com/2026/08/13/apple-adds-iphone-x-and-2018-macbook-pro-to-obsolete-products-list/))

## Articles Worth Reading

**Apple's Revolving Executive Door** ([Source](https://www.macworld.com/article/3212388/apples-revolving-door-continues-to-spin-with-more-big-name-exec-changes.html))
Apple is navigating a significant wave of high-profile executive departures as it prepares for incoming CEO John Ternus to take the reins on September 1. The departures of Jennifer Bailey, head of Pay and Wallet services, and legal chief Katherine Adams represent a major loss of institutional knowledge, sparking concerns of a broader senior executive exodus. To stabilize operations, Ternus is bringing back key trusted deputies, such as retired hardware VP Laura Legros, and hiring external veterans like Nate Gatten for government affairs. This piece is an essential read for understanding how Apple’s management structure is pivoting during one of its most intense transitions in decades.

**The App Store Curation Dilemma** ([Source](https://www.macstories.net/linked/raising-the-gates-is-not-the-way-to-improve-the-app-store/))
Following developer Jeff Johnson’s exposure of rampant App Store submission scams and "AI slop," debate has erupted over how Apple should clean up its digital marketplace. While some propose raising developer tiers and fees to price out bad actors, author John Voorhees argues that erecting higher barriers to entry is a form of gatekeeping that would harm hobbyists, students, and indie creators. Instead of punishing fresh innovators, the piece argues that Apple should aggressively target the active spammers and enforce existing rules. This article provides a crucial, balanced perspective on how platform security shouldn't come at the cost of developer accessibility.

**Has Apple TV Lost the Plot?** ([Source](https://www.macworld.com/article/3211324/apple-tv-has-lost-the-plot.html))
This provocative analysis breaks down why the Apple TV 4K is rapidly losing its appeal to cord-cutters following a steep 67% price increase for the base model. The set-top box currently sits at a steep disadvantage compared to cheaper rivals, running a four-year-old chip and being completely left behind by modern Apple Intelligence features due to RAM limitations. The piece argues that Apple TV needs to embrace unique software strategies, like a native Steam Link equivalent or an Xbox Cloud Gaming competitor, to establish itself as a true home entertainment hub. It’s a compelling look at Apple's pricing challenges during a broader technology memory crisis.
