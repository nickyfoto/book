# Apple — Week of 2026-09-05 to 2026-09-11

## Week in Review
Apple marked a monumental transition this week as John Ternus officially assumed the role of CEO, taking center stage at the "Surprise and Shine" keynote following Tim Cook’s move to Executive Chair. The headline event delivered Apple's long-awaited hardware revolution, debuting the \$1,999 foldable iPhone Duo alongside the flagship iPhone 18 Pro lineup powered by 2nm A20 Pro silicon. Broad ecosystem updates rounded out the milestone week, including the S11-powered Apple Watch Series 12, active noise cancellation on base AirPods 5, and final Release Candidates for iOS 27 ahead of public rollout.

## Top Stories
* **[John Ternus Takes CEO Helm Amid App Store Monetization Shift]** · [Source](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/09/05/top-stories-suprise-and-shine-preview/)
  John Ternus officially succeeded Tim Cook as Apple CEO ahead of the fall keynote, with Cook transitioning to Executive Chair and intentionally stepping back from event videos to solidify Ternus as the sole face of Apple's hardware. In parallel, a strategic drive by Ternus and Services SVP Eddy Cue to boost App Store margins led longtime Apple Fellow Phil Schiller to step aside from managing the platform over concerns regarding developer and regulatory backlash. Ternus subsequently led his first product keynote on September 9, ushering in Apple's foldable era and setting a new trajectory for executive leadership.

* **[Apple Enters Foldables with the \$1,999 iPhone Duo]** · [Source](https://www.macstories.net/news/iphone-18-pro-and-iphone-duo-the-macstories-overview/)
  Following years of development and supply chain rumors, Apple officially unveiled the book-style iPhone Duo, featuring a 5.4-inch outer cover screen that opens into a 7.6-inch nano-texture inner display driven by the 2nm A20 Pro chip and C2 modem. The device introduces tailored iOS 27.1 multitasking—enforcing a fixed 50/50 Split View to preserve app aspect ratios—while utilizing Touch ID in the side button and dual 48MP Fusion cameras. Executive interviews revealed Apple secured a three-year exclusive deal with Samsung Display for its \$250 M16 OLED panels, with pre-orders scheduled for October 16 at \$1,999 prior to an October 23 launch.

* **[iPhone 18 Pro Debuts 2nm A20 Pro Chip and Variable Aperture Camera]** · [Source](https://www.macworld.com/article/2953687/iphone-18-pro-max-2026-release-date-design-specs-price.html)
  Architectural hints from the M6 chip and iOS 27 code leaks culminated in the launch of the iPhone 18 Pro and Pro Max, built on TSMC's 2nm A20 Pro processor and equipped with a 3x larger surface area vapor chamber. The new camera architecture features a 48MP Fusion lens with a 6-blade mechanical variable aperture (f/1.48 to f/4.0), stock Camera app manual controls, and hardware-level Apple Reference Image verification. Pre-orders open Saturday, September 12 starting at \$1,199 for the Pro and \$1,299 for the Pro Max, with retail shipping set for September 18.

* **[Apple Watch Series 12 & Ultra 4 Feature Health Sensing and Audio Intelligence]** · [Source](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/09/09/apple-watch-series-12-announced-all-day-hr-sensor/)
  Early watchOS 27 code discoveries materialized into the official reveal of the Apple Watch Series 12 and Ultra 4, powered by the new S11 chip paired with 4GB of RAM. The refreshed lineup incorporates an upgraded Health Sensing System capable of 5-second heart-rate tracking and daytime Readiness scores, alongside ambient Audio Intelligence features like Live Rewind and Siri Recap protected by Secure Enclave hardware isolation. Starting at \$399 for Series 12—which revives ceramic chassis choices—and \$799 for Ultra 4, both wearables ship on September 18.

## Also Worth Knowing
* **AirPods 5 & Fall OS Release Candidates**: Apple added Active Noise Cancellation to its base \$129 AirPods 5 while seeding Release Candidates for iOS 27, macOS Golden Gate, and watchOS 27 ahead of public launch on Monday, September 14.
* **Sonera Acquisition**: EU filings confirmed Apple’s acquisition of startup Sonera, securing proprietary biomagnetic sensors for non-invasive neural and muscle sensing across future wearables.
* **Bug Bounty Portal Throttling**: Apple instituted submission caps and a 30-day cooldown on its security reporting portal to filter AI-generated vulnerability reports, raising concerns among defensive security researchers.
* **Remaining 2026 Hardware Pipeline**: Reports indicate Apple still plans to launch eight additional hardware products before year-end, including an OLED iPad mini, M6-powered Macs, a redesigned Apple TV, and a smart Home Hub.
