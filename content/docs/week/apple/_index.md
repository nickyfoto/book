# Apple — Week of 2026-08-15 to 2026-08-21

## Week in Review
This week, Apple underwent major strategic realignments, balancing sweeping **regulatory concessions in Europe** with a significant **workforce restructuring** that prioritized artificial intelligence over spatial computing. A series of unprecedented **operating system leaks** exposed the company's multi-year hardware pipeline, including concrete details of unreleased smart home devices and advanced dual-camera accessories. At the same time, Apple modified its upcoming hardware release schedule, **delaying standard iPhone models** while preparing to showcase its premium flagships.

## Top Stories

* **Massive macOS Code Leaks Reveal "HomePad" Hub and Dual-Camera AirPods Specs** · [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/08/17/macos-26-7-unreleased-apple-devices/)
An accidental roadmap exposure within the macOS Tahoe 26.7 release candidate code laid bare a vast slate of unreleased Apple hardware, including the tvOS-powered **"HomePad" smart hub** with a watchOS-style interface and a redesigned Siri Remote. The leaks also showcased unreleased **camera-equipped AirPods** with synchronized dual 1-megapixel RGB "Kino Sensors" that capture dual-angle still frames to feed Siri's Visual Intelligence system. Although an instructional video leaked early, reports confirm the smart earbuds are facing supply chain bottlenecks and remain scheduled for a **late 2027 launch** alongside the 20th-anniversary iPhone.

* **Apple Overhauls EU App Store Fees to Settle Digital Markets Act Battle** · [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/08/20/apples-long-running-app-store-battle-with-the-eu-appears-to-finally-be-over/)
To resolve its long-running antitrust dispute with the European Commission, Apple has officially introduced revised business terms for EU developers effective October 1, 2026. The highly controversial per-install Core Technology Fee is being replaced with a flat **5% Core Technology Commission** on digital transactions inside alternatively distributed apps, while overall commission rates will range from 5% to 26% depending on distribution and payment channels. Although the settlement marks a major policy shift, third-party developers like Epic Games have criticized the updated terms as "junk fees" while continuing to push back against competitor monitoring and installation barriers in other global markets.

* **Cupertino Delays Base iPhone 18 in Unprecedented Fall Launch Shakeup** · [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/08/20/iphone-18-wont-launch-next-month-heres-when-its-coming-instead/)
In a surprising departure from its traditional release pattern, Apple is reportedly splitting its fall hardware launch, holding back the entry-level **iPhone 18, 18e, and iPhone Air 2** until early 2027. This strategic shift, driven by portfolio pacing and the ongoing global memory crisis, leaves next month's keynote exclusive to the premium **iPhone 18 Pro, iPhone 18 Pro Max, and a folding "iPhone Ultra"**. While the Pro models are expected to feature the cutting-edge 2nm A20 Pro chip and an exclusive variable aperture wide camera for the Pro Max, external case listings suggest the physical chassis dimensions will remain virtually identical to the current iPhone 17 Pro.

* **Apple Reorganizes Workforce for AI, Laying Off Siri and Vision Pro Teams** · [Macworld](https://www.macworld.com/article/3217991/apple-lays-off-200-employees-to-focus-on-new-devices-and-ai.html)
Apple has conducted a series of targeted structural reorganizations, laying off approximately **200 employees** across its Siri, software engineering, and Vision Products groups to double down on artificial intelligence. The Vision Pro gaming and immersive video teams bore the brunt of the cuts as spatial computing hardware gets placed "on ice" due to high production costs and shifting leadership priorities. Meanwhile, Siri and Intelligent Systems teams are being thoroughly overhauled to assemble the specialized expertise required for the upcoming **"Siri AI" platform**.

## Also Worth Knowing

* **Walmart Ends Contactless Payment Holdout**: After a decade of resistance prioritizing its QR-based system, Walmart announced a phased, state-by-state rollout of NFC contactless payments, including **Apple Pay**, starting August 24 in Arkansas and reaching all US stores by the end of 2026.
* **Tim Cook Legacy Retrospective**: With Tim Cook set to transition to Executive Chairman on September 1, 2026, and John Ternus preparing to take over as CEO, columns are reflecting on his 15-year tenure which achieved massive commercial growth while establishing strict supplier codes and key environmental and accessibility reforms.
* **\$17 Billion Tax Payout to Ireland**: In compliance with new EU transparency regulations, Apple's tax filings disclosed a massive **\$17 billion corporate tax payment** to Ireland, heavily driven by the resolution of the European Court of Justice's €13 billion illegal state aid ruling.
* **Touchscreen "MacBook Ultra" in Development**: Reports suggest a thinner, lighter MacBook is in development for fall, introducing **touch support** to the Mac line for the first time alongside a tandem OLED display, a Dynamic Island, and existing M5 Pro/Max silicon.
