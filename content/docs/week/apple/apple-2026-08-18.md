# Massive Regulatory Shifts and Hardware Leaks Upend the Apple Ecosystem — 2026-08-18

## Highlights
Today represents a major watershed moment for the Apple ecosystem, driven by sweeping regulatory concessions in Europe and an unprecedented wave of product leaks from Apple's own operating system code. To settle its ongoing Digital Markets Act dispute, Apple has announced a complete overhaul of its App Store commission rates and developer terms in the European Union. Meanwhile, the macOS 26.7 release candidate has exposed Apple's entire upcoming hardware roadmap, detailing everything from camera-equipped AirPods to a smart Home Hub and a foldable "iPhone Ultra". 

## Top Stories
- **Apple Overhauls EU App Store Fees to Settle Digital Markets Act Dispute**: In a massive policy shift, Apple has introduced simplified business terms for EU developers following close collaboration with the European Commission to resolve outstanding disputes. The highly controversial per-install Core Technology Fee is being completely replaced by a flat 5% Core Technology Commission on digital transactions inside alternatively distributed apps. Furthermore, developers are now permitted to offer alternative payment options directly alongside Apple's native In-App Purchase system under newly adjusted commission structures. ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/08/18/eu-app-store-fee-change/))
- **Camera-Equipped AirPods and Enormous Hardware Roadmap Exposed in macOS Code**: A major leak hidden inside the macOS 26.7 release candidate has revealed a demonstration video of unreleased AirPods featuring built-in cameras designed to feed visual data to Siri and "Visual Intelligence". The software update also references nearly 40 unreleased Apple devices, exposing codenames and model numbers for a foldable "iPhone Ultra," touchscreen OLED MacBook Pros, a redesigned iPad mini, and a Siri-enabled smart Home Hub running "Pebble" (homeOS). This massive leak suggests that Apple is rapidly preparing for a highly diverse, multi-category hardware rollout starting as early as next month. ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/08/17/camera-equipped-airpods-macos-26-7/))
- **iPhone 18 Pro Max Rumored to Receive Exclusive Variable Aperture Camera**: Apple is expected to further differentiate its upcoming flagship smartphones by reserving a new variable aperture Wide camera exclusively for the larger iPhone 18 Pro Max. This rumor departs from previous expectations of camera parity across both Pro models, which had been supported by leaked files from supplier Tata Electronics confirming the use of a Sony IMX905 variable aperture sensor. The exclusive upgrade will allow users to manually adjust the aperture for superior depth of field and low-light control, though it may result in a thicker and heavier chassis. ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/08/18/iphone-18-pro-max-camera-upgrade-exclusive/))
- **Apple's U.S. App Store Commission Revenue Falls 18% Under Legal Pressures**: Third-party data reveals that Apple's high-margin services business is experiencing a notable slowdown as regulators force the company to relax its grip on in-app purchases. According to Appfigures, Apple's U.S. commission revenue has plunged 18% since the start of 2026, a decline heavily driven by legal injunctions that permit developers to steer users to external payment platforms. This downward trend contributed to Apple's latest quarterly services revenue of \$30.7 billion falling short of Wall Street's \$31.4 billion expectations, raising concern among investors. ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/08/18/apple-app-store-revenue-falling/))
- **Apple Rebuffs DOJ Reconsideration Bid in Ongoing Federal Antitrust Suit**: Apple has filed a strongly worded response opposing the Department of Justice’s attempt to block its subpoena of documents from 14 federal agencies. Apple argues that the DOJ's motion to reconsider Apple's discovery victory "fails at every level" and represents an impermissible attempt to re-litigate settled issues. Apple originally won the right to seek files from agencies like the CIA, FBI, and NSA to prove that the federal government chose Apple devices for their superior privacy features, which the company argues justifies its closed ecosystem. ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/08/17/apple-dojs-latest-challenge-in-antitrust-case-fails-at-every-level/))
