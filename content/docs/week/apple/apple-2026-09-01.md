# A New Era Begins: John Ternus Takes the Helm as Apple CEO Ahead of ‘Phenomenal’ Fall Event — 2026-09-01

## Highlights
Today marks a monumental shift in Cupertino as **John Ternus officially takes over as Apple’s CEO**, with Tim Cook transitioning to Executive Chairman. This historic leadership milestone comes alongside official teasers for a **"phenomenal" hardware launch event on September 9**, featuring the highly anticipated foldable **"iPhone Ultra"**. Meanwhile, Apple faces an escalating legal feud with **OpenAI** over trade secrets, shifts its mapping database to comply with a **political renaming of Lake Ontario**, and continues **winding down the Intel Mac era**.

## Top Stories
- **John Ternus officially becomes Apple CEO**: Today, Apple officially updated its leadership page, marking John Ternus's first day as CEO while Tim Cook transitions to Executive Chairman. In his first employee memo, Ternus praised Cook and teased upcoming products, while his updated official bio highlights a strong focus on hardware reliability, durability, and materials innovation. SEC filings reveal Ternus's target compensation is set at \$58 million, compared to Cook's \$47 million in his new role. ([Source](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/09/01/john-ternus-is-now-apple-ceo/))
- **Apple Teases ‘Huge Launch’ for September 9 Fall Event**: In his inaugural memo, new CEO John Ternus teased a "huge launch next week that's going to be phenomenal". Apple marketing chief Greg Joswiak also teased on X that the company has "more to say" on Wednesday, September 9, when the company will hold its next special event at 10 a.m. Pacific Time. The keynote is expected to unveil the iPhone 18 Pro, iPhone 18 Pro Max, Apple's first-ever foldable iPhone ("iPhone Ultra"), and new Apple Watches. ([Source](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/09/01/apple-teases-huge-launch-next-week/))
- **Fresh Specs and Multitasking Details Leak for Foldable ‘iPhone Ultra’**: In an interview with Tom’s Guide, Bloomberg's Mark Gurman revealed that Apple’s upcoming foldable "iPhone Ultra" will support MagSafe charging and sport a 5.5-inch cover display with a 7.8-inch inner screen. Powered by the A20 Pro chip and a new Apple modem, the device will utilize iPad-like app layouts, side-by-side multitasking, and drag-and-drop capabilities in iOS 27. Apple has reportedly designed a highly durable, "crease-less" liquid metal hinge to meet standard iPhone durability guidelines. ([Source](https://9to5mac.com/2026/09/01/new-iphone-ultra-details-arrive-for-specs-multitasking-launch-timing-more/))
- **Apple Maps Renames Lake Ontario to ‘Lake America’ for U.S. Users**: Following a controversial executive order by President Donald Trump to rename Lake Ontario, Apple has updated Apple Maps to display "Lake America" for users within the United States. Canadian users will still see "Lake Ontario," while international users will see both names. President Trump reportedly reached out to Apple directly to request the change, mirroring a similar renaming of the Gulf of Mexico to "Gulf of America" last year. ([Source](https://9to5mac.com/2026/09/01/apple-maps-renaming-lake-ontario-to-lake-america-following-trump-order/))
- **OpenAI Fires Back at Apple, Calling Trade Secret Lawsuit a ‘Mess of Apple’s Own Making’**: OpenAI has submitted a federal court filing denying Apple’s allegations that former employees stole hardware trade secrets, arguing Apple failed to show any actual intellectual property theft. OpenAI claims Apple’s own sloppy procedures—such as encouraging personal iCloud use for work and quick offboarding windows—are to blame for any files remaining on employee devices. The AI startup characterizes the dispute as a retaliation against employees choosing to leave a slow-moving Apple for a fast-moving competitor. ([Source](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/09/01/dispute-with-openai-said-to-be-a-mess-of-apples-own-making/))
- **Apple Watch Series 12 Upgrades Include Ceramic Cases and Always-On Heart Rate Sensor**: Rumors suggest the upcoming Apple Watch Series 12 will feature a more powerful, AI-focused S-class chip designed to power the demanding Siri AI in watchOS 27. The Series 12 will reportedly add an always-on heart rate sensor for continuous daily data collection and introduce premium white and dark gray Ceramic casing options. The S12 chip is crucial for future-proofing, as watchOS 27 reportedly drops support for Apple Watch models older than three years due to high AI processing requirements. ([Source](https://9to5mac.com/2026/09/01/apple-watch-series-12-three-new-features-coming-next-week/))

**“The impact was the biggest surprise“: Swiggy’s transition to native pays off** ([Source](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=o5spbekm))
This developer profile details how Swiggy, India’s leading convenience platform, successfully migrated its high-volume Instamart and Dineout services from hybrid web environments to native Swift and SwiftUI apps. The development team shares how going native resolved issues like high page load times, app hangs on large SKU lists, and poor rendering of rich restaurant media. Beyond dramatic performance gains, the team was surprised by how the transition directly boosted business metrics like conversion rates and slashed bounce rates. This is an incredibly insightful read for developers wanting real-world evidence of the business and design value that SwiftUI and native iOS architecture bring to complex, highly-scaled platforms.

**Perplexity Introduces ‘Hybrid Compute’ for Computer Agent on Apple Silicon Macs** ([Source](https://www.macstories.net/news/perplexity-introduces-hybrid-compute-to-keep-sensitive-data-local/))
This article breaks down Perplexity’s new "hybrid compute" feature, which splits complex AI tasks between cloud-based frontier models and local, private models on M-series Macs. It details how an on-device classifier screens tasks for sensitive PII, allowing users to process private data locally using models like Qwen 3.8 before returning results to the cloud orchestrator. The feature showcases how Apple silicon’s unified memory architecture (requiring a minimum of 24GB of RAM) is uniquely capable of running heavy, local-first agentic tasks. It is essential reading for anyone interested in the future of data privacy in AI workflows and the practical applications of Apple silicon hardware for local machine learning.
