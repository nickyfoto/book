# Legal Drama, App Store Extortion, and September Launch Prep — 2026-08-04

## Highlights
Today's Apple news is dominated by high-stakes legal and security drama, featuring a massive public rebuttal with receipts from OpenAI and a bizarre App Store removal of Telegram engineered by "takedown extortionists". Meanwhile, Apple is laying the logistical groundwork for its highly anticipated September event, even as pricing rumors suggest significant hardware price hikes are on the horizon. In software news, EU regulations are forcing Apple's hand to finally plan cross-device clipboard integration with Windows PCs.

## Top Stories
- **[OpenAI Fires Back at Apple's Trade Secrets Lawsuit With iMessage Receipts](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/08/04/openai-posts-public-rebuttal-to-apple/)**: OpenAI has published a highly public, detailed rebuttal to Apple's preliminary injunction request, calling the trade secrets lawsuit "careless, aggressive and oddly personal". The response includes iMessage screenshots showing Apple employees asking the accused former engineer for help finding files months after his departure, and argues that Apple's own "residual access" system management is at fault. Additionally, OpenAI revealed that Apple's legal team initially emailed the wrong person in February because they confused two Asian last names. ([Source](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/08/04/openai-posts-public-rebuttal-to-apple/))
- **[Telegram Briefly Pulled From App Store After Takedown Extortion Campaign](https://9to5mac.com/2026/08/04/telegram-ceo-says-extortionist-planted-illegal-content-that-triggered-app-store-removal/)**: Telegram disappeared from the App Store for roughly 40 minutes on Monday night due to a child sexual abuse material (CSAM) violation, but was quickly restored after the group was banned. Telegram CEO Pavel Durov subsequently revealed that the platform was targeted by a "takedown extortionist" who used automated accounts to plant AI-modified content by editing old messages in public groups to trigger an immediate, automated Apple takedown. Durov warned that Apple's sudden reaction without prior contact represents a systemic risk for any mobile application hosting user-generated content. ([Source](https://9to5mac.com/2026/08/04/telegram-ceo-says-extortionist-planted-illegal-content-that-triggered-app-store-removal/))
- **[Apple to Bring Universal Clipboard-Style Sharing to Windows PCs](https://9to5mac.com/2026/08/03/apple-plans-to-open-iphone-clipboard-access-to-windows-pcs/)**: Following an interoperability request from Microsoft in the European Union, Apple has agreed to build a new solution allowing users to copy and paste text and content between iPhones and Windows PCs. The upcoming feature will utilize the AccessorySetupKit framework for one-time user consent to share clipboard data with paired PC accessories. Apple notes that this is a significant engineering effort and expects the solution to debut around fall 2027, likely with iOS 28. ([Source](https://9to5mac.com/2026/08/03/apple-plans-to-open-iphone-clipboard-access-to-windows-pcs/))
- **[Retail Lottery Hints at First-Half September iPhone 18 Event](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/08/04/apple-september-iphone-event-preparation/)**: Apple has opened a lottery for U.S. retail employees to travel to Apple Park to assist with guest check-in, lines, and directions at its upcoming September launch event. The internal memo indicates the event will occur in the first half of September, with Wednesday, September 9 heavily favored as the target date to allow holiday travel around Labor Day. The showcase is expected to feature the iPhone 18 Pro, Apple Watch Series 12, and Apple's first-ever foldable smartphone, rumored to be the "iPhone Ultra". ([Source](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/08/04/apple-september-iphone-event-preparation/))
- **[Apple Limits Bug Bounty Submissions Over AI-Generated Vulnerability Deluge](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/08/04/aple-bug-bounty-limits-ai/)**: Apple has implemented a cap on the number of open submissions a security researcher can have in its bug bounty program due to a massive wave of low-quality reports. Amateur bug hunters have increasingly used generative AI tools like ChatGPT to locate and submit hallucinated, non-existent software vulnerabilities, clogging Apple's human review system. While researchers can request an increase, the restriction was highlighted after cybersecurity startup Bynario hit the submission limit after identifying actual privilege escalation exploits. ([Source](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/08/04/aple-bug-bounty-limits-ai/))

This sharp analysis addresses a multi-billion dollar class-action lawsuit currently proceeding against Apple under Illinois's Biometric Information Privacy Act (BIPA). The author breaks down how the court fundamentally misconstrues the technology: unlike Meta, which processed facial scans on centralized servers, Apple's algorithms run entirely on-device. Furthermore, Apple has no idea who is in the pictures; the system merely groups similar visual appearances locally and relies on the user to manually name them. It is an insightful look at why courts and legislators struggle to grasp the difference between privacy-busting cloud systems and Apple's secure, on-device machine learning.
