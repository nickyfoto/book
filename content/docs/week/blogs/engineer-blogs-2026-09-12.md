# Engineering Reads — 2026-09-12

## The Big Idea
Xe Iaso's biting satire dismantles the performative altruism of AI safety calls, exposing how corporate demands for global research pauses are often thinly veiled rent-seeking designed to let lagging players catch up and monetize proprietary architectures. The fundamental lesson for systems practitioners is to look past high-minded regulatory posturing and evaluate AI governance calls strictly through the lens of economic moats and competitive incentives.

## Deep Reads

**[Everyone should slow down AI development except for me](https://xeiaso.net/notes/2026/everyone-slowdown-but-me/)** · Xe Iaso · [Source](https://xeiaso.net/notes/2026/everyone-slowdown-but-me/)
Xe Iaso delivers a sharp satirical critique of the AI safety landscape, arguing that public calls for global pauses on frontier model research are frequently driven by commercial self-interest rather than genuine existential risk management. Framed through the absurd lens of a fictional lab ("Lygma AGI lab") demanding a moratorium so its own "Intelliga" models can achieve market dominance, the post skewers common industry tropes—such as hand-waving "AGI" as a magical fix for absurd engineering goals, gating subliminal ad-free model outputs behind paid tiers, and fabricating synthetic benchmarks like "FelonyBench". By contrasting noble rhetoric about preventing "XK-class" end-of-the-world scenarios with blatant financial objectives like maximizing bank account balance zeroes, Iaso highlights how safety posturing is routinely weaponized as a moat in the tech ecosystem. The commentary underscores the absurdity of trusting profit-driven entities to self-regulate or dictate the cadence of model deployment while secretly pursuing recursive self-improvement. Systems engineers and infrastructure leaders navigating the current hype cycle should read this piece for a concise, technically honest reality check on the economic incentives driving AI alignment politics.
