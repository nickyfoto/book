# Lock and Key: Seahawks Survive QB Scare in Week 1 After Roster Overhaul — Week of 2026-08-27 to 2026-09-09

## Week in Review
From locking down cornerstone defensive tackle Leonard Williams to navigating complex 53-man roster mechanics under General Manager John Schneider's "70-man" philosophy, Head Coach Mike Macdonald's squad spent the final week of August fortifying their Super Bowl-defending nucleus. As new offensive coordinator Brian Fleury installed a dynamic tight-end scheme from the sideline, the Seahawks transitioned smoothly into regular-season action, culminating in a resilient Week 1 victory over New England despite starting quarterback Sam Howell exiting early with an injury. Off the field, the franchise signaled long-term stability under new ownership with the Khosla family guaranteeing football operations autonomy to leadership while delivering a major uniform win for the 12s.

## Top Stories

**Leonard Williams Anchors Defense with Extension and Championship Leadership** · [Seahawks Press Pass](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cjncM8t7mpE)
Defensive tackle Leonard Williams locked in a three-year contract extension, cementing his role as the anchor of Seattle's defensive line and calling the franchise's family atmosphere life-changing. Head coach Mike Macdonald praised Williams as an All-Pro force who demands double-teams, while Williams revealed he has been consulting hall-of-famers Ronnie Lott, Richard Sherman, and Kam Chancellor to maintain a clean-slate mindset for defending the Super Bowl title. Beyond his leadership role, Williams noted a key scheme tweak that shifts explosive pass-rusher Byron Murphy II into the 3-technique to maximize one-on-one pass-rushing opportunities.

**Secondary Remodel: Trevon Diggs, Avery Smith, and Roster Maneuvers** · [Seahawks Press Pass](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zy0v4JamvJ8)
General Manager John Schneider executed a fluid secondary overhaul by stashing veteran Trevon Diggs on the practice squad following a preseason interception, while trading for defensive back Avery Smith and keeping Terrion Arnold on the commissioner's exempt list. Schneider highlighted his strategy of using the practice squad as part of an active "70-man" roster concept to shield developmental prospects from waivers while keeping proven depth in the building. The rapid integration of Diggs and Smith alongside emerging corner Nehemiah Pritchett gives Macdonald a versatile, deep secondary capable of handling heavier personnel shifts.

**Brian Fleury’s Sideline Play-Calling Debut and Tight End Blueprint** · [Seahawks Press Pass](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jaMhxCx-PCA)
First-time offensive coordinator Brian Fleury opted to call plays from the sideline rather than the booth to ensure direct face-to-face communication with his quarterbacks. Fleury and tight end AJ Barner revealed an adaptable, heavy-motion offense that deploys tight ends dynamically across H-back and fullback positions to create misdirection and downhill blocking angles. The new offensive approach proved its poise in Week 1, making aggressive mid-game adjustments and converting high-stakes situational downs when starting plans had to be adapted on the fly.

**Week 1 Victory and QB Resilience: Howell Injury and Drew Lock's Relief Duty** · [Seahawks vs. Patriots Postgame Press Conference - 2026 Week 1](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gKIlJdjUtAk)
Starting quarterback Sam Howell suffered an early injury on the fourth snap of the Week 1 opener against New England, but initial postgame testing provided major relief by ruling out a bone fracture. Backup Drew Lock stepped in cold without mid-week first-team reps, relying on his nightly whiteboard preparation to convert a clutch 4th-and-short pass to Jaxon Smith-Njigba that turned into a 45-yard splash play. Coupled with a toe-tapping interception by Josh Jobe and a bone-jarring hit from Julian Love, Seattle showcased its trademark 'next man up' physical identity to secure the season-opening win.

**Khosla Family Era Begins with Autonomy for Leadership and White Throwbacks for 2027** · [Khosla Family Introductory Press Conference At Lumen Field | Seattle Seahawks](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=B0IZrk2nARI)
In their formal introductory press conference at Lumen Field, new owners Vinod, Neil, and Anu Khosla promised full operational autonomy to GM John Schneider and Head Coach Mike Macdonald while maintaining ticket accessibility standards. Neil Khosla delighted fans by officially announcing the return of the fan-favorite white throwback uniforms for the 2027 season. The family also introduced community initiatives like free AI tutoring for Seattle youth, reinforcing a seamless ownership transition that keeps football decisions strictly inside the front office.

## Patterns
The Seahawks demonstrated a distinct organizational philosophy this week by embracing a "70-man" roster mindset that treats practice squad depth as active, game-ready extensions of the 53-man squad. Across both sides of the ball, Macdonald's coaching staff continues to emphasize positional versatility—using tight ends in fullback alignments, defensive linemen in new techniques, and three-linebacker sets—to win situational matchups and stay scheme-adaptable. Above all, a culture of fierce accountability and veteran mentorship from core stars like Leonard Williams and Sam Darnold has prevented post-Super Bowl hangover and instilled a relentless, reset mindset for the 2026 campaign.
