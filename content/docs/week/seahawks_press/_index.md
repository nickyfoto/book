# Khosla’s \$9B Era Begins as Devastating Injuries and Secondary Remodels Shape Macdonald's Culture — Week of 2026-08-23 to 2026-08-30

## Week in Review
The Seahawks entered a historic new era off the field with NFL owners unanimously approving the **\$9 billion sale of the franchise to the tech-focused Khosla family**. However, the celebratory mood was heavily sobered by **devastating, season-ending injuries** to fan-favorites like safety Bud Clark and wideout Jake Bobo. Despite these major blows to the depth chart, head coach Mike Macdonald and GM John Schneider spent the week reinforcing a **fiery, veteran-led culture** that refuses to tolerate post-Super Bowl complacency as they aggressively re-engineered the roster for Week 1.

## Top Stories
1. **The \$9 Billion Ownership Transfer and the Khosla Era** · [Khosla Family Press Conference](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gpfn1Zx2Fdw)
NFL owners unanimously approved the historic **\$9 billion transfer of the Seahawks** from the Paul Allen estate to the tech-focused Khosla family. The new ownership group emphasized deep humility, committing to a community-centric approach and remaining in a **"learning mode" under current management** for the foreseeable future. Meanwhile, GM John Schneider expressed immense excitement about leveraging the incoming ownership’s **advanced technological resources** to keep pushing the franchise forward.

2. **Heartbreak in the Secondary and Receiver Room** · [Seahawks Press Pass](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lni0Qk2xPCc)
The Seahawks' roster depth took massive hits, starting with safety Bud Clark, who is officially **out for the season and facing surgery** for a broken ankle. The receiver room suffered a parallel tragedy when teammate Cooper Kupp confirmed that **wideout Jake Bobo is out for the year** with a serious injury, while veteran Cody Thompson ("Tori") was also sidelined with a long-term injury. This rash of training camp injuries directly influenced coach Mike Macdonald’s highly protective player-safety standards, leading him to **proactively pull a healthy Kupp off the practice field** in Tennessee to preserve the star veteran.

3. **Leonard Williams Secures the Defensive Foundation** · [Seahawks Press Pass](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cjncM8t7mpE)
Seattle locked up All-Pro defensive tackle Leonard Williams to a crucial **three-year contract extension**, cementing him as the premier anchor of the defensive front. Williams spoke emotionally about how Seattle's family-oriented culture **"changed my life,"** while Macdonald praised him as a **"synergistic" force** who absorbs double-teams to free up younger pass rushers. Embracing his prominent role on the team's Leadership Council, Williams is actively **mentoring the next generation of defenders**, specifically calling out rookie additions like Byron Murphy.

4. **Schneider's "70-Man" Roster Remodel and Cornerback Carousel** · [Initial 53-Man Roster Press Conference](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zy0v4JamvJ8)
In finalizing the active roster, GM John Schneider emphasized an evolving **"70-man" philosophy** that utilizes the practice squad to shield young developmental players from the waiver wire. This strategy was put on full display when newly acquired cornerback Trevon Diggs was **stashed on the practice squad** to ramp up his conditioning following seven months away from live action. To fortify the secondary, Seattle traded for versatile DB Avery Smith, secured third-round corner Julian Neal despite a quad injury, and welcomed **former substitute teacher Montorie Foster Jr.** onto the 53-man roster after an explosive training camp.

A major developing thread is Mike Macdonald’s **highly collaborative, player-first coaching style**, where veterans on the Leadership Council hold a direct voice in scheduling, conditioning, and player safety. Additionally, the team is instilling a **fierce "hustle-first" mentality**, as evidenced by dramatic backup offensive operational strides, young players chasing down the ball, and hungry roster-bubble guys taking extreme ownership of the team's standard. Finally, John Schneider's active roster maneuvers show a clear pattern of **continuous depth-building in the secondary**, demonstrating that the front office is constantly preparing for contingencies and refusing post-Super Bowl complacency.
