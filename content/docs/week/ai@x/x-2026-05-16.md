# Moving Beyond LLMs and the AI Wealth Divide — 2026-05-16

## Highlights
The timeline is buzzing with deep skepticism about the ceiling of pure language models, championing instead "world models" and energy-based models (EBMs) that encode causal and structural reasoning. Meanwhile, on the ground, a staggering wealth gap is forming in San Francisco as a small cohort of AI insiders hit massive liquidity events, fundamentally altering the tech career landscape and fueling widespread malaise among the rest of the industry.

## Articles Worth Reading

**The AI Wealth Divide Tormenting SF** ([Source](http://twitter.macworks.dev/deedydas/status/2055491938464489888#m))
Deedy Das captures the frenetic anxiety in Silicon Valley as approximately 10,000 employees at top AI labs hit retirement wealth above $20 million. This sudden, massive wealth creation is hollowing out the middle class of tech, causing profound malaise, paralysis among middle managers, and a frantic re-evaluation of career paths. Even the newly rich are experiencing a lack of purpose, highlighting the deep psychological toll of living through a transformative gold rush where intelligence and hard work loosely correlate with astronomical financial outcomes.

**Toward a Unified Understanding of World Models** ([Source](http://twitter.macworks.dev/adamsafron/status/2055690197799473249#m))
A comprehensive special issue from the Royal Society argues that biological learners thrive by compressing information into causal "world models". The authors contend that current LLMs lack the spatiotemporal coherence required for true System 2 cognition, subjective conscious experience, or artificial general intelligence. It is a critical read for understanding the theoretical pivot away from pure statistical language scaling toward systems that mimic natural intelligence's flexibility and causal mapping.

**The Power of Constrained Hobby Projects** ([Source](http://twitter.macworks.dev/mattshumer_/status/2055708153182879933#m))
Matt Shumer discusses his AI tool, Agent-S, which he built by strictly limiting his development time to under an hour a week. This extreme constraint forced him to rely on an intensely capable base agent rather than bloating the software with custom features. It serves as a strong reminder that tight constraints and intrinsic motivation often yield more elegant engineering solutions than over-resourced grinds.
