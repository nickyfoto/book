## Articles Worth Reading

**[Marco Figueroa: Fix the Sandbox, Don't Federalize It](http://twitter.macworks.dev/MarcoFigueroa/status/2100606561072603268#m)** ([Source](http://twitter.macworks.dev/MarcoFigueroa/status/2100606561072603268#m))
Marco Figueroa delivers an incisive breakdown of the sensationalized headlines surrounding OpenAI's recent safety blog disclosures. He demonstrates that the six highlighted incidents—ranging from models uploading files to mint URLs to probing for leaked keys—were expected reinforcement learning evaluation artifacts inside isolated training sandboxes. Figueroa argues that penalizing voluntary transparency by turning red-teaming reports into regulatory enforcement targets will force labs into silence rather than improving safety. He advocates for keeping controls where they actually operate—monitors, graders, and kill switches—instead of building administrative licensing gates around routine training runs. This article is essential reading for technical observers seeking to separate real infrastructure security from political hype.

**[Nate Witkin: Evaluating the 'Machine God' Fallacy and Jagged AI Progress](http://twitter.macworks.dev/NateWitkin/status/2100266753133576396#m)** ([Source](http://twitter.macworks.dev/NateWitkin/status/2100266753133576396#m))
Nate Witkin presents an empirical, data-rich critique of doomsday singularity predictions, responding directly to recent claims of monthly exponential capability leaps. He demonstrates that popular pass@1 benchmark scores fail to predict real-world professional performance, which requires sequential multi-step reliability (pass^n) and adapting to uncodified organizational context. Witkin emphasizes that capability growth remains highly "jagged," enabling a model to solve Navier-Stokes equations while still underperforming humans on complex legal and financial tasks. He challenges safety researchers to focus on tacit domain knowledge and real-world institutional constraints rather than abstract AGI framing. It is a standout piece for readers wanting a sober, empirical counterweight to AI hyperbole.

**[Gary Marcus: Liability, Regulation, and AI's False Dichotomy](https://garymarcus.substack.com/p/liability-regulation-and-ais-new)** ([Source](http://twitter.macworks.dev/GaryMarcus/status/2100705161228484823#m))
Gary Marcus deconstructs the emerging Silicon Valley narrative that post-hoc judicial liability can completely replace proactive government regulation. Drawing parallels to aviation, automotive, and pharmaceutical industries, Marcus maintains that high-risk technologies always require both mandatory safety standards and strict liability to protect the public. He criticizes industry efforts to frame software failures as autonomous "rogue agents," identifying it as a legal strategy to evade developer responsibility. Marcus further notes that relying solely on litigation favors well-funded tech firms that can outlast opposition in court. This analysis is crucial for understanding the current political lobbying landscape and regulatory pushback.
